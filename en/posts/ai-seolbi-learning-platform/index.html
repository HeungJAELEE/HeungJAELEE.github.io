    Maintenance Engineer Exam Masterbook

    The case follows the learner's actual path from question selection through mistake review.

    Problem

    Maintenance-qualification material fragments questions, answers, theory, NCS units, and practical visuals across sources. A fluent AI explanation can still teach the wrong thing when answer candidates conflict or image rights are unclear. A learning flow also fails if the answer or explanation is already present in the client response before submission, even when the screen hides it.

    The project did not start with a question-volume target. It publishes only material with verified answer, source, and rights evidence. Anything missing one of those elements keeps the same ID and an explicit reason while staying out of the public set.

    Representative user scene

    A learner selects a CBT question, chooses an answer, and submits it. Before submission, the learner receives no answer, explanation, or option-level feedback. After submission, the learner sees the answer and explanation together with the linked source and theory evidence. A wrong option becomes a path back through that evidence for mistake-based learning.

    Written-response and practical material follow the same evidence rules without being forced into one answer format. A question or visual without sufficient publication support stays out of the learning path with its deferral reason preserved.

    What a first-time visitor sees

    The top navigation mirrors the learning sequence. A learner starts with Theory, moves to the Written mock exam, practices constructed answers in the written-response areas, and uses Practical information to review pneumatic, hydraulic, welding, preparation, and test-center differences. Search is the entry point for returning to a specific concept or question.

    What the tool does—and does not do

    The tool connects question selection, submission, evidence review, and mistake learning. Each question carries a stable ID, assessment type, answer state, theory link, source, rights state, publication decision, and deferral reason. Publication eligibility means the answer, source, and rights have all been checked; it does not merely mean that content exists.

    The tool does not replace official exam material or guarantee a passing outcome. It does not publish an explanation merely because AI finds it plausible. Answer conflicts remain held_answer_conflict; missing visual evidence or unclear rights remain held_asset_missing.

    Data, AI, and human roles

    The data keeps evidence and review state beside the question. Question, theory, NCS evidence, answer state, rights, and publication status share a stable identity so later evidence can be added without erasing the history of uncertainty.

    AI assisted with classification, duplicate and semantic-mismatch candidates, explanation and option-feedback drafts, and regression-check ideas. A human reviewed original sources, actual answers, conflicts, image and drawing rights, publication decisions, and final deployment approval. AI plausibility is not publication evidence.

    Screen, data, and repository structure

    The learner surface is only the last layer. Source material does not flow straight into a page. It is structured, assigned a publication state, and transformed into a learner-safe response. The submission API can verify an answer, but the pre-submit response excludes the answer, explanation, and option feedback.

    1. Import and generation steps turn source material and review notes into stable IDs and structured records.
    2. Content repositories read each question together with theory, source, rights, and review state.
    3. A publication filter separates published, in-review, and held material.
    4. App Router pages and APIs receive only the public DTO needed for theory, practice, submission, and review.
    5. Unit, integration, and browser tests check the data contract, answer leakage, and representative learner flows.

    There is no separate public Git repository. The following is a disclosure-safe responsibility map; it does not expose private source files or credentials.

    src/app/           pages, learner routes, and submission APIs
src/components/    question cards, tabs, progress, and review UI
src/lib/content/   question/theory repositories and publication decisions
src/lib/security/  pre-submit answer-leak protection
scripts/           source import, generation, verification, and packaging
tests/             unit, integration, and browser user flows
supabase/          account, review-role, and data policies

    A repository reader should start with src/app to see entry routes, move to src/lib/content to understand what reaches the screen, and then inspect src/lib/security and tests for the publication and no-answer-leak contracts.

    Build sequence

    1. Inventory source material and reviewed notes, then assign stable IDs.
    2. Separate CBT, written-response, and practical types and connect questions to theory, NCS evidence, and sources.
    3. Review answer conflicts and visual rights, keeping incomplete material out of publication.
    4. Use Next.js App Router, TypeScript, Zod, and Tailwind to build the select→submit→evidence→mistake-learning flow.
    5. Exclude answers, explanations, and option feedback from pre-submit responses; check the data and representative flow with Vitest and Playwright/Chromium.
    6. Read back the static artifact through Cloudflare Pages and Sites.

    Company value and limitations

    The same approach can support company training or maintenance-knowledge systems. Keeping source, revision state, rights, and human review beside work knowledge makes AI-generated explanations easier to distinguish from approved training content. Connecting question selection to evidence review and mistake learning also turns a document collection into a designed learning behavior.

    The limits remain visible. Material without answer or rights evidence is still deferred, and full-E2E residuals remain. Official source material takes priority when it differs from the site. In-progress skill changes and an unimplemented inventory contract are excluded from this result.

    Problem

    When questions, answers, theory, and practical visuals are fragmented, AI can produce plausible explanations that still rely on conflicting answers or material without publication rights.

    Goal

    Connect question, theory, NCS source, answer state, and HOLD reason through stable IDs while preventing answer exposure before submission across written and practical learning.

    Success

    Only source-, answer-, and rights-qualified items publish; HOLD stays hidden; and build, no-answer-leak, and representative E2E checks reproduce on one static artifact.

    How AI and human roles were divided

    AI role
    Assisted classification, duplicate-candidate discovery, explanation and theory-link drafts, test cases, and regression checklists.
    Human role
    A human reviewed original sources, answer conflicts, image rights, PUBLIC/HOLD decisions, and final deployment approval.
    Publication gate
    Only items with source, answer, and rights evidence become PUBLIC. Missing evidence retains held_asset_missing or held_answer_conflict; personal data and source binaries are not republished.
    External effect
    Two static learning deployments expose read and study surfaces only. There is no payment, qualification decision, or external-account permission change.

    Scope and boundaries

    In scope

    • Source-aware contracts for questions, theory, NCS, and answer state
    • CBT and written/practical flows with pre-submit answer non-disclosure
    • PUBLIC/HOLD gates, responsive UI, build, regression, and E2E checks

    Out of scope or unverified

    • Auto-publishing rights-unclear images or conflicting answers
    • Guaranteeing official exam questions or passing outcomes
    • Claiming in-progress skill or inventory work as complete

    Implementation and verification

    What had to work

    • Link each question to theory, source, state, and HOLD reason through stable identity.
    • Exclude answer, explanation, and option feedback from client responses before submission.
    • Expose source and publication state across written and practical learning.

    Quality and operating rules

    • Fail closed to HOLD on answer conflict, missing asset, or unclear rights.
    • Record Node 24 build, unit/integration, and representative E2E evidence.
    • Disclose remaining full-suite failures instead of hiding them.

    Why it was designed this way

    1. Prioritize publication gates over generated volume

      One unsupported question can damage learning trust more than many correct items help.

    2. Enforce answer non-disclosure in the data contract

      CSS-only hiding still leaks answers through network responses or HTML source.

    3. Preserve HOLD with explicit reasons

      Stable identity allows later evidence review without losing uncertainty history.
