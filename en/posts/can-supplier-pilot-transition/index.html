Manufacturing case study

Pilot supplier transition

Alternative Can Supplier Pilot Transition

A supplier-transition case that translated material and geometry differences into process risks and verified quality and safety across three lots

Project period
Validated during Kumyang employment · pilot supply transition in Mar 2025
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · alternative supplier validation for a cylindrical-cell component
Scope
Change review, assembly-condition reset, three-lot process/quality/safety testing, and process and approval documentation

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
The incumbent supplier could not cover the planned follow-on volume alone, so a new can supplier required manufacturing and quality validation.
What I owned
I translated material-property and bottom-geometry differences into beading, crimping, and sealing risks, then set the assembly conditions and validation sequence.
Verified result
After 600 cells across three lots and an official UN38.3 pass, purchasing and subsequent pilot production moved to the alternative can after existing stock was consumed.
Validation scope 600 cells · 3 lots
Safety verification Official UN38.3 pass

Validation flow

The incumbent supplier could not cover the planned follow-on volume alone, so an alternative can supplier required process and quality validation. My responsibility was not supplier selection or price negotiation. I assessed how material-property and geometry differences affected assembly and cell safety, then defined the gate for a pilot supply transition.

01

4M-based component-change validation

Translating material and geometry differences into assembly conditions

We separated part differences from equipment settings, then reset beading and crimping/sizing conditions.

Change observed

  • The candidate can differed in alloy ratio, bottom thickness, and some tolerances.
  • Even within the same drawing family, elasticity and hardness could affect beading and sealing geometry.
  • We linked incoming inspection to cell safety tests instead of approving the new part under the previous settings.

4M cause map

ManSupplier and inspector judgment variationCompared drawings, certificates, and incoming dimensions under the same itemsSeparated incoming, process, and outgoing quality responsibilities.
MachineExisting knife and crimping/sizing settingsCompared total height, inner diameter, geometry, and sealing resultsReset knife height, depth, pressure, and crimping/sizing load and height.
MaterialAlloy properties and bottom-thickness differenceCompared certificates, dimensions, and appearance with the incumbent canTraced the change through cell process and safety tests.
MethodMissing end-to-end approval sequenceComponent inspection alone could not establish sealing or burst-pressure fitnessDefined incoming→assembly→section/teardown→safety-test gates.

Direct actions

  • Defined the change review and assembly validation sequence.
  • Reset beading knife height, depth, and pressure.
  • Adjusted crimping/sizing load and height.
  • Prepared the sample evaluation, change review, IQC criteria, component specification, limit samples, and process conditions.

Retest

  • Built 600 cells: 200 cells in each of three lots.
  • Checked incoming dimensions, appearance, beading/crimping geometry, weld condition, sealing, and downstream results under the same criteria.
  • Used weld sectioning and cell teardown to inspect internal effects.
  • Reviewed drop, vent burst-pressure, upper/lower compression, and official UN38.3 testing.

Decision

  • The official UN38.3 test passed.
  • After incumbent inventory was consumed, subsequent pilot production and purchasing moved to the alternative can in March 2025.
  • Commercial production did not start because of company financing, so long-run high-volume results were not established.
