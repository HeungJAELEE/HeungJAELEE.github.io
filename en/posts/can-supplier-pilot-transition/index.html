TECHNICAL CASE STUDY

2170 Can Second-Source Qualification

A supplier-change case that qualified an alternative can with different alloy, thickness, tolerance, burr, and surface characteristics through incoming inspection, assembly-condition reset, cell quality/safety/performance checks, 600 cells across three lots, official UN38.3, and a pilot production and purchasing transition

Aug 5, 2026 · 6 minutes · Last reviewed Aug 9, 2026

  • Supplier qualification
  • 4M change control
  • UN38.3

Project background and objective

Planned production volume could not remain dependent on one can supplier, so an alternative source had to be qualified. The candidate can differed from the incumbent in alloy ratio, bottom thickness, dimensional tolerance, burrs, and surface condition. Even when component dimensions were within tolerance, those differences could affect beading and crimping geometry, sealing, can-cap laser welding, and vent safety.

As the lead process-validation engineer, I connected drawing and certificate review, incoming-inspection criteria, reset of the existing assembly conditions, sample-cell builds, sectioning and teardown, internal quality and safety tests, and cell-performance evaluation. Purchasing owned supplier, quotation, and order changes; quality owned incoming and outgoing disposition plus the official report; cell engineering owned formation, capacity, and life evaluation; production supported lot-level sample builds.

The objective was to move beyond component inspection and verify the alternative can through actual cell processing and safety performance, establishing both the usable line conditions and the basis for a pilot supply transition.

Supplier-change validation flow

Component change Potentially affected process Primary check
Alloy and bottom thickness Beading, crimping, and vent Geometry, press conditions, burst pressure
Dimensions and tolerances Beading and crimping/sizing Total height, inner diameter, sealing, and airtightness
Burrs and surface condition Incoming, assembly, and appearance inspection Measurement points, limit samples, and assembly damage
Can-cap contact condition Laser welding Weld appearance, cross-section, and teardown result
Cell-level effect of the change Formation, performance, and safety Capacity, life, compression/drop, and UN38.3

The change was checked in the order of incoming quality, process conditions, cell safety, and performance. No single stage was treated as sufficient on its own; the same component changes had to remain acceptable through the next process and test.

1. [Material·Measurement] Component comparison and IQC criteria

Compare incumbent and candidate parts

Drawings and material certificates were cross-checked, followed by actual measurement of the bottom and wall sections, dimensional tolerances, burrs, scratches, and surface condition. Measurement points and appearance disposition were fixed so that the incumbent and candidate parts were compared under one basis.

The differences found at incoming inspection were linked to the next process instead of ending as component pass/fail decisions. Bottom thickness and alloy were tied to beading, crimping, and vent behavior; dimensional tolerance to total height, inner diameter, and sealing; burr and surface condition to assembly damage and appearance checks.

Incoming-inspection gate

  • Cross-check drawings, certificates, and actual measurements
  • Repeat bottom and wall measurements at fixed locations
  • Compare burrs, scratches, and surface condition with limit samples
  • Assign each change to affected processes and downstream checks
  • Release only accepted candidate parts into sample-cell builds

The results were transferred into IQC criteria, the part specification, the change-review sheet, and limit samples. Incoming inspection served as the first gate into cell-level process validation rather than the final supplier decision.

2. [Machine·Method] Existing-equipment processability and condition reset

Beading and crimping/sizing

Different can thickness and tolerance could change beading geometry, crimping pressure, total height, inner diameter, and sealing even under the incumbent settings. Beading-knife height, depth, and pressure, along with crimping/sizing pressure and height conditions, were rechecked for the candidate can.

Conditions were not changed all at once. Process-specific samples compared beading shape, total height, inner diameter, crimp appearance, and airtightness. The selected conditions were then checked under the same positions and criteria during the lot trials so that the same setting could be used across operators and lots.

Can-cap laser welding

Changes in can-cap contact condition and gap could alter the joint under the same laser settings. Candidate-can weld samples were evaluated through appearance, sectioning, and cell teardown.

Component difference, equipment alignment, and laser condition were separated before the applicable condition was selected from the actual joint result. Weld disposition was connected with beading/crimping geometry, sealing, and downstream cell tests rather than treated as an isolated result.

Field application

The confirmed beading, crimping/sizing, and laser conditions were transferred into the process-condition sheet and sample-evaluation record. Manufacturing used one setting sequence for each lot, while engineering and quality applied the same height, sealing, and weld criteria.

3. [Measurement] Cell quality, safety, and performance validation

The can is both the external shell and part of the sealing and pressure-relief system. Correct assembly geometry was insufficient if leakage, vent behavior, transport safety, or cell performance changed.

Internal quality and safety tests

  • Assembly appearance, total height, inner diameter, airtightness, and leakage
  • Top and bottom compression
  • Drop testing
  • Vent burst-pressure check
  • Weld sectioning and cell teardown

After the internal geometry, sealing, and pressure-relief checks, official-test samples were built. Quality reviewed the test results and official report, while engineering linked them to the assembly route and sample history.

Cell performance and official UN38.3

Cell engineering checked formation, capacity, and life for the alternative-can condition. The condition meeting the process, safety, and performance criteria proceeded to the official UN38.3 transport-safety test and passed.

This connected component inspection, processability, internal safety tests, and official transport safety in one change-control sequence.

4. 600-cell / three-lot pilot validation and supply transition

The selected conditions were used to produce 200 cells per lot across three lots, 600 cells in total. Each lot used the same criteria for assembly geometry, total height, inner diameter, airtightness, laser joints, and downstream performance.

Development, cell engineering, process engineering, quality, production, purchasing, and related managers reviewed the lot results, internal quality/safety evidence, cell-performance evaluation, and official UN38.3 report. Based on that review, follow-on pilot production and purchase orders moved to the alternative can from March 2025. Existing inventory was consumed first, after which subsequent production input followed the new-can basis.

Standardization and handoff

  • Change-review sheet: material, dimensional, and surface differences plus affected processes
  • IQC criteria: measurement locations, dimensions, tolerances, burrs, and appearance disposition
  • Part specification and limit samples: incoming component and surface criteria
  • Process-condition sheet: beading, crimping/sizing, and can-cap laser conditions
  • Sample-evaluation sheet: lot-level process, quality, safety, and performance results
  • Supplier-approval package: official UN38.3 and cross-functional review results
  • Purchasing and production handoff: criteria for pilot production and order change

I prepared the change-risk logic, sample builds and evaluation, and the first drafts of the control documents. Quality, cell engineering, production, and purchasing reviewed the results so the same basis could be used from incoming inspection through production input.

Final results

  • Established incoming and process-impact criteria for an alternative can with different alloy, thickness, tolerance, burr, and surface characteristics
  • Reset applicable conditions on the existing beading, crimping/sizing, and can-cap laser equipment
  • Connected internal sealing, compression, drop, and vent tests with formation, capacity, and life evaluation
  • Completed a 600-cell pilot validation across three lots of 200 cells
  • Passed the official UN38.3 transport-safety test
  • Transitioned pilot production and purchase orders in March 2025 and handed off the field standards
