Supply risk and the manufacturing question

One existing can supplier could not reliably cover the planned production volume alone. Adding an alternative source required more than comparing price and delivery. The candidate can differed in alloy ratio, bottom thickness, tolerances, burrs, and surface condition; those differences could affect beading and crimping geometry, sealing, can-cap welding, and vent safety.

The manufacturing question was: Can the candidate can run through the existing assembly equipment while preserving cell quality, safety, and performance sufficiently for a pilot supply transition? I directly owned the change review, incoming inspection, assembly-condition definition, sample-cell builds, sectioning and teardown, safety and performance tests, and process-standard documentation. Purchasing owned supplier, price, and orders; quality owned incoming and outgoing decisions; cell engineering reviewed performance impact.

Case 01 — Turning component differences into cell-level risks

Symptom and success criterion

Even when the candidate can met component-level dimensions, its alloy ratio, bottom thickness, tolerance, and surface differed from the incumbent part. A component pass alone could not prove beading geometry, crimp sealing, or vent safety. The success criterion was to connect each change to process and inspection items and verify quality and safety on actual cells.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared drawings and certificates for existing and candidate parts
  • Measured bottom, wall, and tolerance features
  • Checked burrs, scratches, and surface condition
  • Linked each change to affected processes and inspection methods
  • Compared incumbent and candidate parts under the same manufacturing route

Action and retest

A change-review sheet and incoming-inspection items were established, and only candidate parts passing the component gate proceeded to cell builds. Dimensions and appearance were controlled separately, then the same changes were checked through beading, crimping, laser, and safety testing.

Result and standardization

Incoming results were transferred into IQC criteria, the part specification, and limit samples. Component inspection served as the entry gate to cell-level process validation, not the final supplier-transition decision.

Case 02 — Resetting processability on existing assembly equipment

Symptom and success criterion

Different bottom thickness and tolerance could change beading geometry, crimping/sizing height, and sealing under the same equipment settings. Can-cap laser welding also required renewed checks of part gap, contact condition, weld quality, and internal-beam risk.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared beading shape, total height, and inner diameter
  • Checked total height, appearance, and sealing after crimping and sizing
  • Reviewed can-cap laser appearance and sectioned welds
  • Used cell teardown to check internal damage and beam entry
  • Repeated the same conditions across lots to compare variation

Action and retest

Beading-knife height, depth, and pressure were reset. Crimping and sizing pressure and height were adjusted, while can-cap weld gap and sections were checked through sectioning and teardown. Conditions were changed by process rather than all at once, and the selected settings proceeded to the lot validation.

Result and standardization

Confirmed settings were transferred into the process-condition sheet and sample-evaluation record. Operators used one setting sequence, while quality and engineering applied the same height, sealing, and weld decisions.

Case 03 — Cell-safety and performance gate

Symptom and success criterion

The can is both an external cell component and part of the sealing and pressure-release system. Correct assembly geometry alone was insufficient if sealing, vent burst, transport safety, or cell performance changed. Process checks, internal safety tests, official UN38.3, and cell performance were therefore linked in one approval flow.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Leakage, sealing, and appearance inspection
  • Top/bottom compression and drop tests
  • Vent burst-pressure check
  • Formation, capacity, and life comparison
  • Official UN38.3 sample build, test, and report review

Action and retest

Internal sealing, compression, drop, and vent tests were completed before official UN38.3 samples were built. Quality reviewed the official report, while cell engineering checked the material-change impact through formation, capacity, and life.

Result and standardization

The internal quality and safety tests plus official UN38.3 passed. Test items and decisions were connected to the sample-evaluation sheet, supplier-approval package, and component and inspection standards.

600-cell / three-lot validation and pilot supply transition

The selected conditions were used to build 200 cells per lot across three lots, 600 cells in total. Assembly geometry, sealing, welds, and downstream performance were checked under the same criteria in each lot. After cross-functional review of lot results and the official UN38.3 report, purchase orders for follow-on pilot production moved to the new can in March 2025. Existing stock was consumed first, then subsequent input switched to the alternative can.
