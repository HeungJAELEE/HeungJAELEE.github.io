Manufacturing case study

Pilot supplier transition

Alternative Can Supplier Validation and Pilot Supply Transition

A supplier-transition case that translated material and geometry differences into process risks and verified quality and safety across three lots

Project period
Validated during Kumyang employment · pilot supply transition in Mar 2025
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · alternative supplier validation for a cylindrical-cell component
Scope
Change review, assembly-condition reset, three-lot process/quality/safety testing, and process and approval documentation

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
The incumbent supplier could not cover the planned follow-on volume alone, so a new can supplier required manufacturing and quality validation.
What I owned
I translated material-property and bottom-geometry differences into beading, crimping, and sealing risks, then set the assembly conditions and validation sequence.
Verified result
After 600 cells across three lots and an official UN38.3 pass, purchasing and subsequent pilot production moved to the alternative can after existing stock was consumed.
Validation scope 600 cells · 3 lots
Safety verification Official UN38.3 pass

Why an alternative supplier needed validation

The incumbent supplier could not cover the planned follow-on volume alone. The new can could not be inserted into production without validation. Its overall geometry was similar, but alloy proportions and bottom geometry differed, so their effects on assembly and cell safety had to be checked again.

My responsibility was process validation rather than supplier selection or price negotiation. After reviewing drawings, certificates, and incoming dimensions, I defined a sequence covering sample-cell production, beading, crimping and sizing, can-to-cap-assy laser welding, sealing, and safety testing.

Component differences became process risks

Material elasticity, hardness, and bottom geometry could create several downstream risks.

ChangeMaterial properties and bottom geometry BeadingGeometry, height, and inner diameter CrimpingSealing and sizing stability WeldingPenetration and leak risk CellSafety and performance recheck

I did not begin by changing every recipe. I compared the incumbent and alternative components, separated component issues from equipment-setting issues, and then reset the remaining process factors: beading-knife height, depth, and pressure, plus crimping/sizing pressure and height.

Process conditions were reset across three lots

After selecting the conditions, the team built 200 cells per lot for three lots, 600 cells in total. Incoming dimensions and appearance, beading and crimping geometry, laser welds, sealing, and downstream results were checked using the same criteria in every lot.

The review did not stop at in-process values. Cell teardown and weld cross-sections were checked together with formation, capacity, and life results. When an issue appeared, we separated component changes from equipment conditions, narrowed the remaining cause, and repeated the test under the same acceptance criteria.

Quality and safety were rechecked through multiple tests

Correct process geometry alone was not enough for a supply transition. The validation included drop testing, vent burst-pressure testing, top and bottom compression testing, sealing checks, and an official UN38.3 test, which passed.

I established the process conditions. Quality reviewed incoming and outgoing quality, the battery team assessed cell-performance effects from the material change, and purchasing handled the supplier and purchase orders. The final transition was approved across the responsible development, battery, engineering, quality, production, and purchasing functions.

The validation became a pilot supply-transition standard

The work left a sample evaluation, change review, IQC criteria, component specification, limit samples, process conditions, and supplier-approval records. After the incumbent-can inventory was consumed, purchasing and subsequent pilot production moved to the alternative can in March 2025.

The confirmed endpoint is a pilot supply transition. Commercial production did not start because of company financing, so the business-case estimate was not treated as realized savings.
