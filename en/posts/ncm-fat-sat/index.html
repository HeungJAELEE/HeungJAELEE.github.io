NCM Prismatic Pilot-Line Acceptance and Initial Stabilization

Taking 11 core machines and 13 main processes from FAT and SAT through actual cell production

Project period
Employment May 2023–May 2024 · FAT May–Jul 2023 · SAT Jul–Nov 2023
Context
Completed at Jastech, now JAS Robotics, in the Cell Development Team
Scope
Acceptance and retesting of 11 core machines, process checks across 13 operations, and pilot planning and traceability

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Limited FAT samples and individual auto cycles could not prove end-to-end flow, repeatability, or readiness for actual production.
What I owned
I built acceptance sheets and punch lists, tracked supplier corrections and retests, and led actual cell production plus measurement and process-standard work.
Verified result
The team observed an initial snapshot of about 95% over roughly five days and 1,000 cells, including cells meeting criteria after rework, and documented FAT/SAT, MSA, and operating standards. It is not FPY or long-term production yield.
Initial assembly yield About 95%
Acceptance scope 11 core machines · 13 main processes

Project scope and my responsibility

The NCM prismatic pilot line could not be accepted by confirming that each machine moved independently. Eleven core machines and thirteen production processes had to build actual cells, recover from abnormalities, and apply consistent quality decisions. I translated drawings and process concepts into acceptance criteria and connected FAT, SAT, punch closure, and initial production operation.

Flow from acceptance to early production

  1. CRITERIABuild acceptance criteriaConverted drawings and process concepts into function, safety, quality, and cycle checks.
  2. FATSupplier function acceptanceChecked I/O, sensors, alarms, interlocks, repeatability, and sample quality.
  3. SATSite installation and integrationRetested installation, process links, operator flow, and abnormal recovery.
  4. PUNCHClose nonconformitiesRecorded symptom, impact, owner, due date, and retest method and verified under the same criteria.
  5. PILOTBuild actual cellsChecked process conditions, inspection, and production history over roughly five days and 1,000 cells.
  6. HANDOFFTransfer operating standardsReflected results in MSA, SOP, QCP, checklists, and production-history control.

My acceptance and early-production scope

  • Converted product drawings and process concepts into acceptance checks.
  • Controlled owners, due dates, retests, and completion criteria for supplier punch items.
  • Operated production priority, material/WIP/lot history, and semi-finished decisions during actual-cell production.
  • Organized master-jig and reference-sample measurement into pilot MSA criteria.

What FAT and SAT had to close

  • I/O, sensors, alarms and interlocks, accuracy and repeatability, cycle time, recipe storage, safety, and recovery were checked on inspection sheets.
  • When FAT and SAT results differed, products and measurements made on site were prioritized.
  • Punch items were not closed by a completion reply alone; they were retested under the same criteria.

Manufacturability checked with actual cells

Acceptance conditions for 11 core machines and 13 major production processes were connected to actual cell production. About 1,000 cells were produced over five days, an average of roughly 200 cells per day, while process conditions, inspection, lot history, and rework flow were reviewed together.

Stage-by-stage cause narrowing

Cell-thickness increase after pre-charge

The occurrence point was narrowed through process-stage thickness and teardown results rather than one finished-cell value.

Stage checks

  • Thickness was checked from electrode through J/R, before and after filling, pre-charge, seal pin, and formation.
  • The increase became clear after pre-charge.
  • Teardown appearance, cell thickness, capacity, and life were compared together.

Causes separated from measurement

AreaCheckDecision
EquipmentPre-charge and degassing vacuum and compressionRetained as a core candidate
MaterialMoisture, separator, and electrolyteRemoved as primary candidates because comparison showed no clear difference
ProcessGas-release sequence and pressureNarrowed as the remaining cause
MeasurementThickness and teardown sequenceFixed so each stage could be compared consistently

Action and boundary

  • Pre-charge and degassing vacuum and compression pressures were adjusted.
  • Rebuilt cells were checked for thickness, teardown appearance, capacity, and life.
  • This was not calculated as the sole cause of total assembly yield.

Connected manufacturability validations

  • Pre-forming: The conversion of operator-dependent forming into jig and acceptance criteria is explained as a separate case.
  • Cell S/N production history: The tool that linked process MES and Excel records by Cell S/N for defect trace-back is also separated as its own case.
  • C-cut component change: Candidate geometry ranges were checked with condition-specific cells, while follow-on high-volume trial production remained a separate stage.

These are not merged into one FAT/SAT technology list. They are connected as different manufacturing decisions within the broader pilot-line history.
