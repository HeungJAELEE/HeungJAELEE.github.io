    NCM Prismatic Pilot Line: FAT/SAT for 13 Processes and 11 Systems

    FAT/SAT, punch-list closure, production planning, measurement-system review, and process checks for 13 NCM prismatic-cell processes and 11 core systems

    Jul 31, 2026 · 5 minutes · Last reviewed Jul 31, 2026

    • NCM
    • FAT
    • SAT
    • Cpk
    • Gage R&R

    Problem and context

    An automatic cycle on one machine does not mean a pilot line is ready. Upstream and downstream interfaces, cycle time, precision, repeatability, sensors, alarms, interlocks, operator safety, and recovery must work together. After product input, measurement variation must also be separated from process variation.

    This project covered 13 detailed processes and 11 core systems on an NCM 50Ah prismatic-cell assembly pilot line.

    What I owned

    • Prepared FAT/SAT acceptance sheets and checked specifications, intermediate-part tolerances, and acceptance criteria.
    • Managed vendor punch lists, action dates, and retest results.
    • Reviewed process capability (Cpk) separately from the measurement system (Gage R&R).
    • Checked laser, ultrasonic, and resistance welding plus filling through tensile, cross-section, leak, Hi-Pot, IR, and X-ray results.
    • Managed materials, WIP, staffing, and lot history for a 200-cell daily plan.
    • Used and improved an Excel VBA tracking sheet where MES was not yet available.

    What I checked and changed

    StageChecksMethod
    FATFunction, cycle, I/O, alarms, interlocks, safetySpecification and acceptance-sheet comparison; punch registration
    InstallationUtilities, reference points, precision, repeatabilityRecheck after installation
    SATInterfaces, sample run, restart, and recoveryRepeated tests under site conditions
    Process checkCpk, Gage R&R, welding, and fillingCompared process and measurement variation separately
    Production operationMaterial, WIP, people, lots, daily outputManaged priorities against the 200-cell plan

    Process capability (Cpk) describes the stability of process output against specification. Gage R&R checks variation from the measurement system and operators. They are not one “process index.”

    Result and limits

    • Completed FAT/SAT and retesting for 13 processes and 11 core systems.
    • Reached at least 95% pilot assembly yield and 100% on-time completion.
    • Documented work standards, incoming-evaluation criteria, and production-history rules.

    These are pilot-production results, not long-term mass-production Cpk or a claim about every machine’s individual capability. Detailed Gage R&R values, equipment drawings, customer specifications, recipes, defect images, and raw data remain private.

    Problem

    Even when individual equipment runs, pilot defects remain hard to reproduce if line I/O, interlocks, measurement variation, welding conditions, and MES trace fields are disconnected.

    Goal

    Manage FAT/SAT for 11 core systems, process capability (Cpk), the measurement system (Gage R&R), welding/filling, and production planning in one acceptance flow.

    Success

    Retest equipment punches and operate a 200-cell daily plan to reach at least 95% pilot assembly yield and 100% on-time completion.

    Scope and boundaries

    In scope

    • FAT/SAT and punch closure for 11 core assembly systems
    • Process capability (Cpk), measurement system (Gage R&R), welding and filling
    • 200-cell daily plan, material, WIP, staffing, and lot history

    Out of scope or unverified

    • Vendor drawings, customer specifications, raw recipes, and defect data
    • Generalizing pilot yield to long-term high-volume performance
    • Redistributing certification or customer approval documents

    Implementation and verification

    What had to work

    • Separate FAT and SAT criteria for I/O, safety, performance, recovery, and documents.
    • Trace measurement and welding by lot, equipment, recipe, and inspection result.
    • Include punches, standard work, training, and MES fields in acceptance.

    Quality and operating rules

    • Review process capability and the measurement system separately; hold condition acceptance when measurement confidence is insufficient.
    • Check alarm, restart, and manual-recovery paths as well as normal cycles.
    • Separate pilot from production and team outcome from individual scope.

    Why it was designed this way

    1. Separate FAT and SAT acceptance

      Standalone vendor conditions and on-site utilities and interfaces carry different risks.

    2. Separate Cpk and Gage R&R

      High measurement variation makes welding and defect cause conclusions unreliable.

    3. Include MES fields in equipment acceptance

      Without lot, recipe, and inspection linkage, post-startup root-cause analysis returns to manual work.
