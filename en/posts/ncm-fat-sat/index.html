Manufacturing case study

Pilot / validation

NCM Prismatic Pilot-Line Acceptance and Stabilization

Taking 11 core machines and 13 main processes from FAT and SAT through actual cell production

Project period
Employment May 2023–May 2024 · FAT May–Jul 2023 · SAT Jul–Nov 2023
Context
Completed at Jastech, now JAS Robotics, in the Cell Development Team
Scope
Acceptance and retesting of 11 core machines, process checks across 13 operations, and pilot planning and traceability

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Limited FAT samples and individual auto cycles could not prove end-to-end flow, repeatability, or readiness for actual production.
What I owned
I built acceptance sheets and punch lists, tracked supplier corrections and retests, and led actual cell production plus measurement and process-standard work.
Verified result
Initial assembly yield was about 95% across roughly 1,000 cells over five days, including reworked cells that met quality criteria.
Initial assembly yield About 95%
Acceptance scope 11 core machines · 13 main processes

How the project progressed

The goal was not merely to see equipment move. It was to confirm that the line could repeatedly produce actual cells. The scope covered 11 core machines and 13 main production processes; the EOL height-inspection machine was managed separately.

  1. FATCriteria and functionChecked I/O, sensors, alarms, interlocks, repeatability, cycle time, and safety through an inspection sheet.
  2. SATOn-site verificationRetested installation, process interfaces, operator flow, and abnormal recovery against the same criteria.
  3. PUNCHClose gapsRecorded the issue, impact, owner, due date, and retest method.
  4. PILOTValidate with cellsConfirmed semi-finished tolerances, process conditions, and measurement reliability in production.
  5. HANDOFFTransfer operating controlsMoved confirmed conditions into QCP, SOP, checklists, and production-history management.
01

Acceptance Gate

FAT and SAT of 11 core machines

Items were closed through same-criterion retests and actual cell flow, not supplier completion statements.

Acceptance criteria

  • Built the inspection sheet from product drawings and process concepts.
  • Checked I/O, sensors, alarms and interlocks, accuracy and repeatability, cycle time, recipe storage, operator safety, and recovery.
  • Included upstream and downstream interfaces and semi-finished tolerances.

Verification sequence

  • FAT covered function and basic repeatability with limited samples.
  • SAT repeated the same items after installation.
  • When FAT and SAT differed, on-site products and measurements took priority.
  • Potential causes were separated into work sequence, alignment and sealing, material and semi-finished variation, and recipe and inspection method.

Punch and retest

  • Logged the symptom, quality and safety impact, owner, deadline, and retest method.
  • Did not close an item because a supplier reported that it had been fixed.
  • Retested against the same criterion and confirmed that an actual cell could move to the next process.

Decision and handoff

  • Accepted repeatable items and returned failed items for additional action.
  • Moved confirmed conditions into OPL, SOP, QCP, and manufacturing checklists.
02

Diagnostic Root Cause Flow

Swelling after Pre-Charge

I traced thickness by process stage instead of inferring a cause from the finished cell.

Initial signal

  • Initial cell thickness was abnormally high.
  • During post-charge disassembly, a yellow appearance suspected to be related to a side reaction was observed on the electrode surface.
  • There was no normal-cell baseline at the time, so appearance alone was not treated as proof.

Stage-by-stage measurement

  • Tracked thickness through electrode, J/R, filling, Pre-Charge, Seal Pin, and Formation.
  • The increase became distinct after Pre-Charge.
  • Compared disassembly appearance, thickness, capacity, and life.

Narrowing candidates

4MCandidate reviewedEvidence and decision
ManStage-by-stage thickness and disassembly methodAligned the measurement sequence so stages could be compared.
MachinePre-Charge and Degassing vacuum and pressureThickness increased after Pre-Charge, so these remained primary variables.
MaterialMoisture, Al₂O₃-coated separator, and electrolyteNo clear difference explained swelling; removed from primary candidates.
MethodGas-release sequence and pressure conditionsInsufficient gas release remained as the leading process hypothesis.

Action and revalidation

  • Adjusted Pre-Charge and Degassing vacuum and pressing pressure.
  • Rechecked thickness reduction and additional reaction marks after disassembly.
  • Reviewed capacity and life before fixing the direction.

Result boundary

  • This was a swelling-analysis result.
  • It was not presented as the sole cause of the overall assembly yield of about 95%.

Two separate manufacturability validations

Pre-Forming process validation

  • Investigated foil tearing, active-material shedding, and short risk before ultrasonic welding.
  • Replaced manual shaping with a jig and defined position and geometry.
  • Verified conditions through short testing and disassembly, then used them in later pilot production.
  • The detailed case is available in Pre-Forming manufacturability validation.

C-Cut component-change validation

  • Tested whether a supplier’s Cap Plate tooling proposal could preserve existing cell quality and safety.
  • Produced 35 cells across seven conditions.
  • Compared fit, incoming-part condition, laser penetration and internal beam entry, beading geometry, and pressure stability.
  • Proposed an allowable range and drafted an IQC revision.
  • The follow-up 2,000 parts did not arrive before I left, so production application and cost impact are not presented as completed outcomes.

Measurement and pilot operating controls

  1. Measurement reliability
    • Confirmed repeatability with a Master Jig and reference samples during FAT.
    • Carried the same method into the pilot MSA control.
    • Reflected the decision method in QCP, internal process standards, and manufacturing checklists.
  2. Pilot production
    • Produced about 1,000 cells over roughly five days.
    • Initial assembly yield was about 95%, including reworked cells that met the quality standard.
    • This was a team result; my scope included priorities, material and WIP, and lot-history management.
  3. Production history
    • Managed disconnected cell-level MES history as a separate improvement project.
    • Built a Cell S/N production-history tool connecting electrode records through Formation.
