Initial pilot-line acceptance condition

FAT could confirm limited samples and individual machine auto cycles. After installation, however, process handoffs, semi-finished tolerances, operator flow, inspection results, and production history had to agree. A supplier completion notice alone was not enough to start pilot production.

I applied one acceptance basis to 11 core machines and 13 major production processes. The EOL height-inspection unit remained separate from the 11 core machines. My direct responsibility covered specification and acceptance criteria, FAT/SAT inspection, punch retests, process and measurement methods, and early production operation.

Case 01. FAT/SAT punch retest

01

FAT/SAT · punch retest

Rechecking supplier completion through actual cell results

The decision focused on repeated process links and product quality under one acceptance basis, not isolated machine motion.

Symptom and success criterion

A machine that passed individual FAT operation could behave differently after site installation because of sensor position, process handoffs, operator flow, or semi-finished conditions. Success required function, safety, and product quality to pass together under the same inspection basis.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Reused the FAT inspection-sheet structure for site SAT
  • Checked auto cycle, sensors, alarms, interlocks, and cycle time
  • Compared semi-finished tolerances with assembly, weld, leak, and electrical results on real cells
  • Connected pre/post-correction run records and cell results to each punch item

Local 4M+2E decision

ManDifferent supplier and operator check methodsPunch explanations did not match measurement resultsManagement causeFix owner, due date, and check method
MachineSensors, alarms, interlocks, jigs, cycles, and installation alignmentRepeated site errors and product resultsPrimary causeCorrect and retest under the same basis
MaterialSemi-finished tolerance and assembly-part lotLot comparison under the same machine conditionProcess-specific factorDecide with incoming and lot history
MethodFAT/SAT conditions, work order, and punch dispositionGap between function checks and production conditionsStructural causeRedefine the site test sequence
MeasurementMaster jigs, reference samples, and gauge differencesRepeated measurement and product inspectionDecision evidenceFix the method as the pilot standard
EnvironmentInstallation, dry-room, and operator environmentReproduction under site SAT conditionsInstallation conditionMaintain process-specific checks

Action and retest

Each punch item carried the symptom, quality impact, owner, due date, and retest method. After supplier correction, the same inspection and product criteria were applied again. This connected the 11 core machines and 13 main processes into actual cell production.

Standardization

The inspection sheet, supplier punch list, SAT checklist, and work/inspection criteria retained the action and recheck method.

Case 02. Cell-thickness increase after pre-charge

02

Stage-by-stage 4M+2E

Cell-thickness increase after pre-charge

Thickness and teardown results were followed from electrode through formation to locate the occurrence stage.

Symptom and success criterion

Initial cell thickness was abnormally high. A yellow unreacted-looking pattern was observed on teardown, but its composition was not asserted. The goal was to locate the stage where thickness increased and then check thickness, teardown appearance, capacity, and life after action.

Reproduction and measurement

Thickness was compared across electrode, J/R, before and after filling, pre-charge, seal pin, and formation. The increase became clear after pre-charge.

Local 4M+2E decision

4MCandidateEvidenceDecisionFollow-up
ManTeardown and thickness-measurement variationResults after fixing location and sequenceRemoved as primary causeKeep one measurement sequence
MachinePre-charge and degassing vacuum and compressionRepeated thickness change after pre-chargePrimary candidateRetest vacuum and compression
MaterialUncontrolled-moisture cells, Al2O3-coated separator, electrolyteNo clear difference in comparison trialsRemoved as primary causeMaintain change history
MethodGas-release sequence during pre-chargeStage thickness and teardown appearanceNarrowed as the core causeAdjust vacuum and compression
MeasurementStage thickness, teardown appearance, capacity, and lifeBefore/after results linked through the same cell flowDecision evidenceFix inspection and teardown criteria
EnvironmentMoisture-control conditionMoisture comparison did not explain the swellingRemoved as primary causeMaintain as a process requirement

Action and retest

Pre-charge and degassing vacuum and compression conditions were adjusted. Cells were rebuilt under the same flow and checked for thickness, teardown appearance, capacity, and life.

Result and standardization

Thickness decreased and capacity/life direction improved. The confirmed conditions were reflected in process sheets plus inspection and teardown criteria.

Case 03. Cap-plate C-cut acceptance range

03

Component change and acceptable range

Cap-plate C-cut acceptable-range validation

Seven conditions prepared by the parts team were built into real cells to propose a range that retained existing process and safety behavior.

Symptom, responsibility, and decision criteria

The parts team designed material/dimensional work and seven conditions. I owned incoming inspection, condition-specific cell builds, existing laser-welding and beading manufacturability, weld sectioning, cell teardown, internal beam checks, and pressure safety. The goal was an acceptable range that preserved existing process conditions and cell quality/safety.

Reproduction and measurement

Seven conditions × five cells, 35 cells total were compared first. Beading shape, can-cap gap, penetration depth, internal beam entry, burst pressure, and compression safety were checked.

Local 4M+2E decision

4MCandidateEvidenceDecisionFollow-up
ManCondition-specific build and inspection orderSame work and inspection basisRemoved as primary causeMaintain test order
MachineExisting laser-welding and beading settingsManufacturability and section results by conditionExisting-setting feasibilityRemove risky conditions
MaterialCap-plate geometry, thickness, and C-cut differenceParts-team measurement and incoming inspectionCore change axisLink to cell results
MethodSeven parts-team conditions and cell-test sequenceAssembly and safety results from 35 cellsAcceptable-range axisPropose range to supplier and parts team
MeasurementShape, gap, penetration, beam, burst, and compressionSectioning, teardown, and safety testsDecision evidenceReflect in IQC draft
EnvironmentPilot-line environmentComponent geometry explained more than environmentRemoved as primary causeRetain existing process conditions

Action and retest

Conditions that introduced risk were removed, and the candidate acceptance range was narrowed to conditions that retained the existing laser-welding, beading, and safety basis. Section, teardown, and pressure results were reviewed with the parts team, followed by a request for a 2,000-unit production trial.

Result and standardization

The acceptance range was proposed to the supplier and parts team, and an IQC revision draft was prepared. The changed parts did not arrive before my departure, leaving repeat production as the next stage.
