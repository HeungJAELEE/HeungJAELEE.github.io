FAT/SAT of 11 Core Machines for an NCM Prismatic Pilot Line

Acceptance, retesting, and production operation across 13 production processes plus a separate EOL height inspection

Project period
Employment May 2023–May 2024 · FAT May–Jul 2023 · SAT Jul–Nov 2023
Context
Completed at Jastech, now JAS Robotics, in the Cell Development Team
Scope
Acceptance and retesting of 11 core machines, process checks across 13 operations, and pilot planning and traceability

Problem and context

An automatic cycle on one machine does not mean a pilot line is ready. Upstream and downstream interfaces, cycle time, precision, repeatability, sensors, alarms, interlocks, operator safety, and recovery must work together. After product input, measurement variation must also be separated from process variation.

The scope covered 13 major production processes and 11 core machines spanning auto winding, pre-welding and short inspection, main welding, can/cap insertion, can-cap welding, beading appearance, Hi-Pot, X-ray alignment, filling, aging, HVC, degassing, and seal-pin welding. A separate EOL height-inspection unit was not counted among the 11.

What I owned

I compared equipment specifications with product requirements and prepared FAT/SAT acceptance sheets. The checks covered more than automatic motion: I/O and sensors, alarms, interlocks and fail-safe behavior, precision and repeatability, cycle time, intermediate-product dimensions and quality, operator safety, recipe storage and changes, recovery after faults, and consistent results after repeated operation.

Nonconformities were managed in one punch list containing the problem, risk, supplier, due date, retest method, and closure status. On the filling equipment, O-ring contact, sealing, and interchangeability were rechecked through leak, fill-volume, contamination, and repeated-run trials for about one month and roughly 20 batches before review and approval.

Continuous measurements such as height, weight, IR, and thickness were reviewed through Cpk. Destructive checks such as tensile tests used control limits and SPC criteria. Master jigs and Gage R&R were used to check measurement reliability.

Separating acceptance risks with 4M

FAT/SAT could not stop at confirming automatic motion. I separated acceptance risks with 4M and connected each item to the acceptance sheet and punch list.

4MRisk before acceptanceFAT/SAT check
ManFive operators shared several machines, so work order, abnormal response, and safety actions could varyStandardized roles, work sequence, alarm response, restart, and training items
MachinePrecision, repeatability, I/O, sensors, interlocks, O-ring sealing, cycle time, and recovery affected productionRepeated the same checks at FAT and post-installation SAT and registered gaps as punches
MaterialIntermediate dimensions, material lots, O-rings, and wear parts could affect welding, filling, and assemblyConnected incoming and intermediate tolerances and lot history to process results
MethodSupplier-specific checks, recipe changes, inspection decisions, rework, and retest methods could differUsed common acceptance sheets, Cpk/Gage R&R rules, and explicit punch-closure conditions

An issue that looked like a machine problem could begin with an intermediate-part tolerance or an inspection method. Each punch therefore recorded not only the corrective action but also the 4M factor and retest method to be checked.

Connecting machine acceptance and production with DMAIC

  1. Define — Separated the CTQs and acceptance scope for 13 production processes, 11 core machines, and the separate EOL inspection unit.
  2. Measure — Used consistent criteria for cycle time, precision, repeatability, Cpk, Gage R&R, alarms, interlocks, and initial production results.
  3. Analyze — Classified gaps with 4M and separated machine variation, material or intermediate-part differences, and measurement variation.
  4. Improve — Retested supplier corrections, O-ring sealing, J/R pre-forming preparation, and recipe changes against the same acceptance items.
  5. Control — Used punch closure, FAT/SAT sheets, manufacturing-control drawings, work standards, and cell-history lookup to keep acceptance conditions visible in production.

Process capability (Cpk) describes process output against specification. Gage R&R checks variation introduced by the measurement system and operators. They answer different questions and were reviewed separately.

Production operation and trace lookup

Initial production covered about 1,000 cells over roughly five days. Five operators had to share several machines, so materials, WIP, equipment state, and defects needed to be visible in one workflow.

Monthly MES CSV files were preserved as source records and merged with Power Query. A VBA search field used a cell serial number to retrieve process, equipment, and material history, linking lot–cell–process–machine–material without storing operator personal information.

Result and production handoff

  • Completed FAT/SAT for 11 core machines and documented acceptance criteria across 13 major production processes.
  • The representative initial snapshot covered about 1,000 cells over roughly five days and showed about 95% assembly yield, including reworked units that met the quality criteria.
  • Reduced J/R pre-forming preparation time from more than 20 seconds to 5 seconds or less.
  • Produced FAT/SAT sheets, punch lists, manufacturing-control drawings, work standards, and the production-history lookup flow.

The EOL height-inspection unit was managed separately from the 11 core systems. The 95% figure is an initial pilot-line team result and includes reworked units that later met the quality criteria. The tracking tool was used internally for about two months, but no exact time-saving measurement was recorded.
