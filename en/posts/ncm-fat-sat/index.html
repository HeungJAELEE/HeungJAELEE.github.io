Problem and context

An automatic cycle on one machine does not mean a pilot line is ready. Upstream and downstream interfaces, cycle time, precision, repeatability, sensors, alarms, interlocks, operator safety, and recovery must work together. After product input, measurement variation must also be separated from process variation.

This project covered 13 detailed processes and 11 core systems on an NCM 50Ah prismatic-cell assembly pilot line.

What I owned

  • Prepared FAT/SAT acceptance sheets and checked specifications, intermediate-part tolerances, and acceptance criteria.
  • Managed vendor punch lists, action dates, and retest results.
  • Reviewed process capability (Cpk) separately from the measurement system (Gage R&R).
  • Checked laser, ultrasonic, and resistance welding plus filling through tensile, cross-section, leak, Hi-Pot, IR, and X-ray results.
  • Managed materials, WIP, staffing, and lot history for a 200-cell daily plan.
  • Used and improved an Excel VBA tracking sheet where MES was not yet available.

What I checked and changed

Stage Checks Method
FAT Function, cycle, I/O, alarms, interlocks, safety Specification and acceptance-sheet comparison; punch registration
Installation Utilities, reference points, precision, repeatability Recheck after installation
SAT Interfaces, sample run, restart, and recovery Repeated tests under site conditions
Process check Cpk, Gage R&R, welding, and filling Compared process and measurement variation separately
Production operation Material, WIP, people, lots, daily output Managed priorities against the 200-cell plan

Process capability (Cpk) describes the stability of process output against specification. Gage R&R checks variation from the measurement system and operators. They are not one “process index.”

Result and limits

  • Completed FAT/SAT and retesting for 13 processes and 11 core systems.
  • The representative initial snapshot covered about 1,000 cells over roughly five days and showed about 95% assembly yield, including reworked units that met the quality criteria.
  • Documented work standards, incoming-evaluation criteria, and production-history rules.

The EOL height-inspection unit is separate from the 11 core machines. These are pilot-production results, not long-term mass-production Cpk or a claim about every machine’s individual capability. Detailed Gage R&R values, equipment drawings, customer specifications, recipes, defect images, and raw data remain private.

The article prioritizes the equipment-acceptance and production-operation flow that I can explain in an interview. A future public artifact could be a fully de-identified FAT/SAT checklist example, not company evidence.
