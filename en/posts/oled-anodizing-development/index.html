High-Temperature Anodizing Film Development for OLED Parts

Addressing crack and particle issues under repeated high-temperature use through sample scale-up and bath-control criteria

Project period
Development and production application during YMC employment
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Cause hypotheses, sample scale-up, analysis criteria, customer evaluation, and production documentation

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Film cracking and particles recurred within warranty life under repeated high-temperature use.
What I owned
A colleague handled recipe and bath analysis; I handled staged sample production and evaluation, inspection criteria, and customer-result feedback.
Verified result
The revised condition entered production and was monitored for about a year through the transition from legacy-condition parts.

Development flow

OLED process parts are repeatedly exposed to high temperature, so a normal initial appearance and film thickness do not guarantee field life. Cracks were also found in retained samples. I therefore reviewed material, pretreatment, coating conditions, bath state, and inspection together instead of assigning the issue only to customer use.

01

Scale-up Validation

High-temperature cracking and particles

The film, bath, scale, and customer-use conditions were validated as one controlled system.

Problem and success criteria

  • Film cracking and particles recurred within the warranty period.
  • The issue affected maintenance intervals and warranted part life.
  • The direction had to hold on near-full-size parts and under customer-use conditions, not only on internal coupons.

Small-sample screening

  • Compared cracking and surface condition across film conditions and thicknesses.
  • Narrowed candidates before moving to more expensive large-part tests.
  • Recorded bath analysis together with recipe conditions.

Scale-up and retest

  • Applied the candidate condition to medium samples and checked location-dependent film and appearance variation.
  • Moved to near-full-size samples and confirmed whether geometry and area changed the direction.
  • Reviewed uniformity and repeatability, not only averages.

Customer-use validation

  • Brought customer-evaluation findings back into process and inspection review.
  • Repeated checks until internal and customer evaluations moved in the same direction.
  • Did not treat one internal pass as production completion.

Result and control

  • Applied the improved condition in production.
  • Tracked the application for about one year through a transition period with legacy-condition parts.
  • Left the film recipe, bath-analysis criteria, sample scale-up sequence, inspection method, and customer checks as one control set.

My scope and collaboration

  1. Direct scope
    • Staged sample fabrication and inspection
    • Location-based variation review during scale-up
    • Reconfirmation of customer-evaluation findings
    • Inspection and decision-criteria documentation
  2. Joint work
    • A colleague led recipe and bath analysis.
    • We connected coating conditions with chemical control criteria.
  3. Publication boundary
    • Company recipes, chemical compositions, and internal specifications are not published.
    • The unverified 30% to 5% claim is not used.

Principles retained

  • Do not set production conditions from lab results alone.
  • Recheck area and geometry variation at every scale-up stage.
  • Design recipe, bath analysis, and inspection controls together.
  • Finalize shop-floor controls only after customer-use validation.
