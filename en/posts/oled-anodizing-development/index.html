Field application

High-Temperature Anodizing Film Development for OLED Parts

Addressing crack and particle issues under repeated high-temperature use through sample scale-up and bath-control criteria

Project period
Development and production application during YMC employment
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Cause hypotheses, sample scale-up, analysis criteria, customer evaluation, and production documentation

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Film cracking and particles recurred within warranty life under repeated high-temperature use.
What I owned
A colleague handled recipe and bath analysis; I handled staged sample production and evaluation, inspection criteria, and customer-result feedback.
Verified result
The revised condition entered production and was monitored for about a year through the transition from legacy-condition parts.

Problem and success criteria

Coating cracks and particles appeared on OLED process components during repeated high-temperature use. Similar cracks were also found on retained samples, so customer use conditions could not be treated as the only cause. Pretreatment, coating conditions, bath, material, and measurement method were compared in the same test record.

AreaScope

Causes and measurement method

Staged scale-up

Small-sample results were not transferred directly to full-size conditions. The scope expanded through four stages.

StageValidation targetDecision
1Small sampleCompare cracks and surface state by film condition and thickness
2Medium sampleCheck position-dependent film and appearance variation
3Near-full-size partConfirm reproduction after geometry and area increase
4Customer use conditionConfirm the same direction under repeated actual use

At each stage, process conditions, bath, material, and measurement position were connected in the same trial record before moving to the next size.

Result and standards left behind

After the revised condition entered production, its application was followed for about one year, including the transition period when legacy-condition parts remained. The public quantitative scope is therefore four-stage scale-up plus about one year of follow-up, together with film recipes, bath-analysis criteria, sampling locations, inspection methods, and customer checks.
