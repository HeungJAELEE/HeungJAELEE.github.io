High-Temperature Anodizing Development for Display Equipment Parts

Mixed-acid film redesign for an OLED dry-etch upper-electrode part and oxalic-acid high-temperature qualification for a display PECVD susceptor

Project period
Mixed-acid film development Jan–Jun 2017 · oxalic-acid high-temperature qualification Jul–Oct 2018
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Defect-history and sample review, condition-based production, thermal and Hi-Pot evaluation, 100×100 to 500×500 scale-up, customer qualification, and standards

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
A higher OLED process temperature exposed cracks and particles in a mixed-acid film for upper-electrode-type parts, while susceptor-type parts required a separate high-temperature qualification.
What I owned
Using a Six Sigma GB problem-solving sequence, I produced and evaluated samples, separated causes with 4M+2E, ran scale-up tests, and documented process and inspection criteria.
Verified result
The mixed-acid condition passed customer qualification and received about one year of additional follow-up; the oxalic-acid condition completed repeated 450℃ screening and customer vacuum-heat qualification before about one year of use.

Project overview

This page separates two high-temperature anodizing projects completed in YMC’s Technical R&D Center. Both concerned display-equipment parts, but they began with different problems and used different completion gates.

I earned a Six Sigma Green Belt in my final university year and used that problem-solving sequence in the field. These were not formally registered Six Sigma projects with charters and tollgates. I checked the phenomenon and measurement first, narrowed causes with 4M, 5 Why, Pareto, and comparison trials, and then closed the work through retesting and standards.

01

Production-condition redesign from a DMAIC perspective

Mixed-acid anodizing for an OLED dry-etch upper-electrode part

Crack, particle, and etch-rate issues after a process-temperature increase were checked from small samples through large-area parts.

Why the film was redesigned

As the OLED process temperature increased, upper-electrode-type parts using the existing mixed-acid anodized film showed lower etch rate, more particles, and cracking. Rather than changing the bath immediately, I first reviewed defect run sheets, certificates, work dates, sample specifications, temperature and humidity, and bath history.

The completion criteria were:

  1. no cracks after thermal exposure at the customer-requested film thickness;
  2. Hi-Pot results meeting the internal thickness-specific criteria on the same sample;
  3. reproduction of the 100×100 mm result on a 500×500 mm large-area sample; and
  4. customer qualification followed by production application.

Actual validation sequence

Review defect history and legacy conditions
→ build samples by variable
→ compare the commonly requested 40, 45, and 50 μm range
→ 24-hour thermal exposure in air
→ repeat the same condition at least three times
→ check cracks, appearance, and Hi-Pot
→ 100×100 mm sample
→ 500×500 mm large-area sample
→ customer qualification
→ production application

The 100×100 and 500×500 figures are sample dimensions, not quantities. Large-area trials rechecked current distribution, racking, and position-dependent variation before the small-sample condition was transferred.

A representative historical comparison at 50 μm recorded approximately 260℃ to 350℃ in thermal comparison and 2.4 kV to 2.7 kV in Hi-Pot comparison. These values describe one representative condition and are not a common specification for every 40, 45, and 50 μm product. The exact voltage ramp and internal criteria remain confidential.

Result and standardization

  • The customer qualified the revised condition and it entered actual product use.
  • The application was followed for about one additional year after development, including the transition from legacy-condition parts.
  • Run sheets, trial reports, change reports, process standards, and inspection criteria were updated.
  • Film recipes, bath composition, customer specifications, and part numbers are not published.
02

Requirement, sample gate, and customer qualification

Oxalic-acid anodizing for a display PECVD susceptor

A global equipment company's high-temperature requirement was separated into internal air screening and customer vacuum-heat qualification.

Requirement and design constraints

A susceptor-type part that supports and heats glass substrates in a display PECVD chamber required higher-temperature reliability. Because the existing bath concentration could not be changed freely during the collaboration period, film conditions, thickness, hybrid approaches, and racking position were used as design alternatives.

This was not presented as a DMAIC mass-production improvement. It followed these gates:

Confirm the high-temperature requirement
→ compare film, thickness, and racking alternatives
→ internal air screening
→ build separate customer samples to the passing specification
→ customer vacuum-heat qualification
→ actual product use

Why internal screening and customer qualification were separated

SamplesThe same three samplesSeparate samples built to the internally accepted specification

The same three samples completed five repeated 450℃ × 24-hour cycles in air. Cracks were checked visually and by microscope after each cycle. Separate samples were then built to the same accepted specification for the customer’s vacuum heat test.

Result and publication boundary

  • Samples built to the internal passing condition entered customer qualification.
  • The customer vacuum-heat qualification was completed, followed by about one year of actual use.
  • Although management reported a comparison with several candidate suppliers, I did not directly inspect the other suppliers’ conditions or results, so the public page does not claim that YMC was the sole passing supplier.
  • Exact equipment models, recipes, film settings, and customer specifications are not published.

Confirmed result and limits

Both projects moved beyond selecting a film condition into sample scale-up, repeated testing, customer qualification, and actual use. The mixed-acid case was a production-stabilization response to changed use conditions, while the oxalic-acid case was a technical qualification for a new high-temperature requirement.

Representative 50 μm values are shown only with their test condition in the body. Defect-rate figures, exact recipes and bath-analysis values, customer specifications, approval documents, and competitor results are not published as outcomes.
