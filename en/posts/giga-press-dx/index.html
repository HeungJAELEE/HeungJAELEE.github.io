What the team set out to solve

Adding equipment does not guarantee a higher output. Cycle time, bottlenecks, transfer distance, and buffers have to be considered together. The team created a hypothetical Giga Press expansion scenario to review production flow and bottleneck candidates in a virtual factory before physical installation.

I planned the project purpose, overall structure, and team roles and wrote the final report. Teammates implemented the detailed Factory I/O, ladder, and SCADA work. I owned the connection that translated virtual-factory state and production data into analysis inputs and manufacturing decision items.

Production flow and bottleneck candidates came before equipment count

02

DESIGN DECISION

Process time and transfer time were separated to locate congestion

Equipment count was reviewed only after waiting and buffer locations became visible.

Decision sequence

  1. The target output was decomposed into process cycle times.
  2. Transfer time and buffer waiting were connected separately.
  3. Factory I/O flow and PLC/SCADA state were checked against the same process.
  4. Equipment count and layout scenarios were reviewed after congestion candidates became visible.

Verified scope

  • The team demo identified roughly one second of congestion at a T-shaped logistics junction as a bottleneck candidate.
  • Normal communication and virtual production flow were connected in one training demonstration.
  • This was not a finalized optimized layout or a measured plant-productivity improvement.
  • Communication loss, fault/recovery, and AI-result feedback to the PLC remain future gates.

Team result and current stage

The ten-person team connected Factory I/O, PLC, SCADA, OPC UA, and SQLite and demonstrated virtual production flow and production metrics. I owned overall planning, the report, and the state-data-to-analysis-input boundary.

The verified result is the normal data flow and expansion-scenario review. Plant application would still require measured cycle time and material flow, safety PLC integration, vendor protocols, fault and communication recovery, hardware-in-the-loop testing, and FAT/SAT acceptance criteria.
