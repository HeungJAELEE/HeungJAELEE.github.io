    TECHNICAL CASE STUDY

    GIGA Press Virtual-Factory Expansion

    A 10-person training PoC that simplified machining, inspection, tray transfer, and stacking in Factory I/O, then defined the boundaries among PLC, SCADA, OPC UA, web, and AI. I led the overall project planning, wrote the final report, and owned the Digital Twin–AI integration scope.

    I was not the project manager or the sole developer. The team lead managed schedule, risk, and the final presentation. I led the overall structure and integration order, wrote the final report, and directly worked on the Digital Twin–AI connection. This distinction shows system-level judgement without attributing teammates’ Ladder and SCADA work to me.

    How the problem was framed

    A GIGA Press can consolidate many body components and downstream assembly steps, but its scale and capital cost make repeated physical try-out expensive. The team therefore treated the assignment as a virtual-commissioning and interface problem.

    This project did not model molten-metal filling, die thermal deformation, casting pressure, or other physical die-casting behavior. Factory I/O represented the GIGA Press stage with a simplified CNC process, followed by inspection, tray transfer, stacker loading, and release.

    Material input → machining (CNC substitute) → good/defect decision
               → good tray transfer → stacker loading → release command
               → defect discharge

    The precise question was therefore:

    Not whether the project reproduced a full GIGA Press, but whether process sequence and cross-layer data responsibility could be reviewed before physical equipment arrived.

    The important design decision was not merely adding AI. It was defining who owns final machine authority.

    LayerResponsibilityBoundary
    Factory I/OReproduce process motion and virtual sensor/actuator statesDoes not prove hydraulic, servo, die, or safety behavior
    PLC/LadderSequence, interlocks, stop/restart logic, and final run permissionDoes not execute network or AI output without validation
    SCADADisplay state, alarms, and operator-command resultsDoes not replace safety circuits or PLC interlocks
    OPC UA/WebProvide a readable upper-layer data pathSuccessful communication does not prove data validity or safety
    AI candidateEvaluate optimization possibilities from equipment stateDoes not own emergency stop, safety judgement, or machine authority

    The training report uses language about DQN real-time learning and control. This public case deliberately limits that claim to DQN candidate selection and integration design. There was no repeated live-equipment dataset, validated response time, safety-constrained closed loop, or production comparison.

    Decisions and deliverables I owned

    1. Fixed the virtual-commissioning scope before discussing AI

    Claiming to reproduce the full physics of a GIGA Press within a short training project would create an unverifiable scope. I limited the scenario to machining–inspection–transfer–stacking and focused the team on the PLC, SCADA, OPC UA, web, and AI interfaces that could actually be demonstrated.

    2. Connected separate team implementations through one integration order

    The overall plan followed this sequence:

    1. Define normal flow and stop conditions in Factory I/O.
    2. Make the PLC own step transitions and interlocks.
    3. Show the same state and alarms from the operator’s SCADA perspective.
    4. Define the upper-layer data boundary through OPC UA.
    5. Allow web and AI consumers to read the data without taking PLC authority.

    Teammates implemented the detailed Ladder/Factory I/O and SCADA/OPC UA components. My contribution was to define how those outputs fit one process meaning and sequence, then integrate the team result in the final report.

    3. Defined the Digital Twin–AI insertion point

    I did not begin with the statement that AI would autonomously control the line. I first separated:

    • which virtual-sensor and PLC states could become AI inputs;
    • what recommendation, rather than direct machine command, could be returned;
    • how the recommendation would remain below PLC interlocks and run permission;
    • how stale data, network loss, and reconnection would invalidate the recommendation.

    This established a DQN candidate location and data path. It did not prove model superiority or field performance.

    4. Separated verified output from expected benefit in the final report

    The team material includes expected statements such as a 50–75% setup-time reduction, 50% lower expansion effort, minimized MTTR, and zero hardware-damage risk. These were not measured through a live-factory before/after comparison and are not presented as my achievements.

    • Ladder linked to the Factory I/O virtual equipment;
    • a SCADA view linked to stacker states and the virtual line;

    These screens support an integrated training demonstration across multiple layers. They do not support the following claims:

    • deployed predictive-maintenance or energy-management models.

    Interview questions and answer boundaries

    Why use a Digital Twin?

    To review missing sensors, sequence delays, stops, restarts, and cross-layer signal mismatches before discovering them on physical equipment. The value of this PoC is the pre-commissioning logic and interface review process, not a high-fidelity physical model.

    Why did AI not directly control the PLC?

    AI inference and networks contain latency and uncertainty, while the training model had no validated safety integrity. Final run permission and interlocks therefore remain in the PLC, with AI limited to an upper-layer recommendation candidate.

    Why was DQN selected?

    Its state–action–value structure was suitable for explaining action candidates under different process states in a training setting. The project did not conclude that DQN was better than alternative reinforcement-learning methods. State, reward, safety constraints, and baselines require new comparison before field use.

    What is the largest limitation?

    Factory I/O cannot replace live GIGA Press physics or its safety system. The project also lacks representative field data, long-duration communication testing, operator-alarm validation, and AI closed-loop testing.

    Validation required before field use

    1. Hardware-in-the-Loop testing with real I/O and a safety PLC.
    2. Missing-sensor, stale-value, network-loss, reconnect, and restart scenarios.
    3. Quality and timestamp alignment for AI inputs and explicit recommendation invalidation.
    4. PLC interlocks, approve/reject/rollback procedures, and manual-operation priority.
    5. Before/after measures under the same basis: debugging time, tag mismatches, recovery time, and retest count.
    6. Stability under real takt, load, and long-duration operation.
