Project overview

This project was not a high-fidelity simulation of GIGA Press casting physics. The team simplified raw-material input, machining, inspection, tray transfer, stacker loading, and discharge in Factory I/O so that sequence and equipment state could be checked before repeated physical try-out.

The PLC controlled the process order, and OPC UA carried the same state into SCADA and the web interface. AI did not directly control the equipment. The DQN work stopped at defining state and action candidates and identifying where the model could be connected without replacing PLC safety control.

The project was completed by a ten-person training team. I worked in the Digital Twin team and organized the manufacturing scenario, system layers, state interfaces, AI boundary, and communication setup. Detailed PLC Ladder and SCADA implementation belonged to the assigned teammates. I also wrote the final report that connected the team outputs into one manufacturing flow.

The project did not measure try-out cost reduction or chronic-loss improvement. Its completed scope was the operating sequence and state-transfer structure in a training environment.

Team objectives and completion criteria

Team objectives

  1. Simplify the GIGA Press process into operations available in Factory I/O.
  2. Build the sequence from material input to stacker discharge.
  3. Connect Start, Reset, Stop, and Emergency Stop inputs to the PLC.
  4. Align PLC and Factory I/O process-stage and equipment-state definitions.
  5. Transfer PLC state to SCADA and web monitoring through OPC UA.
  6. Display rack and stacker position and loading state consistently.
  7. Define DQN state/action candidates and the integration boundary.
  8. Demonstrate the normal integrated flow.

My role — Digital Twin system design and integration

I organized the process sequence and system interfaces needed to move the manufacturing flow into Factory I/O.

Material input, machining, inspection, tray transfer, stacker loading, and discharge were separated into process stages. I then organized the state that PLC, Factory I/O, SCADA, and web monitoring needed to exchange at each stage. I also separated the PLC control domain from the DQN analysis domain so the proposed AI layer did not appear to replace safety control.

The assigned teammates implemented the detailed Ladder and SCADA screens. I reviewed whether the subsystem outputs described the same process and equipment state and documented the architecture, AI scope, and limitations in the final report.

Team design review — Ladder modularization

The proposed structure separated functions such as Emit, Machine, Move, Stacker, and Vision. The report’s 50% reduction in expansion effort was an expected benefit, not a measured result of this PoC.

Future extension design — DQN

Rack, stacker, and equipment conditions were organized as state candidates, while movement, loading, and discharge were action candidates. The next step would record the recommendation, operator approval, and PLC result separately in a shadow test or HIL setup.
