Project objective

GIGA Press can consolidate many part-production, assembly, and welding operations into a large casting process, but the equipment scale, capital burden, and try-out effort are also substantial. The report proposed an autonomous-factory Digital Twin training PoC that could check a virtual process, control state, and monitoring interface before repeated testing on physical equipment.

The scope was not a high-fidelity physical simulation of molten-metal casting. The team simplified the process into equipment available in Factory I/O, then connected raw-material input, processing, inspection, loading, and discharge with PLC, SCADA, OPC UA, web monitoring, and a future AI-control architecture.

Why a Digital Twin was needed

Manufacturing problemLimitation of the conventional approachWhat the Digital Twin was intended to check
Extended try-outPhysical equipment and material are repeatedly used to search for operating conditionsSequence and interlock before physical installation
Weak data integrityDesign, PLC, equipment, and monitoring may use inconsistent state meaningsWhether Factory I/O, PLC, and SCADA refer to the same state
Chronic-loss investigationDegradation is difficult to locate when equipment state and the virtual model are disconnectedArchitecture linking state, alarms, history, and future analytical inputs

The project did not measure the financial benefit of these items. Its completed scope was the normal operating flow of the virtual process, communication, control, and monitoring in a training environment.

Simplified GIGA Press process in the virtual factory

Factory I/O did not provide a GIGA Press asset, so the die-processing stage was simplified as a CNC process. Product color represented inspection status; good parts moved through the tray and stacker, while defects were discharged separately.

flowchart LR A["Raw-material input"] --> B["Die processing\nsimplified as CNC in Factory I/O"] --> C{"Defect inspection"} C -->|Defect| D["Defect discharge"] C -->|Good| E["Tray transfer"] --> F["Stacker loading"] --> G["Stacker discharge\nshipping command"]

The connected sequence was raw-material input → die processing → defect inspection → tray transfer → stacker loading → stacker discharge.

OPC UA provided the boundary through which PLC, SCADA, web, and the proposed AI layer could share tag meaning. Communication connectivity was not treated as proof of safe process control.

Based on the separately confirmed contribution record, I also wrote the final report that connected each team’s implementation into one manufacturing flow. Detailed ladder and SCADA implementation remains attributed to the respective teams.

Implemented result 1 — Factory I/O and PLC control

The team checked Start, Reset, Stop, and Emergency Stop inputs together with PLC Ladder state. This is a team result connecting virtual-process input to control logic; detailed ladder authoring belonged to the PLC team.

Implemented result 2 — SCADA and stacker monitoring

Rack and stacker loading state were reviewed in both the virtual factory and the SCADA interface. The purpose was to confirm that virtual equipment position and loading status retained the same operational meaning in the monitoring layer.

Implemented result 3 — Web operating view

The web prototype displayed equipment state, rack location, production information, and analysis candidates. It was an operating-interface prototype, not validated predictive-maintenance or optimal-control performance.

Ladder modularization as a design direction

The report proposed separating spaghetti-style ladder into process-oriented programs or function blocks. Functions such as Emit, Machine, Move, Stacker, and Vision can reduce the code area that must be reviewed during a change or fault investigation.

ObjectiveWhat the project producedValidation still required
Functional responsibilityProcess-oriented program structureCompare engineering effort before and after expansion
Narrower fault scopeDesign that isolates the affected functionFault injection and measured MTTR
ReuseBlock structure for repeated operationsReuse testing on another layout and PLC

The report’s 50% expansion-effort reduction was an expected benefit, not a measured result of this PoC.

How far DQN was applied

The report selected a Q-Learning/DQN concept in which rack, stacker, and equipment conditions form the state and movement, loading, and discharge commands form the actions. The discrete state-action representation was used as the selection rationale.

In the public project scope, DQN remained at the model-selection and integration-design stage. Sub-0.1-second response, real-time learning, direct AI-to-PLC commands, and autonomous process-variation control were not supported by a separate benchmark or closed-loop safety test.

The next validation step is Hardware-in-the-Loop and shadow testing that keeps DQN output separate from PLC safety control. Recommended action, operator approval, and PLC execution results need independent records without bypassing interlocks.

Team result and next stage

The ten-person team completed a training demonstration between approximately April 20 and 30, connecting a Factory I/O virtual process, PLC/Ladder, SCADA and OPC UA, and a web interface. I organized the manufacturing scenario, layer boundaries, Digital Twin–AI integration, and final report.

Predictive maintenance, DQN/PPO autonomous control, MADRL energy management, and setup-time reduction were future goals or external benchmarks in the report. The next stage should first validate disconnect, stale value, timeout, reconnect, equipment fault and recovery, measured cycle and logistics data, and a physical-PLC Hardware-in-the-Loop setup.
