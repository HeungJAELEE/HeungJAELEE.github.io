Giga Press Factory Expansion through a Digital Twin

A 10-person manufacturing-DX PoC that checked process sequence, communications boundaries, and failure conditions before physical commissioning

30-second summary

Debugging sequence and communication for the first time on physical equipment is expensive, so the project demonstrated why signal ownership and failure cases should be checked in a virtual environment first.

The flow below generalizes the team exercise for a virtual-commissioning context.

Why the shop-floor decision mattered

With high-capital equipment, a sequence or communications problem discovered after installation is more expensive and riskier to correct. When sensor state, PLC logic, and operator displays disagree, the team also spends time deciding which information is authoritative. This project used a Giga Press factory context to examine how process and data connections could be checked before physical commissioning.

The scope was not a complete production Giga Press. It was a team PoC that connected Factory I/O, PLC, SCADA, and an upstream communications path to define what should be tested before moving to physical equipment.

Why a digital method was needed

Testing only on physical equipment can reveal missing sensors, delayed completion signals, and restart conditions late in the project. A virtual process allows those abnormal cases to be repeated while the team checks whether control state and displayed data remain consistent.

The team kept control authority in the PLC. Factory I/O represented the virtual process, the PLC owned sequence and interlock decisions, SCADA displayed state and alarms, and the OPC UA path defined how upstream systems and an AI candidate could receive data. AI remained an optimization candidate rather than a safety controller.

My actual role

This was a ten-person team project. My scope in the Digital Twin group was limited to AI-model selection and training, communications setup, and technical research. I reviewed a DQN candidate using realtime sensor and PLC data and organized where the OPC UA communications path fit within the team scenario.

Ladder and SCADA implementation belonged to other team members. They are described as parts of the team architecture, not as my individual work.

Data flow

Field signals → PLC sequence and interlock decisions → Factory I/O virtual process and SCADA state display → OPC UA boundary → web and AI candidates

The layers see related signals but have different responsibilities. A displayed or AI-generated value does not become a safety command by itself; run permission and safe behavior during communications loss stay within PLC and dedicated safety design.

Implementation sequence

  1. The team defined the normal process sequence together with stop, delay, and restart conditions.
  2. The responsible team member connected Factory I/O scenarios to Ladder logic.
  3. The responsible team member configured SCADA state and alarm visibility.
  4. I selected and trained the AI candidate and handled communications setup and supporting research.
  5. The team integrated PLC, SCADA, Factory I/O, web, and AI-extension concepts into one demonstration and report.
  6. The report kept projected benefits separate from measured outcomes.

Verified output and current stage

The verified output is the team demonstration and report connecting process flow, Ladder and Factory I/O, SCADA, web, and an AI-extension structure. Validation with a production Giga Press safety PLC, hydraulic and servo dynamics, and real production I/O is outside the completed scope.

The report’s 50–75% setup-time reduction and 50% equipment-expansion labor reduction are expected benefits or targets for virtual commissioning and Ladder modularization. They are not controlled before-and-after plant results and are not presented as achievements.

Deployment conditions and company value

Field use requires hardware-in-the-loop tests with actual I/O and safety requirements, communications-loss and reconnect tests, versioned tag specifications, alarm priorities, network segmentation, and access-control review. If an AI candidate is used, it cannot own run or safety commands; recommendations need approval, rejection, and rollback paths.

Under those conditions, virtual commissioning could move logic and data-integrity checks earlier than physical trial and give teams a reusable review standard. The company value is earlier change-impact review and accumulated commissioning knowledge. Time or labor savings can be confirmed only after measurement against a controlled baseline.

Problem

PLC sequences, SCADA tags, sensor faults, and network delay are difficult to validate together before physical equipment, so defects are discovered late during plant trial.

Goal

Define the Factory I/O–PLC–SCADA–OPC UA responsibilities and data contract first, then review normal, failure, and restart scenarios in a virtual environment.

Success

The team demo and report must trace data flow, ownership, and tests while clearly separating expected benefits from measured plant outcomes.

Scope and boundaries

In scope

  • Signal flow and ownership across Factory I/O, PLC, SCADA, and OPC UA
  • Normal run, missing sensor, communication delay, and restart scenarios
  • Review of a DQN candidate using real-time sensor and PLC data

Out of scope or unverified

  • Production deployment on a real Giga Press safety PLC
  • Presenting Ladder or SCADA work by teammates as my individual work
  • Treating the 50–75% and 50% targets as measured factory results

Implementation and verification

What had to work

  • Shared tags include equipment, signal, value, quality, and timestamp.
  • The PLC owns interlocks and final run permission; AI provides advisory output only.
  • SCADA traces status, alarms, delay, and manual-command results through the same tags.

Quality and operating rules

  • Communication loss moves the process to a safe state instead of holding the last command.
  • Failure and recovery scenarios are repeated in addition to the normal path.
  • Targets, team results, individual contribution, and unverified work remain separate.

Why it was designed this way

  1. Keep control authority in the PLC

    Non-deterministic AI output or network delay must not bypass safety interlocks.

  2. Use OPC UA as the upper-layer boundary

    Semantic tags and quality metadata reduce coupling between control, SCADA, and analytics.

  3. Inject failures in Factory I/O first

    Missing sensors, delay, and mode conflicts are safer and cheaper to repeat virtually.
