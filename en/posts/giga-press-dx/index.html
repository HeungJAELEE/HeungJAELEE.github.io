Project background and vision

The GIGA Press flow was abstracted into operations available in Factory I/O: material input, CNC machining, defect inspection, tray transfer, stacker loading, and discharge.

The objective was not the 3D view itself. It was a shared state and history basis through which process engineers could review cycle time, transfer time, buffer state, production count, and bottleneck candidates across PLC, virtual equipment, monitoring, and history.

Control and analysis authority were separated by design. The PLC retained sequence and interlock authority. The AI layer consumed OPC UA and SQLite state in read-only form. Its outputs were process-engineering advisory candidates for input timing, bottleneck review, buffer/stacker operation, and scale-up scenarios rather than equipment actuation commands.

The central architecture decision was the decoupling of the PLC control domain from the AI advisory domain. Safety and motion authority remained in the PLC, while the AI layer analyzed state and history for engineering decisions.

Integrated implementation evidence

Factory I/O and PLC control

Start, Reset, Stop, and E-Stop input were compared with PLC step and Factory I/O motion. Detailed Ladder authoring belonged to the PLC team.

SCADA, rack, and stacker state

Rack position, stacker loading state, and alarms were reviewed under the same meaning in SCADA and the virtual process.

Web process-engineering view

The web screen placed equipment state and production information in one process-engineering view. The same user flow can host future advisory output.

Function-oriented PLC structure proposal

The report proposed separating Emit, Machine, Move, Stacker, and Vision functions so process changes and scenarios could be reviewed by module. Detailed implementation belonged to the PLC team.

Advisory Agent extension design

The DQN candidate was placed after OPC UA and SQLite. Rack, stacker, cycle, buffer, and production count were state candidates; outputs were converted into bottleneck, input, capacity, and scale-up scenario candidates rather than equipment commands.
