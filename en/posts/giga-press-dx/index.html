Giga Press Factory Expansion through a Digital Twin

A 10-person manufacturing-DX PoC that checked process sequence, communications boundaries, and failure conditions before physical commissioning

The first minute

In this case, “Giga Press” names a training scenario in which a factory adds another process cell around a large casting machine. Instead of waiting for the equipment to arrive before aligning PLC sequences, screen tags, and upstream data, the team connected them around a virtual machine and repeated failure cases first.

The factory-expansion scenario

The team assumed that a factory was adding a cell similar to an existing process. Copying the machine is not enough. The teams also need to agree on the sensor sequence, when the previous process sends completion, which alarms the operator sees, and whether upstream systems understand equipment state under the same names.

If that work starts only on the physical machine, missing sensors, delayed completion signals, manual/automatic mode conflicts, and restart conditions may appear late. This PoC used Factory I/O in place of the machine while connecting the PLC sequence, SCADA display, and OPC UA data flow. It reviewed stop and recovery conditions as well as the normal run.

Each component had a distinct responsibility.

Component Responsibility Not its responsibility
Factory I/O Run a virtual process with sensors and actuators and repeat fault conditions Prove hydraulic, servo, or safety behavior of the physical machine
PLC and Ladder Decide process sequence, interlocks, and final run permission Execute an AI output without review
SCADA Display state, alarms, and operator-action results Replace the safety circuit or PLC interlocks
OPC UA Carry PLC data to upper layers as tags Guarantee a value is safe merely because communication succeeded
Web and AI candidates Display state or examine optimization potential Own emergency stop or machine authority

The Ladder and SCADA implementations belonged to the responsible teammates. I do not present this architecture as a solo build. My work was to review and train the DQN candidate, set up the communications path including OPC UA, and research how the components should meet at their boundaries.

How one signal moves

Consider a virtual “die ready” signal.

  1. A virtual sensor in Factory I/O creates the ready state.
  2. The PLC checks the current mode, completion of the preceding step, and related interlocks.
  3. If the conditions agree, the PLC permits the next action. If they do not, it keeps the process stopped with a cause.
  4. SCADA presents the same state as an equipment name and alarm an operator can understand.
  5. OPC UA carries equipment, signal, value, quality, and time information to upper layers.
  6. A web view or AI candidate consumes that data, but it does not bypass the PLC safety decision to move equipment.

The team design treated matching signal names as insufficient. equipment, signal, value, quality, and timestamp were needed together so a stale value after communications loss would not look like a current healthy state. The actual tag list and equipment addresses remain in internal team material and are not reproduced here.

My model and communications work

DQN candidate selection and training

I selected and trained a DQN candidate to examine whether real-time sensor and PLC states could support process optimization. The important questions were not the model name alone:

  • Which PLC and sensor states should become observations?
  • How should a reward distinguish shorter cycle behavior from safety violations?
  • Where should an AI output remain advisory so it cannot bypass PLC interlocks?

The project has no repeated production-machine measurement of model performance or operating benefit. The DQN is therefore described as an upper-layer optimization candidate, not a production controller.

OPC UA setup and technical research

I organized where the OPC UA connection belonged so PLC values could retain a common meaning across SCADA, web, and AI layers. The setup considered the meaning of a tag, communication quality, and time rather than transferring addresses alone. I also researched why loss of communication, stale values, and state synchronization after reconnect should be separate test cases, then reflected those concerns in the team scenario.

Work sequence

  1. Define the expansion case. The team mapped the normal sequence plus stop, delay, missing-sensor, manual/automatic mode, and restart conditions.
  2. Assign signal responsibility. It separated PLC decisions, SCADA display, and data sent to upper layers.
  3. Connect virtual process and control. The responsible teammate connected Factory I/O scenarios to Ladder logic.
  4. Build the operator view. The responsible teammate configured SCADA state, alarms, and action results on the same signal basis.
  5. Review AI and communications candidates. I selected and trained the DQN candidate, set up the OPC UA flow, and researched the supporting design.
  6. Integrate normal, failure, and recovery flows. The team combined the parts into one demonstration and report.
  7. Review claim boundaries. The team output, individual contribution, expected effects, and uncompleted plant validation were kept separate.

Problems and decisions

Problem Decision Reason
The virtual machine, PLC, and screen can interpret one signal differently Define a shared tag meaning together with value, quality, and time A screen can look healthy while showing a stale value
Failure cases are difficult and risky to repeat on physical equipment Review missing sensors, delay, and restart in Factory I/O first Find logic and display errors before physical trial
AI response and networks introduce delay and variability Keep final run permission and interlocks in the PLC An optimization candidate must not replace safety decisions
Different people implemented different layers Separate the team result from individual roles in the document Explain the whole context without overstating individual implementation
The report included efficiency targets Label targets separately from measured results Prevent expected effects from being mistaken for achieved outcomes

Verified result and potential benefits

The verified result is the ten-person team demonstration and report connecting the process flow, Factory I/O and Ladder, SCADA, OPC UA, and the placement of web and AI extensions. It showed a method for reviewing sequence, signal names, alarms, and communications failures on one basis before equipment installation.

The report’s 50–75% setup-time reduction and 50% equipment-expansion labor reduction are expected effects or targets for successful virtual commissioning and Ladder modularization. They are not controlled before-and-after plant measurements and are not presented as project achievements.

After a controlled comparison against a plant baseline, potential benefits could include:

  • finding sequence and tag mismatches before physical equipment trial;
  • reusing signal definitions and failure/recovery test order during expansion;
  • giving controls, operator-interface, communications, and analytics teams a common description of state; and
  • reviewing how a logic change affects other process steps and alarms before commissioning.

Current limitations and next validation

The PoC was not validated on physical Giga Press hardware, a safety PLC, hydraulic or servo dynamics, live takt conditions, or production I/O. It also did not measure DQN performance in a live plant, long-duration communications stability, or operator response to alarms.

Field use would require hardware-in-the-loop testing with actual I/O, FAT, communications-loss and reconnect tests, tag-version changes, alarm priorities, network segmentation, and access review. Even if an AI candidate is used, it needs accept, reject, and rollback paths for its recommendations. Machine commands and safety decisions must remain with validated PLC and safety systems.

Design choices and work flow

This public plan explains why virtual commissioning was needed, what was implemented or reviewed, and how team delivery was broken into traceable tasks and acceptance criteria.

Problem

PLC sequences, SCADA tags, sensor faults, and network delay are difficult to validate together before physical equipment, so defects are discovered late during plant trial.

Goal

Define the Factory I/O–PLC–SCADA–OPC UA responsibilities and data contract first, then review normal, failure, and restart scenarios in a virtual environment.

Success

The team demo and report must trace data flow, ownership, and tests while clearly separating expected benefits from measured plant outcomes.

Scope and boundaries

In scope

  • Signal flow and ownership across Factory I/O, PLC, SCADA, and OPC UA
  • Normal run, missing sensor, communication delay, and restart scenarios
  • Review of a DQN candidate using real-time sensor and PLC data

Out of scope or unverified

  • Production deployment on a real Giga Press safety PLC
  • Presenting Ladder or SCADA work by teammates as my individual work
  • Treating the 50–75% and 50% targets as measured factory results

Implementation and verification

What had to work

  • Shared tags include equipment, signal, value, quality, and timestamp.
  • The PLC owns interlocks and final run permission; AI provides advisory output only.
  • SCADA traces status, alarms, delay, and manual-command results through the same tags.

Quality and operating rules

  • Communication loss moves the process to a safe state instead of holding the last command.
  • Failure and recovery scenarios are repeated in addition to the normal path.
  • Targets, team results, individual contribution, and unverified work remain separate.

Why it was designed this way

  1. Keep control authority in the PLC

    Non-deterministic AI output or network delay must not bypass safety interlocks.

  2. Use OPC UA as the upper-layer boundary

    Semantic tags and quality metadata reduce coupling between control, SCADA, and analytics.

  3. Inject failures in Factory I/O first

    Missing sensors, delay, and mode conflicts are safer and cheaper to repeat virtually.
