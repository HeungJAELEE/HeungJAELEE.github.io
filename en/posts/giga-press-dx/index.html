Factory I/O Virtual Factory Expansion Scenario

A team training project connecting Factory I/O, PLC, SCADA, and OPC UA to review virtual production flow and bottleneck candidates against a target output

Project period
Ten-person AI-convergence training project
Context
Team training PoC · no real-plant deployment
Scope
Communication setup, AI-candidate review, and technical research

User, build, and validation summary

User problem
Meeting a target output required checking cycle time, equipment count, and layout before adding equipment.
Build scope
In a ten-person team, I handled communication setup, technical research for throughput and layout calculations, and AI-candidate review; ladder and SCADA implementation remain team contributions.
Result / current stage
The team connected Factory I/O, PLC, SCADA, and OPC UA with a virtual expansion scenario sized to a target output.

Why the project was done

Adding equipment does not guarantee that a factory will meet a higher output target. Cycle time, bottlenecks, equipment count, travel distance, and layout must be reviewed together. This ten-person training PoC used a hypothetical Giga Press expansion to review virtual production flow and bottleneck candidates before physical investment.

System flow

flowchart LR A["Target output and orders"] --> B["Cycle time by process"] B --> C["Equipment count and layout"] C --> D["Factory I/O\nvirtual process"] D <--> E["PLC\ncontrol sequence"] E --> F["SCADA\nstate view"] E --> G["OPC UA\nupstream data"] G --> H["AI candidate\nfuture analysis"]

Factory I/O represented the layout and process flow, the PLC controlled the sequence, SCADA displayed current state, and OPC UA exposed the PLC state under shared names. AI was reviewed only as a future consumer of production and status history; it was not implemented as a control layer.

My scope and the team scope

I aligned communication so Factory I/O, PLC, SCADA, and OPC UA referred to the same state and researched the information needed to compare equipment count and layout against a target output. Detailed ladder and SCADA implementation belonged to other team members. The process scenario and presentation were team outputs.

I can explain the overall system, but I do not present the ladder and SCADA implementation as individual work.

Implementation order

  1. Define target output and process flow.
  2. Compare process cycle times and bottleneck candidates.
  3. Create equipment-count and layout options.
  4. Build the virtual process in Factory I/O.
  5. Connect PLC, SCADA, and OPC UA and compare their states.
  6. Organize the production and status data that a future AI layer could use.

Verified result

The team connected Factory I/O, PLC, SCADA, and OPC UA in one educational demonstration and produced a virtual expansion scenario sized to a target output. This was not a measured plant-productivity improvement, and fault scenarios such as sensor failure and communication loss were not regression-tested.

Setup-time and expansion-effort reductions in the team report were expected effects for later plant verification, not measured PoC results.

What a plant deployment would still need

A real project would require measured cycle times, material-flow data, safety-PLC integration, vendor protocols, hardware-in-the-loop testing, and explicit fault/recovery acceptance criteria. The next stage would connect the virtual scenario to FAT/SAT documents rather than stopping at a normal communication demonstration.
