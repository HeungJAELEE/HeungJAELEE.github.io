    Official-source Unemployment Benefit Q&A: Turning 165 Questions into Verification Paths

    A personal AI static service connecting 165 de-identified questions to eight categories, official sources, practical guides, and structured data

    Jul 31, 2026 · 6 minutes · Last reviewed Jul 31, 2026

    • Unemployment Benefit
    • Official Sources
    • Static Site
    • Schema.org
    • Privacy

    Problem: similar questions can produce different administrative outcomes

    A short question such as “Can I qualify after resigning?” may depend on separation reason, insured periods, documents, job-search activity, an individual notice, and agency review. A website that decides the case can conflict with current law or the actual disposition.

    The service is therefore a static verification aid, not an eligibility chatbot. It helps a user find:

    • the closest question type;
    • facts and documents to check;
    • the relevant Work24, ministry, or statute source;
    • what to confirm through an individual notice or competent employment center.

    Why 165 questions become eight categories

    Publishing raw conversations would expose personal context and create duplicates. One giant FAQ would make discovery difficult.

    The pipeline de-identifies 165 questions, groups them into eight categories by intent and verification requirement, and connects repeated questions to guides and search terms. The completed snapshot includes nine practical guides and 21 search pages.

    Problem

    Eligibility can depend on separation reason, insured periods, job-search activity, and individual notices, so a short generic answer can conflict with the administrative decision.

    Goal

    Classify de-identified questions and connect them to Work24, ministry, statute sources, practical guides, and the user's official confirmation path.

    Success

    Users can navigate 165 questions across eight categories while official links, structured data, and cache updates reproduce without replacing individual decisions.

    How AI and human roles were divided

    AI role
    Drafted taxonomy, duplicate and search clusters, answer structures, and metadata while assisting static-generation checks.
    Human role
    A human de-identified source conversations and reviewed official sources, legal wording, and sentences requiring individual administrative judgment.
    Publication gate
    Raw conversations, personal notices, and identifying data do not publish. Unsupported or change-sensitive conclusions remain unpublished.
    External effect
    Only static information sites are deployed. The service does not submit claims, decide eligibility, access Work24 accounts, or perform administrative action.

    Scope and boundaries

    In scope

    • 165 de-identified questions, eight categories, and practical guides
    • Work24, Ministry of Employment and Labor, and statute sources
    • Static generation, search pages, Schema.org, and hash-based cache busting

    Out of scope or unverified

    • Determining individual eligibility, payment, or administrative disposition
    • Publishing raw chats, personal notices, contacts, or case details
    • Placing site wording above current statutes or agency guidance

    Implementation and verification

    What had to work

    • Classify each question by type, situation, checks, and official source.
    • Provide conditions requiring individual confirmation and an official next step.
    • Generate pages, search, and structured data from one source.

    Quality and operating rules

    • Separate official source from explanation and mark change risk.
    • Block personal data, raw conversations, and private repository URLs.
    • Put content hashes in asset URLs to invalidate stale CSS and JavaScript.

    Why it was designed this way

    1. Use typed static Q&A instead of free-form chat

      The same source, caveat, and next action can be repeatedly validated and indexed.

    2. Pair evidence with an official action path

      Users should verify personal notices and agency guidance rather than infer eligibility from an article.

    3. Control deployment caches with asset hashes

      Content and behavior must not drift because old CSS or JavaScript remains cached.
