Project at a glance

This education team project connected a three-Indy7 miniature automotive assembly flow with QR product identity, YOLOv11 inspection, PLC/SCADA, MySQL/MES, a digital-twin view, and Raspberry Pi 5/Hailo-10H edge inference.

The team PDF lists ten members and the process demonstration but does not include member-level R&R. Separate portfolio material records my scope as Robot/Digital Twin. Team results are therefore not presented as individual-only achievements.

Architecture

flowchart LR A["QR product ID"] --> B["MySQL / MES history"] B --> C["PLC process sequence"] C --> D["Three Indy7 cobots"] D --> E["Camera"] E --> F["Pi5 + YOLOv11"] F --> G["Hailo-10H acceleration"] G --> C C --> H["SCADA / digital-twin view"] G --> B

The process shown in the report runs from QR serial registration through part input, body/window assembly, vision checks, and good/reject routing. The important data relationship is product ID → process step → robot/equipment → inspection result → event time.

Dataset size, split, repeated-run variance, and long-duration thermal behavior are not fully disclosed. These numbers do not prove universal production accuracy or sustained line-speed performance.

Technical decisions

Keep AI decisions behind PLC checks

The vision result should include request ID, completion state, prediction, confidence, model version, and error state. A PLC confirms that the result belongs to the current product and remains within timeout before transitioning the physical sequence.

Treat edge FPS as one validation dimension

The 5→18 FPS comparison shows acceleration potential. Production readiness also requires conversion-fidelity checks, camera latency, preprocessing/postprocessing time, temperature, restart recovery, and missed-frame handling.

Keep the digital twin observational

A synchronized 3D view can help explain robot and process state, but it does not replace safety circuits, coordinate calibration, or physical validation. Control authority remains in the PLC, robot controller, and safety system.

What is and is not claimed

Public evidence supports the team process flow, QR/MySQL relationship, vision Ladder and communications Ladder, MES direction, two F1 values, and one edge-FPS comparison. It does not support:

  • continuous production deployment or OEE improvement;
  • separate model-performance values not reproduced in the team report’s direct comparison table;
  • a separate speed claim that conflicts with the team report’s comparison table;
  • a completed Omniverse production deployment;
  • individual-only ownership of every team result.

Career relevance and next steps

Robot teaching/overhaul experience informs coordinate and physical-state validation. Battery-line FAT/SAT informs staged checks before automatic operation. MES setup experience informs the product/equipment/model/event data contract.

Next steps are product/Lot-based dataset splits, class confusion matrices, long-duration Hailo benchmarking, PLC/Vision handshake regression tests, correlation IDs across robot/PLC/Vision/MES, and a replayable hardware-free test environment.

This case is valuable because it connects traceability, robot sequence, model evaluation, edge inference, and MES evidence in one manufacturing flow while keeping team roles and evidence limits visible.
