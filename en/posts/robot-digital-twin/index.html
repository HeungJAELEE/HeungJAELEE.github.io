    Collaborative-Robot Assembly Twin and Edge-Vision PoC

    An education team PoC connecting QR traceability, Indy7 robots, vision inspection, PLC/SCADA/MES, and a digital twin

    • Digital Twin

    The problem

    Separate demonstrations of robot motion, vision results, PLC sequences, and MES records do not reconstruct what happened to one product.

    This education team PoC used a QR serial as the common key across an Indy7 assembly flow, edge-vision inspection, PLC and SCADA, MySQL/MES, and digital-twin state.

    Why the flow is structured this way

    flowchart LR A["Register QR serial"] --> B["MySQL / MES\nproduct and process history"] B --> C["PLC sequence"] C --> D["Indy7 assembly and transfer"] D --> E["Camera + edge vision"] E --> F["Inspection result"] F --> C F --> B C --> G["SCADA and Digital Twin\nprocess-state view"]

    Use the QR serial as the shared key

    The minimum history contains product serial, process step, equipment or robot ID, inspection-model version, prediction and score, and event time. These fields must form one work record before a defect can be traced back to the relevant step and equipment state.

    Separate AI judgment from physical control

    A vision result does not directly command robot motion. The PLC checks completion, communications, and interlocks before the next transition. This boundary reduces the chance that a model error or stale message becomes a physical action.

    Keep the twin observational

    The twin aligns robot and inspection state on one timeline. A moving 3D scene does not prove machine safety, so physical authority remains with the PLC, robot controller, and safety circuit.

    My scope

    • Review the collaborative-robot process sequence and state representation
    • Define robot-state fields consumed by the twin
    • Use an offline-to-single-motion-to-process-flow validation order
    • Connect product ID, process step, inspection result, and timestamp
    • Clarify the boundary between vision output and physical sequence control

    The team presentation included model and edge-device comparison figures, but a public visitor cannot reproduce the data split, repeated evaluation, or long-running thermal conditions. Those figures are therefore not used as headline achievements on this page.

    Result

    The team connected product identification, robot steps, inspection results, and MES history in one demonstration flow. My work focused on the Robot and Digital Twin scope: what state should be shared, what the twin may observe, and where it must not cross into control authority.

    My FAT/SAT experience informed the staged validation order: check functions, interlocks, repeatability, and production history separately before treating the connected demonstration as a complete system.

    Problem

    If product ID, robot motion, inspection images, PLC sequence, and MES results remain separate, product-level process history cannot be reconstructed.

    Goal

    Use QR serial as a shared key across three Indy7 robots, YOLOv11 inspection, PLC/SCADA, MySQL/MES, and digital-twin state.

    Success

    The team demo reconstructs product→step→equipment→inspection→time and reports edge-performance evidence without expanding it into a plant claim.

    Scope and boundaries

    In scope

    • QR serial registration and MySQL/MES process history
    • Three-Indy7 input, body/window assembly, and loading sequence
    • YOLOv11 inspection and Pi5 versus Hailo-10H inference comparison
    • PLC, SCADA, and Digital Twin state connection

    Out of scope or unverified

    • Presenting team results as my individual-only implementation
    • Treating the related repository and complete team PDF as one artifact
    • Generalizing an education cell to automotive plant performance

    Implementation and verification

    What had to work

    • QR serial is the common key for process and inspection history.
    • The PLC coordinates robot steps and good/reject routing.
    • SCADA, MES, and twin display the same step and state.

    Quality and operating rules

    • Separate team artifacts, individual role, and related repository.
    • Do not use model or speed figures that public visitors cannot reproduce as headline achievements.
    • Distinguish education demo constraints from plant safety and performance.

    Why it was designed this way

    1. Use QR serial as the end-to-end key

      It allows product-level reconstruction of process, inspection, and robot events.

    2. Compare an edge accelerator

      The Pi5 versus Hailo-10H comparison informs practical placement.

    3. Separate team output and extension repository

      The PDF and PC-HMI repository support different claims.
