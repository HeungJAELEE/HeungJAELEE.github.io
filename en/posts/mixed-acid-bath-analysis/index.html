Mixed-Acid Anodizing Bath Analysis and Replenishment Standardization

Connecting component-level bath analysis with batch-test film results and applying the method in Korea and China

Project period
Jul 2017–Jun 2018
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Problem definition, sampling and repeated analysis, external comparison, product-sample evaluation, Korea/China rollout, and training

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Legacy bath values appeared normal, but etch-rate, particle, and film-quality problems repeated after about three months.
What I owned
I increased sampling to twice weekly and connected component-level analysis, external comparisons, and batch-test sample evaluation for Korea and China.
Verified result
The project standardized the analysis SOP, log sheet, replenishment/replacement decision, and product-sample checks for the target baths and China site.

Project overview

The bath was replenished by reported consumption and its legacy control value remained within range, yet product performance fell as bath age increased. The issue was not only total chemical loss. Individual components were consumed at different rates, while the previous method converted one neutralization-titration result back into the original blend ratio.

Why a normal analysis value could coexist with poor product results

The legacy method measured total acidity once and calculated replenishment using the initial blend ratio. Actual production consumed components at different rates, so the total value could appear acceptable while the composition drifted.

The revised review connected two timelines:

  • bath component results, free/total acid, dissolved aluminum, pH, and bath age;
  • batch test-sample thickness, cracks, thermal and electrical performance, acid/corrosion resistance, roughness, and appearance;
  • customer etch-rate and particle feedback.

Exact component names and composition are outside the public scope.

Actual verification sequence

Compare legacy analysis logs with product-defect history
→ increase sampling from once to twice per week
→ repeat component-level analysis at the same time point
→ compare results with three external institutions
→ build samples using legacy and revised replenishment logic
→ check batch-test film, Hi-Pot, and appearance
→ monitor through the next bath cycle
→ document SOP, log sheet, and replenishment criteria

Repeated measurements and product-sample comparisons were conducted roughly fifty times across development and stabilization. External results were used to check direction and repeatability, not to replace the internally developed method.

Revalidation at the China site

When the Korean method was transferred to China, performance loss reappeared. The liquid could not be transported back, so samples were built locally and the local analysis results were compared with headquarters evaluation. The method was adjusted to local materials and products rather than copied unchanged, and the local analyst was trained in sampling, repeated measurement, and decision order.

Standards left behind

The project produced an analysis SOP, sampling standard, log sheet, repeated-measurement rule, replenishment decision sequence, bath-replacement decision method, monthly product-sample rule, test reports, and analyst training materials.

Longer bath life was technically considered, but a replacement-cycle change was not adopted as the operating standard during my employment because of customer-VOC and commercial concerns. Legacy Korea/China defect-rate figures are also excluded because their denominators and aggregation method are not closed.
