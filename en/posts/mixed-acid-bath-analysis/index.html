Mixed-Acid Anodizing Bath Analysis and Replenishment Redesign

When titration results no longer matched film performance, I separated the bath components, compared them with product samples and external laboratories, and set replenishment and replacement criteria in Korea and China

Project period
Jul 2017–Jun 2018
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Analysis-log and product-defect comparison, sampling and repeats, external correlation, component-level replenishment, Korea/China rollout, and training

Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
Mismatch between a normal titration value and film-performance degradation with bath age
Key role
Twice-weekly sampling, three repeats, three external laboratories, about 50 bath/product-sample comparisons, and China-site revalidation
Key result
Component-analysis, replenishment, product-sample, and bath-replacement criteria; SOP, log, and training handoff

Project overview

Item Description
Period Jul 2017–Jun 2018
Scope Mixed-acid anodizing baths for OLED equipment parts
Initial signal Etch-rate, particle, crack, appearance, and Hi-Pot issues returning after about three months
Direct scope Analysis-log/product-defect comparison, sampling revision, repeated component analysis, external-lab correlation, Korea rollout, China revalidation, and training
Completion Redesigned analysis, replenishment, product-sample, and bath-replacement decision criteria

The legacy titration value remained within its control range while product-film performance declined with bath age. The core issue was a single neutralization value converted by the original blend ratio, which did not detect different component-consumption rates and cumulative composition drift.

1. Root-cause chain

Single neutralization-titration value
→ replenishment calculated from the original blend ratio
→ different component-consumption rates not represented
→ cumulative composition drift with bath age
→ normal analysis value but degraded product-film performance
→ recurring etch-rate, particle, crack, appearance, and Hi-Pot issues

Bath data and product results were connected on the same timeline.

  • Bath: component analysis, free/total acid, dissolved aluminum, pH, and bath age
  • Product: batch-test-sample thickness, cracks, heat, Hi-Pot, acid/corrosion resistance, roughness, and appearance
  • Customer result: etch rate and particles

2. DMAIC-based analysis and replenishment redesign

Phase Core work Decision and output
Define Mismatch between normal control value and product defects Combined analysis and product-performance scope
Measure Sampling once→twice weekly and three repeats at the same time point Component-consumption and variation baseline
Analyze Legacy method, component analysis, three external labs, and product samples Detection-limit root cause
Improve Component-level replenishment, sampling, log, and decision sequence Revised analysis and replenishment logic
Verify Legacy/revised methods in parallel, about 50 analyses, and product-sample comparison Bath-analysis/film-performance correlation
Control Korea rollout, China-site revalidation, and analyst training SOP, log, replenishment, and replacement handoff

3. 4M plus measurement disposition

Axis Legacy issue Decision Change and control
Man Analyst sequence and principle-understanding variation Standardization item Fixed sampling, titration, calculation, and disposition sequence
Machine No independent component-analysis setup in the legacy method Detection-limit cause Internal method and external-lab correlation
Material Raw-material lots, accumulated components, and Korea/China material differences Local validation axis Connected material, bath, and product-sample history
Method Original-ratio replenishment and low sampling frequency Root cause Component-level replenishment and twice-weekly sampling
Measurement Single-value dependence, repeatability, and weak product linkage Core improvement axis Three repeats, lab comparison, and product-sample gate
Environment Site temperature and Korea/China differences Compensation variable Local samples and headquarters cross-check

4. Validation sequence

Compare legacy analysis logs with product-defect history
→ sampling once→twice per week
→ three repeats at the same time point
→ component comparison with three external laboratories
→ samples using legacy and revised replenishment logic
→ film thickness, cracks, heat, Hi-Pot, corrosion, and appearance
→ follow the next bath cycle
→ Korea implementation
→ China-site sample revalidation

External-laboratory results were used to verify the direction and reproducibility of the internal method. Final disposition required both bath-analysis values and product-sample results from the same period.

5. China-site revalidation

The Korean method was not copied unchanged. Local raw materials, baths, and product conditions were remeasured.

  • Condition-based samples produced at the China site
  • Local analysis cross-checked with headquarters film evaluation
  • Raw-material-lot, site-temperature, and product effects separated
  • Training on sampling, three repeats, and disposition sequence
  • Local log and replenishment criteria updated

6. Risk-item revision and standard-document cascade

7. Results

  • Shifted bath control from a single titration value to component analysis and product-sample disposition
  • Established sampling once→twice weekly and three repeats per time point
  • Completed correlation with three external laboratories
  • Secured bath-analysis/film-performance correlation through about 50 analysis and sample comparisons
  • Completed Korea rollout, China-site revalidation, and analyst training
  • Established the analysis SOP, log sheet, replenishment criteria, replacement decision, and product-sample rule
