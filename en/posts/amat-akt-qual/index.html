    TECHNICAL CASE STUDY

    Global Equipment-Maker Technical & Quality Audit Approval

    A YMC qualification case that reconfigured etching, bead blasting, oxalic-acid anodizing, and inspection through Six Sigma Green Belt-based DMAIC and 4M, then closed one Hi-Pot audit finding through seven repeated readings and three retests

    Aug 1, 2026 · 4 minutes · Last reviewed Aug 10, 2026

    • Six Sigma GB
    • DMAIC
    • 4M
    • Technical Qualification

    Project overview

    ItemDescription
    PeriodDec 2017–Dec 2018
    PartsSusceptor- and diffuser-family components for a global CVD/PECVD equipment company
    Process scopeEtching→bead blasting→oxalic-acid anodizing→material, Hi-Pot, and particle inspection
    Direct scopeEnglish specification and CTQ analysis, process-change design, Process Flow/Run Sheet/qualification documents, counterpart samples, measurement, audit response, and retest closure
    CompletionOne audit finding closed, technical qualification approved, and about one year of follow-on supply

    1. Legacy-process gaps

    Process or controlLegacy stateGap against customer requirement
    Etching and pretreatmentWork sequence and inspection points managed separatelyMismatch with English specification and submission items
    Bead blastingCommon conditions and fragmented evaluation recordsWeak linkage to surface roughness and particle requirements
    Oxalic-acid anodizingInternal film settingsWeak linkage to thickness, Hi-Pot, acid, and corrosion CTQs
    Material and particle checksIndividual test recordsIncomplete process-flow and sample-history linkage
    Hi-PotEquipment, location, repeat, ramp, and sequence differencesInternal/customer disposition mismatch
    Change documentsIndependent revisions by process, inspection, and submissionDocument–field–sample inconsistency

    2. Six Sigma Green Belt-based DMAIC

    PhaseCore workDecision and output
    DefineSupplier registration, part qualification, process capability, and CTQsQualification scope and audit items
    MeasureRun sheets, equipment capability, samples, and measurement baselineLegacy-to-customer gap list
    AnalyzeEtching, bead blasting, anodizing, and Hi-Pot through 4MProcess changes separated from alignment items
    ImproveProduct-family process conditions and inspection specificationsProcess Flow, Run Sheet, and inspection criteria
    VerifyCustomer location, seven repeated readings, aligned sequence, and three retestsOne technical-audit finding closed
    ControlSynchronized process, inspection, sample, and document revisionsTechnical qualification and follow-on production basis

    3. Process-level changes and 4M

    Analysis targetSuspected causeFindingAction
    Etching and pretreatmentMan·Method·Measurement — Work sequence, inspection timing, and sample history separatedThickness, roughness, appearance, and downstream comparisonProcess Flow and Run Sheet sequence realignment
    Bead blastingMachine·Method·Measurement — Product-family surface requirements not representedRoughness, appearance, and particle comparisonConditions, evaluation items, and anodizing-entry basis reconfigured
    Oxalic-acid anodizingMaterial·Machine·Method — Internal setting and customer CTQs misalignedThickness, Hi-Pot, acid, and corrosion checksProduct-family film conditions and inspection specification redesigned
    Hi-PotMachine·Method·Measurement — Equipment, location, repeat, ramp, and arc-reading differencesThree retests on counterpart samplesCustomer location, seven readings, ramp, sequence, and arc basis aligned
    Acid, corrosion, and particlesMethod·Measurement — Internal results weakly linked to customer capabilityInternal, external, and customer-result comparisonTest conditions, inspection specification, and external correlation
    Change controlMan·Method — Process, inspection, and submission revisions misalignedAudit check of document/field consistencyProcess–inspection–sample–document concurrent revision

    4. Hi-Pot measurement correlation

    Counterpart samples from the same bath and manufacturing condition
→ customer-designated measurement locations
→ seven repeated readings per sample
→ internal/customer equipment comparison
→ ramp time and test-sequence alignment
→ Arc Counter disposition alignment
→ revised counterpart samples
→ three retests under one decision basis
→ closure of one technical-audit finding

    Equipment, location, repeat, and sequence differences were removed before changing the process recipe. The three retests then established correlation between internal inspection and customer disposition.

    5. Risk-item revision and standard-document cascade

    Risk item added or revisedPreventive and detection controlStandard document added or revised
    Process sequence–customer specification mismatchProcess Flow and process control itemsProcess Flow and Run Sheet
    Mixed surface and film conditions by product familySeparate conditions and inspection itemsProcess condition sheet and inspection criteria
    Hi-Pot correlation failureAligned location, seven readings, ramp, sequence, and arc basisMeasurement sequence and result report
    Disconnected external and internal analysisSample ID, test condition, and result linkageQualification documents and sample result sheet
    Untracked audit actionFinding–action–retest–closure chainAudit corrective-action report
    Document revision mismatchConcurrent process, inspection, and sample revisionSOP, QCP, and change-control sheet

    6. Results

    • Converted English specifications and CTQs into Process Flow and Run Sheets
    • Reconfigured etching, bead blasting, oxalic-acid anodizing, and inspection conditions
    • Aligned customer locations, seven repeated readings, test sequence, and arc disposition
    • Closed one technical-audit finding through three counterpart-sample retests
    • Obtained technical qualification from the global equipment company
    • Connected approval to about one year of follow-on supply
    • Handed off Process Flow, Run Sheets, English qualification documents, inspection criteria, and corrective-action reports
