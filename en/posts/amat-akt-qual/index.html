Technical Qualification of CVD and PECVD Components for a Global Display-Equipment Maker

Aligned English specifications, process/run sheets, and Hi-Pot measurement conditions to close one audit finding and support about one year of follow-on supply

Project period
Dec 2017–Dec 2018
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
English specifications, process flow, run sheets and qualification package, counterpart samples and measurement, external analysis, customer audit, and corrective-retest closure

Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
New-supplier registration required proof of CVD/PECVD component process capability, while matched-condition counterpart samples received different internal and customer Hi-Pot decisions.
Key role
I converted English specifications into process/run sheets and inspection criteria and led sample production, direct measurement, external analysis, the technical audit, and retest closure.
Key result
I aligned measurement location, seven repeats, voltage ramp, test sequence, and arc interpretation, then closed one finding through three retests and connected qualification to about one year of follow-on supply.

Project background and objective

This project qualified YMC as a supplier of susceptor- and diffuser-type components for a global display-equipment maker’s CVD and PECVD platforms. The customer reviewed more than a passing surface-treatment sample: etching, bead blasting, anodizing, inspection methods, equipment capability, change control, and production readiness were assessed together.

At YMC’s Technical R&D Center, I reviewed the customer’s English specifications and submission requirements and led the Process Flow, Run Sheet, English qualification package, and sample-result reports. I also connected sample production, direct measurement of film thickness, roughness, Hi-Pot, acid/corrosion resistance and particles, external analysis, the customer technical audit, and corrective retesting.

The objective was to align customer requirements with the actual process and inspection basis, resolve the Hi-Pot decision gap between YMC and the customer, close the audit finding, and secure technical qualification.

1. Convert English specifications into Process and Run Sheets

The customer specifications were not treated as translation work. They were converted into actual process controls, inspection criteria, and evidence requirements.

  • Listed customer requirements and mandatory submissions
  • Structured the etching, bead-blasting, and anodizing sequence and control items
  • Led the Process Flow and Run Sheet
  • Linked film thickness, roughness, Hi-Pot, acid/corrosion resistance, and particle checks
  • Separated in-house equipment capability from external-analysis requirements
  • Defined change-control and sample-result reporting formats

Qualification documents lose credibility when the written process differs from field execution. I therefore reviewed the work sequence and measurement method with production and inspection personnel before finalizing the English package. My manager reviewed the final material, and I incorporated the comments back into the process and sample evidence.

2. Build counterpart samples and demonstrate manufacturing capability

Susceptor- and diffuser-type counterpart samples were produced separately under the same bath and manufacturing conditions. The purpose was to confirm whether YMC’s process and inspection system could reproduce the customer’s required condition.

Process and property checks

  • Produced samples by etching, bead-blasting, and anodizing condition
  • Checked film thickness, roughness, and appearance
  • Repeated Hi-Pot measurements
  • Evaluated acid and corrosion resistance
  • Reviewed particle-control results
  • Compared external-analysis results with in-house findings where needed

The sample evidence was linked to the Process Flow, Run Sheet, and equipment capability instead of being submitted as isolated test certificates. The focus was not merely whether one sample passed, but whether the same outcome could be reproduced through a defined process and measurement basis.

3. Analyze Hi-Pot variation and close the audit finding

During the technical audit, YMC and the customer reached different withstand-voltage decisions. Because the counterpart samples came from the same bath and manufacturing conditions, the measurement system and test sequence were compared before the coating recipe was changed.

Measurement-condition alignment

  • Applied customer-specified measurement locations
  • Performed seven repeated measurements per sample
  • Compared internal and customer measurement equipment
  • Aligned voltage ramp and test sequence
  • Aligned Arc Counter interpretation
  • Remeasured corrective samples under the same decision principle

After the measurement location, repeat count, equipment, and test sequence were aligned to the customer basis, the corrective samples completed three retests. The analysis and retest report closed the single technical-audit finding concerning Hi-Pot results.

This separated a process defect from a measurement-condition difference. Rather than introducing new variation by changing the recipe before the measurement basis was aligned, the process capability was reassessed under one customer decision principle.

4. Respond to the technical audit and obtain qualification

The technical audit covered the following areas.

  • Available equipment and measurement principles
  • Manufacturing process and change control
  • Etching, bead-blasting, and anodizing Process and Run Sheets
  • Susceptor and diffuser counterpart-sample results
  • Film thickness, roughness, Hi-Pot, acid/corrosion resistance, and particle evaluation
  • Owners, corrective actions, retests, and closure criteria for findings

I explained the English qualification package and sample results and compiled the root-cause analysis, corrective action, and retest outcome into the final report. After audit closure, technical qualification approval was communicated through the sales organization, and the approved components moved into about one year of follow-on supply.

Final results and standardization

  • Converted customer English specifications into actual Process Flow, Run Sheets, and inspection criteria
  • Demonstrated manufacturing and inspection capability for CVD/PECVD susceptor- and diffuser-type components
  • Aligned Hi-Pot measurement locations, seven repeats, and test sequence on matched-condition counterpart samples
  • Closed one technical-audit finding through three corrective retests
  • Obtained technical qualification from the global equipment maker
  • Connected the approval to about one year of follow-on supply

Deliverables left behind

  • Process Flow and Run Sheets
  • English qualification package and sample-result reports
  • Process and inspection capability material
  • Hi-Pot measurement and decision sequence
  • Audit-finding corrective-action and retest report
  • Change-control and follow-on sample-response criteria

The project established a repeatable technical-response process that connected customer requirements with actual manufacturing, sample evidence, measurement correlation, corrective retesting, and qualification closure.
