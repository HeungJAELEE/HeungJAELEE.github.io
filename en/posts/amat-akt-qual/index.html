CVD and PECVD Component Process Reconfiguration and Technical Qualification

Used a Six Sigma Green Belt-based DMAIC sequence and process-level 4M plus measurement to reconfigure bead blasting, oxalic anodizing, and Hi-Pot conditions and close one audit finding

Project period
Dec 2017–Dec 2018
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
English specifications, process-level 4M plus measurement, bead-blasting/oxalic-anodizing/inspection reconfiguration, counterpart samples, technical audit, and retest closure

Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
Internal process and inspection conditions did not match the global equipment maker's requirements, and matched-condition counterpart samples received different Hi-Pot decisions.
Key role
I converted English specifications into process-level changes, reconfigured bead-blasting, oxalic-anodizing, and inspection conditions, and led Hi-Pot correlation and corrective audit retests.
Key result
I aligned customer locations, seven repeats, voltage ramp, test sequence, and arc interpretation, closed one finding through three retests, and connected qualification to about one year of follow-on supply.

Project overview

  • Period: Dec 2017–Dec 2018
  • Parts: Susceptor- and diffuser-type components for a global display-equipment maker’s CVD and PECVD platforms
  • Process scope: Etching → bead blasting → oxalic-acid anodizing → property, Hi-Pot, and particle inspection
  • Direct scope: English specification analysis, process-change definition, Process Flow and Run Sheet authorship, counterpart-sample production and measurement, external analysis, technical audit, and corrective-retest closure
  • Result: One audit finding closed, technical qualification obtained, and approved components supplied for about one year

What was wrong with the existing process

YMC already had etching, bead-blasting, anodizing, and inspection capability, but the process conditions, Run Sheets, and inspection criteria were not organized as one product-family-specific flow against the global equipment maker’s requirements.

  • The actual sequence from etching through anodizing was not mapped one-to-one to the customer’s English specification and submission items.
  • Bead-blasting and oxalic-anodizing conditions were managed separately from product-family surface and film requirements.
  • Film thickness, roughness, acid/corrosion resistance, and particle results were not consistently linked to Process Flow and sample history.
  • Counterpart samples from the same bath and process received different internal and customer Hi-Pot decisions because equipment, measurement location, repeat count, voltage ramp, test sequence, and arc interpretation differed.

Changing the process recipe alone could not separate a true film defect from a measurement-system difference. Customer requirements therefore had to be translated into CTQs and process-level changes, followed by separate reconfiguration of manufacturing and inspection conditions and retesting under one basis.

1. Reconfigure etching and bead blasting

For etching, the main work was to reconnect the actual pretreatment sequence and control items with customer specifications and sample evidence.

  • Repositioned the etching sequence and control points in the Process Flow and Run Sheet
  • Linked post-treatment surface condition to roughness and appearance
  • Fixed sample history and inspection points before and after bead blasting
  • Aligned terminology and order between customer submissions and actual production records

Bead blasting was reconfigured around product-family surface requirements.

  • Separated bead-blasting conditions by product family
  • Linked roughness, appearance, and particles to the process result
  • Compared the result with downstream anodized-film properties
  • Transferred the selected condition to the Run Sheet and inspection criteria

2. Change oxalic-anodizing conditions and inspection specifications

Rather than retaining the internal setting unchanged, anodizing conditions were reconfigured for the CVD/PECVD component family’s film requirements.

  • Reorganized product-family oxalic-anodizing conditions and recipes
  • Linked film thickness with surface-condition control
  • Updated Hi-Pot, acid-resistance, and corrosion-resistance test conditions and inspection specifications
  • Produced counterpart samples by condition and measured them internally
  • Correlated selected items with external analysis

A condition was selected only when film thickness, Hi-Pot, acid/corrosion resistance, and particle results met the customer requirement together.

3. Correlate the Hi-Pot measurement system

The central audit finding was that matched-condition counterpart samples received different internal and customer withstand-voltage decisions.

Existing measurement differences

  • Internal versus customer equipment
  • Customer-specified versus internal measurement locations
  • Repeat count per sample
  • Ramp time and test sequence
  • Arc Counter interpretation

Change and retest

  • Unified measurement points to the customer-specified locations
  • Applied seven repeated readings per sample
  • Aligned voltage ramp and test sequence
  • Unified the Arc Counter decision principle
  • Produced corrective counterpart samples from the same bath and process condition
  • Completed three retests under one decision basis

This sequence separated true process defects from measurement-condition differences. The root-cause, corrective-action, and retest report closed one technical-audit finding.

4. Close the technical audit and establish control

The audit compared documents and field execution across the following areas.

  • Available equipment and measurement principles
  • Etching, bead-blasting, and anodizing Process and Run Sheets
  • Change control and sample traceability
  • Film thickness, roughness, Hi-Pot, acid/corrosion resistance, and particles
  • External-analysis correlation
  • Finding owner, action, retest, and closure criteria

Standardized outputs

  • Process Flow and Run Sheets
  • Product-family bead-blasting and oxalic-anodizing conditions
  • SOP, QCP, and inspection criteria
  • Hi-Pot measurement and decision sequence
  • English qualification package and sample-result reports
  • Audit-finding corrective-action and retest report
  • Change-control and follow-on sample-response criteria

Final results

  • Separated internal-process versus customer-requirement gaps through process-level 4M plus measurement
  • Reconfigured bead-blasting and oxalic-anodizing conditions by product family
  • Updated Hi-Pot, acid, and corrosion test conditions and inspection specifications
  • Aligned customer locations, seven repeats, voltage ramp, sequence, and arc interpretation
  • Closed one technical-audit finding through three corrective retests
  • Obtained technical qualification from the global equipment maker
  • Connected approved components to about one year of follow-on supply
