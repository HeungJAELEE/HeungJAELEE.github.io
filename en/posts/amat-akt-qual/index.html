Manufacturing case study

Field application

Technical Qualification of CVD and PECVD Components

Aligning same-sample measurement results before documenting approved process and inspection criteria

Project period
Qualification work during YMC employment
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Process and inspection specification rebuild, measurement correlation, sample validation, and technical audit support

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Stable internal results were insufficient when customer measurement methods and acceptance criteria differed.
What I owned
I rebuilt surface-treatment and inspection criteria, including Hi-Pot, acid resistance, and corrosion resistance, and supported same-sample correlation and technical audits.
Verified result
Same-sample measurement correlation was established, and the technical qualification conditions were added to QCPs, run sheets, and inspection standards.

Qualification flow

A part that passes internal inspection may still fail customer acceptance when equipment, measurement location, or decision criteria differ. I therefore did not change the process recipe first. I used the same samples to separate measurement differences from actual process differences.

01

Qualification · Correlation

CVD and PECVD parts qualification

Process and measurement causes were separated before rebuilding the customer-facing verification system.

Problem and acceptance criteria

  • The same sample produced different film-thickness and electrical results internally and at the customer.
  • Surface treatment, Hi-Pot, acid resistance, and corrosion resistance had to be connected to customer criteria.
  • Process and inspection had to remain reproducible in production.

Measurement correlation

  • Repeated film thickness, roughness, withstand voltage, Arc Count, and Ramp Time on the same samples.
  • Aligned measurement location, repetition count, and sample pretreatment.
  • Separated instrument bias and repeatability from actual part differences.
  • Fixed the order so measurement-system variation was not mistaken for a recipe problem.

Rebuilding process and inspection criteria

  • Translated the customer specification into process conditions and inspection items.
  • Connected Bead Blast and Oxalic Anodizing conditions with film thickness, roughness, color, and withstand voltage.
  • Organized Hi-Pot, acid and corrosion resistance, particle, and dimensional requirements.
  • Left unconfirmed items as follow-up sample tests rather than declaring them passed.

Technical audit and retest

  • Split audit actions into process, inspection, and documentation.
  • Confirmed owners and actions, then retested revised conditions with samples.
  • Recorded the reason, sample, position, and decision criterion for comparison with earlier results.

Technical outcome

  • Rebuilt surface-treatment, Hi-Pot, acid-resistance, and corrosion-resistance criteria for CVD and PECVD parts.
  • Converted confirmed conditions into recipes, run sheets, process standards, and inspection criteria.
  • Connected process conditions and measurement decisions into one qualification system.

Technical and business outcomes were kept separate

CategoryConfirmed outcomeInterpretation
TechnicalQualification criteria were established through samples, audit actions, and retests.Manufacturing and quality validation completed
BusinessThe company ended the relationship about a year later because of the high-complexity part mix and profitability.Not presented as commercialization success

Principles retained

  • Do not change recipes before understanding measurement-system differences.
  • Use the same sample to separate part variation from instrument and decision variation.
  • Translate customer specifications into linked process, inspection, and decision criteria.
  • Report technical qualification separately from business and profitability decisions.
