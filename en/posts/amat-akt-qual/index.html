Technical Qualification of CVD and PECVD Components for a Global Display-Equipment Maker

New-supplier qualification closing Hi-Pot differences and a technical-audit finding with matched-condition counterpart samples

Project period
Dec 2017–Dec 2018
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
English specifications, process and run sheets, samples and measurement, technical audit, and retest closure

Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
Counterpart samples from the same bath and process still produced different Hi-Pot decisions when equipment, repeats, and test sequence differed.
Key role
I led the English specification, process and run-sheet work, sample production and measurement, external analysis, technical audit, and corrective retest closure.
Key result
One audit finding was closed through aligned measurement conditions and retesting, followed by qualification notice and about one year of supply.

The work was broader than producing a passing surface-treatment sample. The customer reviewed the process, equipment, inspection method, change control, and production capability together. I translated the English requirements into process conditions, inspection methods, submission documents, and sample evidence.

Scope I owned

I reviewed customer requirements, mapped them to process and inspection capability, led the English process flow, run sheet, qualification material, and sample report, produced and measured samples, coordinated external analysis, attended selected customer meetings, and handled the technical audit and corrective retest.

Counterpart samples—not the same physical sample

The earlier page described a same-sample correlation. The actual structure used different counterpart samples produced from the same bath and manufacturing conditions, not one physical sample measured by both sides.

Build counterpart samples from the same bath and process condition
→ measure seven times at customer-specified locations
→ compare internal/customer equipment and voltage-test sequence
→ align Arc Counter interpretation
→ apply the customer decision method
→ complete three corrective retests
→ close the technical-audit finding

The recipe was not changed first because equipment, repeat count, and test-sequence differences could be mistaken for film failure.

Technical-audit scope

The audit covered equipment lists and principles, manufacturing and change-control processes, etching, bead blasting and anodizing process/run sheets, susceptor and diffuser sample results, thickness, roughness, Hi-Pot, acid/corrosion resistance, particle control, and corrective-action closure.

One audit finding concerned the difference between internal and customer withstand-voltage results. Equipment, repeat count, test sequence, and arc-reading conditions were aligned and the item was closed through retesting.

Qualification was communicated through the sales organization and confirmed through actual supply for about one year. The later reduction was related to lower-than-expected volume and profitability, not a technical failure.

Contracting was a company sales result and is not presented as my individual achievement. Customer specifications, exact Arc Counter settings, equipment models, and approval documents are outside the public scope.
