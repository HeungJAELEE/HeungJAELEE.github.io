The three-minute case

Why it mattered

Anodizing, etching, and cleaning results depend on both bath condition and the measurement method used to judge the sample. Stable internal readings were not enough if the customer instrument produced a different result on the same sample. The process condition and measurement baseline had to be aligned together.

My scope

I reviewed bath analysis and sampling rules and supported sample validation for anodizing, etching, and cleaning. I compared the same samples across internal and customer instruments, confirmed the correlation, and recorded conditions confirmed through customer qualification in recipes, run sheets, and inspection standards.

Workflow

Bath analysis → sample validation → internal/customer cross-instrument correlation → customer qualification confirmation → recipe, run sheet, and inspection-standard updates

Key decisions and verification

  • Bath condition, sample preparation, and measurement method were reviewed separately instead of being changed together.
  • The same samples were used across instruments so sample differences did not distort the correlation check.
  • Only conditions confirmed within customer qualification were transferred into standards, with detailed conditions controlled in the recipe and run sheet.

Result and standardization

Sample validation and cross-instrument correlation established the conditions confirmed through customer qualification. Recipes, run sheets, and inspection standards were updated together so process settings and measurement decisions did not drift apart.

Transferability

A surface-treatment standard cannot stop at bath analysis. Cross-instrument comparison on the same sample and the decision method confirmed in customer qualification must connect back to the process documents.

Limits

Company analysis records and customer specifications remain confidential. Actual bath formulations, recipe values, and product identifiers are anonymized, and a condition confirmed on one sample is not generalized to every material or geometry.

Problem

Cleaning and surface-treatment outcomes change with part condition, ultrasonic energy, bath chemistry, temperature, and analytical method, so one recipe cannot guarantee repeatability or customer correlation.

Goal

Decompose ultrasonic cleaning, anodizing, bath analysis, and measurement methods into controlled variables and connect them to a qualification-ready process window.

Success

Fix the new process and analytical method in standards and explain internal-versus-customer measurements within an agreed correlation boundary.

Scope and boundaries

In scope

  • Anodizing, etching, bead-blast, and cleaning conditions
  • Bath analysis and film-thickness, roughness, dielectric, particle, and dimensional criteria
  • Repeated same-sample measurements and internal/customer instrument correlation

Out of scope or unverified

  • Customer identity, formulations, detailed recipes, and internal specifications
  • Applying one condition set to every material and geometry
  • Claiming public raw analytical data exists

Implementation and verification

What had to work

  • Separate contamination, coating state, energy, chemistry, temperature, and time.
  • Compare before, after, and failure samples with one sampling and analytical rule.
  • Connect the accepted process window to standards and customer-response criteria.

Quality and operating rules

  • Do not directly compare values from incompatible instruments, preparation, or limits of detection.
  • Record boundary conditions and failed samples with the accepted recipe window.
  • Chemical, temperature, ultrasonic-safety, and confidentiality controls override publication.

Why it was designed this way

  1. Control a process window, not one recipe point

    A permitted range for chemistry, temperature, and time is needed to absorb field variation.

  2. Treat analytical method as part of the process contract

    Preparation and instrument settings can change the value even for the same sample.

  3. Keep customer correlation as a separate gate

    Internal acceptance does not guarantee agreement with customer equipment and methods.

Work completed and planned

Customer, bath, and recipe details are removed; only the development and validation method is public.
