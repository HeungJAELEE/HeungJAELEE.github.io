Different outcomes within one career period

At YMC’s Technical R&D Center, I worked on anodizing, etching, cleaning, dry bead blasting, bath analysis, measurement correlation, and customer qualification. The previous article combined these into one case and obscured the differences among process development, technical qualification, and lessons from failure.

The work is now divided around three questions.

Case 1 — How was a production condition created and maintained?

High-temperature anodizing film development covers a crack and particle issue under repeated high-temperature use. Conditions were evaluated from small to near-full-size samples, while bath-analysis and inspection criteria were developed alongside the process.

Case 2 — How were different customer and internal measurement systems aligned?

AMAT/AKT CVD and PECVD component qualification explains process-condition and same-sample correlation work. The technical qualification and the company’s later business-continuation decision are treated as separate outcomes.

Case 3 — What validation sequence came from a failed scale-up?

Diffuser ultrasonic-stripping scale-up failure explains why open lab risks should have stopped equipment fabrication. It turns sample quality, reusability, repeatability, and stop criteria into pre-fabrication gates.

Shared work pattern

The outcomes differ, but the cases share one pattern: separate possible causes with 4M and close each next-stage condition with DMAIC.

LensWhat the three cases reviewed
ManSampling and judgement differences, interpretation of customer requirements, and ownership across teams
MachineRectifiers, jigs, bath conditions, internal/customer instruments, and scale-up repeatability
MaterialBase material, surface, film, bath chemistry, sample condition, and reusability
MethodPretreatment, recipe, inspection order, same-sample comparison, scale-up tests, and stop gates
  1. Define — Set the issue, customer requirement, and CTQ that determines completion.
  2. Measure — Separate process and measurement questions and record differences on the same samples.
  3. Analyze — Use 4M to distinguish actual process variation from measurement and sample differences.
  4. Improve — Change one condition at a time and increase scale and use conditions in stages.
  5. Control — Recheck under customer or field use and update recipes, run sheets, inspection criteria, and stop gates.

Together, the three cases show how I moved from a process symptom to measurement alignment, staged validation, customer review, and shop-floor standards.
