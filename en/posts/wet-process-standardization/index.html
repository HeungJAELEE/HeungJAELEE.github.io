Work performed at YMC

My YMC work ranged from surface-treatment development and bath-control methods to customer qualification, a stopped scale-up, and standard work for a new robot business. The common approach was to align samples, measurement methods, and process history before changing conditions, then narrow causes, retest, and document the accepted method.

I had earned a Six Sigma Green Belt in my final university year and used that problem-solving sequence in practice. These were not formally chartered Six Sigma projects with tollgates. The DMAIC framing describes the actual order of problem definition, measurement, 4M/5 Why/Pareto review, comparison testing, retesting, and standardization.

Documents and handoff

The work produced run sheets, test reports, change reports, process and inspection standards, analysis SOPs, sampling standards, equipment manuals, checklists, parts lists, robot overhaul manuals, English qualification documents, and training materials.

Publication boundary

The public pages contain the problem, decision order, direct scope, sanitized test structure, and standardization result. Bath composition, recipes, customer specifications, approval documents, original run sheets, and internal equipment settings are excluded. Legacy defect-rate and bath-life figures are not presented as achievements because their denominators and actual adoption status are not closed.
