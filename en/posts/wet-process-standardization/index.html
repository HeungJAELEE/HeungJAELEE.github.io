    Semiconductor and Display-Part Surface Treatment and Measurement Correlation

    A case that aligned anodizing, etching, cleaning, dry bead blasting, bath analysis, measurement correlation, and customer qualification into process and inspection standards

    Jul 31, 2026 · 5 minutes · Last reviewed Jul 31, 2026

    • Surface Treatment
    • Anodizing
    • Correlation
    • Qual
    • Run Sheet

    Problem and context

    Surface-treatment results change with material, geometry, contamination, and process conditions even when a nominal recipe is unchanged. My scope included chemical anodizing, etching, and cleaning as well as dry mechanical bead blasting. Because those processes work differently, I managed their conditions and inspection methods separately instead of grouping all of them as wet processing. Even stable internal measurements can fail qualification when the customer’s instrument and rule differ.

    At YMC’s R&D center, I worked on anodizing, etching, bead blasting, and cleaning for semiconductor/display process parts and aligned process conditions with inspection methods.

    What I owned

    • Checked bath concentration and dissolved-component analysis, sampling, and repeat-measurement rules.
    • Organized film thickness, roughness, color, dielectric strength, particle, and dimensional checks around product and customer needs.
    • Repeated measurements on the same samples across internal and customer instruments to check bias and correlation.
    • Supported sample validation and customer technical audits, then applied accepted conditions to recipes, run sheets, process standards, and inspection criteria.
    • Separated process conditions, sample preparation, and measurement methods when investigating an abnormal result.

    What I checked and changed

    Surface treatmentChemistry, concentration, temperature, time, bead media/spray conditions, cleaningProcess-specific recipe and control range
    Surface/analysisThickness, roughness, color, dielectric strength, particles, compositionInstrument, sample, and repeat rules

    The important point was to align the measurement method as well as the process condition.

    Result and limits

    • Documented the process and inspection baseline needed for AMAT(AKT) CVD/Etch part change qualification and production use.
    • Checked correlation between different instruments and recorded the accepted method in field documents.
    • Kept process and inspection rules together so post-change results could be compared on one basis.

    Detailed customer specifications, part identifiers, chemical formulations, recipe values, and raw analysis results remain private. A condition confirmed for one part is not generalized to every material or geometry.

    Problem

    Cleaning and surface-treatment outcomes change with part condition, ultrasonic energy, bath chemistry, temperature, and analytical method, so one recipe cannot guarantee repeatability or customer correlation.

    Goal

    Decompose ultrasonic cleaning, anodizing, bath analysis, and measurement methods into controlled variables and connect them to a qualification-ready process window.

    Success

    Fix the new process and analytical method in standards and explain internal-versus-customer measurements within an agreed correlation boundary.

    Scope and boundaries

    In scope

    • Anodizing, etching, bead-blast, and cleaning conditions
    • Bath analysis and film-thickness, roughness, dielectric, particle, and dimensional criteria
    • Repeated same-sample measurements and internal/customer instrument correlation

    Out of scope or unverified

    • Customer identity, formulations, detailed recipes, and internal specifications
    • Applying one condition set to every material and geometry
    • Claiming public raw analytical data exists

    Implementation and verification

    What had to work

    • Separate contamination, coating state, energy, chemistry, temperature, and time.
    • Compare before, after, and failure samples with one sampling and analytical rule.
    • Connect the accepted process window to standards and customer-response criteria.

    Quality and operating rules

    • Do not directly compare values from incompatible instruments, preparation, or limits of detection.
    • Record boundary conditions and failed samples with the accepted recipe window.
    • Chemical, temperature, ultrasonic-safety, and confidentiality controls override publication.

    Why it was designed this way

    1. Control a process window, not one recipe point

      A permitted range for chemistry, temperature, and time is needed to absorb field variation.

    2. Treat analytical method as part of the process contract

      Preparation and instrument settings can change the value even for the same sample.

    3. Keep customer correlation as a separate gate

      Internal acceptance does not guarantee agreement with customer equipment and methods.
