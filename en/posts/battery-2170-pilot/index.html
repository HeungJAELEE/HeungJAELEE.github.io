2170 Assembly-Line Good-Yield Stabilization

Separated displayed yield, inspection misclassification, and process defects through 4M+2E retests and field standards

Project period
Core stabilization May–Dec 2024 · follow-up Jan–Feb 2025
Context
Completed while working in Kumyang's Battery Technology Team
Scope
Assembly trial direction, owners, deadlines, retests, closure criteria, and control-document revision

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Displayed yield, actual good yield, and observed defects did not agree, requiring aligned definitions and measurement reliability.
What I owned
I set the assembly validation sequence, cross-team retests, and closure criteria, then narrowed causes through MSA, vision, jigs, consumables, and recipes.
Verified result
The team recorded 98.7% monthly average good yield in December 2024, and I directly revised the MSA criteria, SOP, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection standards.
Good yield About 40% → 98.7%

Representative result
98.7% monthly average good yield in Dec 2024 · team result

The equipment-reported yield, the good yield confirmed by full inspection, and defects observed on the floor did not agree. Before changing process settings, total input, good cells, sample-inspection loss, process defects, and measurement reliability were aligned under one basis.

I set the assembly-process validation sequence and coordinated retests across production, quality, equipment, and development. An item was closed only after the result repeated under the same criteria, then the confirmed condition was transferred into the MSA criteria, SOP, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection standard.

Baseline and loss structure

Total input was classified into good cells, sample-inspection loss, and process defects for yield evaluation.

The 98.7% December 2024 monthly average was the team result calculated as total good cells ÷ total input cells. After fixing that basis, inspection misclassification, equipment and jigs, consumables, and process settings were validated in sequence.

4M+2E was not used as a generic checklist. Each process had a local outcome, and position, lot, equipment condition, and measurement evidence were compared to remove or retain candidates.

Representative improvement cases

The project-level 4M+2E table is the common cause map for the line. Each case below applies a separate local 4M+2E analysis to its process outcome and records the evidence used to retain or remove each candidate.

01

Measurement · Method

Vision false rejection and inspection-margin reset

The product specification stayed unchanged while direction, alignment, and inspection conditions were corrected.

Symptom

Normal cells were repeatedly rejected in one rotation direction. Actual thickness remained within the design range, but the direction-specific alignment and inspection margin did not cover normal product variation.

4M+2E cause analysis

Cause axisProblem or candidate checkedDecision evidenceDisposition
ManDirection-specific setting and work-sequence variationCompared whether the same good cell changed decision by operator settingContributing control factor
MachineDirection-dependent vision alignment positionFalse rejection repeated in one direction with a consistent alignment differenceRetained candidate
MaterialElectrode press-thickness variationConfirmed actual thickness remained within the design marginRemoved as primary cause
MethodGood-part and reject ranges narrower than actual variationDirectional rechecks failed in the inspection region despite meeting product specificationPrimary cause
MeasurementReference good samples and repeated directional measurementSame-sample reclassification separated product defects from false decisionsRoot-cause evidence
EnvironmentLighting and surrounding conditionsLess explanatory than direction, alignment, and inspection margin within the checked scopeNot adopted as a primary cause

Improvement and retest

  • Compared direction-specific alignment, work sequence, and thickness on the same samples
  • Removed electrode thickness as the primary cause after confirming the design range
  • Reset the inspection area and good-part decision range
  • Standardized direction-specific settings and recipe-management order

Result and standardization

  • Vision false rejection: about 30% → 0%
  • Setting sequence and maintenance checks transferred into the inspection standard
02

Machine · Method

Filling loss: sealing structure and recipe separation

Jig, sealing, and pressure structure were validated separately from filling recipes.

Symptom

A roughly 2,000-cell batch showed leakage and underweight cells together. Initial filling-related loss affected about 150–220 cells per batch and repeated by position.

4M+2E cause analysis

Cause axisProblem or candidate checkedDecision evidenceDisposition
ManCell loading, jig seating, and alignment variationCompared position-specific loss with work sequenceNot confirmed as a single cause
MachineO-ring contact, springs, jig alignment, sealing and pressure structure, hopper and pumpMatched repeated leakage and underfill positions with equipment conditionRetained as primary candidates
MaterialO-rings, sealing parts, and filling-related material or lot changesSeparated part and sealing characteristics from equipment effectsCase-specific influence checked
MethodFill position and vacuum, pressure, hold, and repeat sequenceRetested recipes separately before and after mechanical correctionsRetained adjustment axis
MeasurementSame-sample weight, leakage, fill quantity, and downstream inspectionConnected each cell’s results under one decision basisRoot-cause evidence
EnvironmentAmbient and cleaning conditionsLess explanatory than sealing structure and recipe within the checked scopeNot adopted as a primary cause

Improvement and retest

  • Separated position-specific loss, leakage, fill quantity, and weight variation
  • Checked O-ring contact, springs, jig alignment, hopper, and pump first
  • Retested only the required pressure, vacuum, and impregnation settings after mechanical actions
  • Used the same samples and leakage/weight criteria

Result and standardization

  • Initial loss basis: about 7.5–11.0% per batch
  • Confirmed conditions reduced impregnation turnaround time by about 18%
  • Mechanical checks and recipe-change order transferred into work standards
03

Machine · Measurement

Resistance-weld integrity and formation-IR stabilization

Head alignment, pressure, electrodes, consumables, and inspection were stabilized before recipe changes.

Symptom

Teardown and process history of formation-IR failures identified missing resistance welds as a major cause. Head alignment, pressure, electrode wear, replacement practice, and welding settings were separated.

4M+2E cause analysis

Cause axisProblem or candidate checkedDecision evidenceDisposition

MachineHead alignment, pressure, electrode wear, and repeat motionMatched teardown of missing welds with equipment conditionRetained as primary candidates
MaterialTab, electrode, and joint material or structural changesSeparated material effects from equipment and setting actionsCase-specific influence checked
MethodCurrent, time, pressure, and inspection sequenceCompared one setting at a time after restoring equipment and electrodesFollow-on adjustment axis
MeasurementPQC, nugget size, tensile force, fracture mode, and formation IRConnected direct joint checks with downstream electrical resultsRetest and closure evidence
EnvironmentSurrounding work conditionsLess explanatory than alignment, electrodes, and welding settings within the checked scopeNot adopted as a primary cause

Improvement and retest

  • Corrected head alignment, pressure, and electrode condition first
  • Checked PQC, nugget size, fracture mode, and tensile force
  • Changed one process condition at a time and rechecked downstream formation IR
  • Standardized electrode replacement cycles and checks

04

Machine · Measurement · Control

Shape and downstream decision stabilization

Product geometry was separated from gauge and inspection variation, then head, knife, cleaning, and gauge-maintenance criteria were standardized.

Symptom

Beading geometry, final cell height, and downstream appearance contained both real product defects and measurement or inspection variation. Head and knife settings, gauge contact, and cleaning condition were rechecked under one basis.

4M+2E cause analysis

MaterialCan, cap, and semi-finished geometry or material changesSeparated them from equipment and measurement effects within the checked scopeNot adopted as a primary cause
MethodCommon good range across four heads, DMC dispensing and cleaning, inspection intervalCompared common conditions and maintenance practice rather than a one-head temporary correctionPrimary control conditions
MeasurementTotal-height MSA, in-process/EOL cross-check, vision, appearance, leakage, and drop checksSeparated actual shape defects from gauge and inspection variationRoot-cause and closure evidence
EnvironmentCleaning and ambient conditionsLess explanatory than heads, knives, and the measurement system within the checked scopeNot adopted as a primary cause

Improvement and retest

  • Set a common good range for beading knife and head position
  • Cross-checked unit-process inspection and EOL total-height measurement
  • Operated cleaning checklists and scheduled 3-jaw gauge checks
  • Updated QCP and inspection criteria for geometry and appearance
