Field application

2170 Assembly-Line Yield Stabilization

A production improvement that separated measurement misclassification from process defects before controlled retests and standardization

Project period
Core stabilization May–Dec 2024 · follow-up Jan–Feb 2025
Context
Completed while working in Kumyang's Battery Technology Team
Scope
Aligned production, quality, equipment, and development teams around yield, defect, downtime, and retest evidence

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Displayed yield, actual good yield, and shop-floor defects did not agree, so measurement and decision reliability had to be checked before process settings.
What I owned
I led assembly-process trials and cross-team coordination, narrowing causes from measurement and sensors through inspection, jigs, consumables, and finally recipes.
Verified result
The team recorded 98.7% monthly average good yield in December 2024, and I directly revised the MSA criteria, SOP, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection standards.
Good yield About 40% → 98.7%

How the full project progressed

Displayed yield, actual good yield, and shop-floor defects did not agree. The initial level of about 40% was based on fully good cells divided by assembly input and was reconfirmed through retrospective inspection. I aligned the denominator, defect names, and measurement and decision system before changing process conditions.

  1. 2024.05–06Align the baselineAligned the denominator, defect names, and measurement and inspection criteria.
  2. 2024.07–08Separate causesReviewed measurement and sensors, inspection and Vision, then jigs, alignment, and consumables.
  3. 2024.09–10Run process trialsValidated Vision, resistance welding, filling, and downstream controls separately.
  4. 2024.11–12Retest and standardizeRechecked with the same criteria and revised shop-floor documents.
  5. 2025.01–02Confirm sustainmentChecked whether revised conditions remained in use.

More than ten batches were fully inspected, and Gage R&R and maintenance MSA criteria were used to verify the measurement system. The review order was measurement and sensors → inspection and Vision → jigs, alignment, and consumables → recipe.

01

Measurement-system-focused DMAIC

Vision false rejection

The product specification stayed unchanged; the way Vision interpreted good parts was corrected.

Problem

  • Good cells were repeatedly rejected at a particular rotation direction after Can Insert.
  • A narrow recognition margin created Type I false rejects.
  • Product geometry and inspection-setting causes had to be separated.

4M candidate map

4MCandidate reviewedEvidence and decision
ManRotation and setting practiceChecked whether operators understood orientation-specific alignment and setting.
MachineVision alignment by rotationConfirmed that alignment shifted slightly as the product rotated.
MaterialElectrode press-thickness variationVariation remained inside the design margin; removed as the primary cause.
MethodGood range and NG screeningThe narrow range did not account for actual thickness variation; retained as the key cause.

Action and retest

  • Realigned Vision by rotation direction.
  • Widened the inspection margin for actual electrode-thickness variation.
  • Rebuilt the inspection area, good range, and NG screening conditions.
  • Controlled inner- and outer-tab settings and rechecked decisions by orientation.

Result and control

  • The product inspection specification was not relaxed.
  • Confirmed Vision settings became controlled parameters.
  • Actual product defects and inspection false rejects could be separated.
02

Focused DMAIC · Sequential cause elimination

Resistance welding and Formation IR

Alignment, head, electrode, and inspection criteria were checked before the recipe.

Problem

  • Incomplete resistance welds were one major contributor to Formation IR defects.
  • Equipment state, electrode control, inspection criteria, and welding settings could affect the same outcome.

4M candidate map

4MCandidate reviewedEvidence and decision
ManElectrode replacement and setupChecked whether operator practice affected electrode condition and decisions.
MachineHead alignment and pressureStabilized alignment and pressure before process trials.
MaterialTab structure and material changeKept the Clad Tab internal-resistance effect separate from process improvement.
MethodInspection and welding settingsVerified decision capability, then changed one setting at a time.

Action and retest

  • Stabilized alignment and electrode-management controls.
  • Changed welding conditions one factor at a time.
  • Retested through PQC, nugget size and fracture, tensile strength, and Formation IR.

Result and control

  • Reduced resistance-welding loss from about 4% to roughly 0.3%.
  • Left a repeatable check sequence covering alignment, electrodes, inspection, and settings.
  • Detailed recipe values and internal limits are not published.
03

Branching Root Cause Validation

Filling, leakage, and weight variation

Mechanical sealing structure and recipe effects were verified in separate stages.

Problem

  • Filling-related loss reached roughly 150–220 cells in batches of about 2,000.
  • Leakage and underweight cells appeared together.
  • Underweight cells also occurred without leakage, so one cause could not explain both.

4M candidate map

4MCandidate reviewedEvidence and decision
ManJig loading, alignment, and work sequenceCompared position-based loss patterns with operating practice.
MachineSealing, alignment, clamping, hopper, and pumpChecked O-ring contact, spring state, hopper, and pump separately.
MaterialO-ring and filling-material changesManaged changes separately from mechanical effects.
MethodPosition, pressure, vacuum, and impregnation sequenceVerified recipe effects after mechanical sealing actions.

Action and retest

  • Did not mix structural actions and recipe changes in the same test.
  • Addressed jig alignment and mechanical sealing first.
  • Retested position, pressure, vacuum, and impregnation conditions step by step.
  • Rechecked leakage, fill quantity, and position variation separately.

Result and control

  • Separated mechanical and process-setting causes.
  • Managed structural improvement and recipe stabilization as different validation stages.
  • Left leakage, fill quantity, and position variation as separate controls.
04

Quality Acceptance Gate

Downstream rust and appearance decisions

Appearance marks and functional risk were separated through limit-sample gates.

Problem

  • Discoloration, weld marks, and scratches could not all be treated as one defect.
  • Rust and environmental conditions also had to be considered.
  • The line needed a clear accept and reject boundary.

4M candidate map

4MCandidate reviewedEvidence and decision
ManAppearance and limit-sample judgmentAligned operator decisions through limit samples.
MachineWelding, washing, and HVAC conditionsSeparated over-weld marks, cleaning, and HVAC bypass effects.
MaterialTubing color and surface damageDistinguished cosmetic difference from functional abnormality.
MethodpH, wash cycle, and scratch judgmentKept unfinished trials open and blocked only confirmed functional or VOC risk.

Gate and result

  • Appearance marks without rust or functional impact were reviewed as acceptable.
  • Scratches with ingress or customer-VOC risk were excluded.
  • Confirmed wash-water temperature, brush-replacement, and HVAC controls were reflected in field criteria.
  • The unfinished pH water-replacement-cycle trial was excluded from completed results.

As a cross-functional team result, the line stabilized from about 40% to a 98.7% monthly average good yield in December 2024. The formula was total good cells ÷ total input cells. My direct scope covered assembly-process trials, cross-team coordination, action schedules, and revision of existing-product standards.

Items excluded from completed results
  • The CPF Pre-Charge PCB history did not have enough evidence to close product causality.
  • The pH water-replacement-cycle test remained open.
  • The 20,000-shot electrode-life target was stopped before achievement.
