Manufacturing project

Field application

2170 Assembly-Line Good-Yield Stabilization

Realigned yield and measurement criteria, then standardized production conditions through local 4M+2E and same-cell retests

Project period
Core stabilization May–Dec 2024 · follow-up Jan–Feb 2025
Context
Completed while working in Kumyang's Battery Technology Team
Scope
Yield and measurement alignment, assembly validation direction, cross-team owners, deadlines, retests, decision criteria, and control-document revision

Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
Equipment-reported yield and full-inspection good yield did not agree, so true process defects and inspection misclassification had to be separated under one basis.
Key role
I aligned the yield denominator and measurement criteria, then managed owners, deadlines, local 4M+2E retests, and decision criteria on the same cells.
Key result
I contributed to the team result of stabilizing monthly average good yield from about 40% to 98.7% and directly revised the MSA criteria, SOP, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection standards.
Good yield About 40% → 98.7%

Initial production condition

The equipment-reported yield did not match the good yield confirmed by full inspection. Repeated shop-floor defects were mixed into the same number, so changing recipes first would have blurred inspection misclassification and actual process defects.

Total input, good cells, sample-inspection loss, and process defects were aligned under one basis. Measurement and decision reliability came first; vision, jigs and alignment, consumables, and process settings were then separated through local 4M+2E analysis and retested on the same cells under the same criteria.

I set the assembly validation direction and the owners, deadlines, retest methods, and decision criteria across production, quality, equipment, and development. Confirmed conditions were transferred into the MSA criteria, SOP, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection standards.

Total input was classified into good cells, sample-inspection loss, and process defects for yield evaluation.

The 98.7% December 2024 monthly average was the team result calculated as total good cells ÷ total input cells. Causes were narrowed in the order of measurement and inspection, jigs and alignment, consumables, and process settings.

Case 01. Vision rejection of good cells

01

Measurement · Method

Direction-specific vision decision reset

Product specifications stayed unchanged while alignment, inspection region, and setting order were corrected.

Symptom and success criterion

Good cells were repeatedly rejected in one rotation direction. When actual thickness stayed within the design range, the material condition remained unchanged. Success meant that the same good cell received the same decision across directions and operators.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Repeated directional inspection of reference good cells
  • Actual thickness versus design margin
  • Vision alignment and inspection-region comparison
  • Operator setting sequence

Local 4M+2E decision

ManDirection-specific setting orderSame good cell retested by operator and directionRecurrence-control factorStandardize the direction-specific setting order
MachineDirection-dependent vision alignmentRejection direction and alignment difference repeatedRetained candidateReset alignment by rotation direction
MaterialElectrode press thicknessActual thickness within the design rangeRemoved as primary causeKeep the press condition unchanged
MethodGood range and inspection regionIn-spec cells failed in the inspection regionPrimary causeReset the good range and inspection region
MeasurementReference good-cell repeatsDirectional decisions on the same cellDecision evidenceRetest the same cell by direction
EnvironmentLighting and surroundingsLess explanatory than direction and inspection settingsRemoved as primary causeRetain existing lighting and ambient controls

Action and retest

Press settings were not changed. Direction-specific alignment and inspection regions were reset, then the same good cells were repeatedly checked for stable good-part decisions.

Standardization

Direction-specific setting order, recipe management, and maintenance checks were transferred into the inspection standard.

Case 02. Filling leakage and underweight cells

02

Machine · Method

Sealing structure and filling-condition separation

Jigs, sealing, and pressure structure were checked before the required filling settings were changed.

Symptom and success criterion

Leakage, underfilling, and underweight cells appeared together by batch and repeated by position. The accepted condition required no leakage, fill weight within the same criterion, and downstream inspection pass.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Position-specific leakage, fill amount, and weight
  • Same-cell filling and downstream inspection linkage
  • O-ring, spring, jig alignment, and sealing condition
  • Hopper, pump, vacuum, and pressure sequence

Jig alignment, sealing, and pressure structure were corrected first. Pressure, vacuum, and hold conditions were then changed one at a time and rechecked on the same cells.

Standardization

The mechanical check order, sealing-part checks, and sequence for changing filling conditions were transferred into SOP, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection standards.

Case 03. Resistance-weld miss and formation IR

03

Machine · Measurement

Joint integrity linked to downstream IR

Head, pressure, and electrode condition were restored before welding settings were compared.

Symptom and success criterion

Teardown of formation-IR failures showed missing resistance welds. Rather than changing welding settings alone, head alignment, pressure, electrode condition, and joint inspection were aligned first. Nugget, tensile force, fracture mode, and formation IR had to meet their criteria together.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Failure teardown and process-history comparison
  • Head alignment, pressure, and electrode wear
  • PQC, nugget size, tensile force, and fracture mode
  • Formation IR on cells made under the same setting

05

Shape defects separated from measurement variation

Symptom and success criterion

CBC showed disagreements among total height, inner diameter, Hi-Pot, and vision decisions. CCR separately showed biting defects, height variation, and 3-jaw wear. The work separated true geometry defects from gauge and inspection variation, then required matching in-process and EOL decisions.

Reproduction and measurement

Case 06. CXI X-ray false decision

Actual winding geometry separated from count and range

Symptom and success criterion

When both electrodes rose in a cone-shaped profile, the inspection produced missing measurements and false decisions. Actual winding defects or active-material position errors still had to be rejected, while geometry accepted as good by quality review had to remain measurable.

Reproduction and measurement

Case 07. Rust, discoloration, appearance disposition, and environmental control

07

Measurement · Method · Environment

Separate true corrosion from acceptable appearance traces

Limit samples and functional disposition were fixed first, followed by separate checks of wash-water, pH, and airflow conditions.

Symptom and success criterion

When rust, discoloration, weld marks, tubing color difference, and scratches were grouped under one appearance defect, true corrosion could not be separated from traces without a functional impact. The success criterion was a repeatable limit-sample decision that distinguished rust from acceptable appearance while keeping washing and airflow conditions controlled.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared rust location and appearance with limit samples
  • Separated actual rust from discoloration and weld marks on over-welded samples
  • Checked appearance by wash-water pH and replacement interval
  • Checked operating condition after the HVAC bypass adjustment
  • Reviewed tubing color differences and scratches separately against function and VOC status

Local 4M+2E decision

AxisCandidateEvidenceDecisionAction
ManOperator differences in rust, discoloration, and scratch dispositionSame cells and limit samples reviewed across operatorsStandardization targetTrain appearance disposition with limit samples
MachineWashing equipment, HVAC bypass, and weld conditionChecked washing-equipment and HVAC operating conditionsOperating-condition checkInspect washing, HVAC, and weld conditions
MaterialCan surface, tubing color, and weld-area appearanceFunctional and corrosion results separated from appearance differenceCase-specific influenceSeparate rust, discoloration, color difference, and scratch disposition
MethodWash-water pH, replacement interval, cleaning method, and limit-sample useAppearance and reinspection compared by conditionPrimary control axisOperate pH, water replacement, cleaning, and limit-sample criteria
MeasurementLimit samples, visual/function checks, and VOC dispositionSame appearance separated into rust or acceptable traceFinal decision basisLink visual, functional, and VOC checks to production disposition
EnvironmentTemperature, humidity, airflow, and washing environmentTest conditions did not support one single environmental causeRetained as controls, not a single root causeMonitor HVAC and cleaning conditions separately from process causes

Action and retest

Rust limit samples and acceptance criteria were established for production disposition. Over-welded surfaces without actual rust were separated from functional defects and managed as discoloration or weld marks. The pH influence period was compared with the water-replacement interval, and the HVAC bypass was adjusted to keep airflow more consistent.

Standardization

Limit samples, appearance-inspection sequence, pH and replacement checks, and HVAC checks were transferred into inspection standards and field controls. Scratches associated with customer VOC were managed separately.

Additional downstream management cases

The downstream work did not change every condition at once. Each process separates conditions adopted through testing, conditions retained, and items deferred because follow-on production was unavailable.

Case 08. CWS cleaning conditions and brush control

08

Machine · Method · Environment

Separate adopted wash-water and brush controls from retained settings

The wash-water temperature and brush controls were changed, while nozzle angle, pH, and cleaning time were retained when their change was not supported.

Symptom and success criterion

The cleaning process had several possible adjustments: wash-water temperature, nozzle angle, brush use, pH, and cleaning time. Changing all of them together would make it difficult to determine which condition affected cleaning and appearance. The criterion was to compare them separately, change only supported items, and retain the remaining conditions.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared post-cleaning condition by wash-water temperature
  • Checked results before and after changing nozzle angle
  • Reviewed brush addition and replacement interval
  • Compared pH and cleaning time with the existing conditions
  • Checked post-cleaning appearance against the existing inspection basis

Local 4M+2E decision

AxisCandidateEvidenceDecisionAction
ManCleaning sequence and brush checksTesting did not support operator difference as the primary causeNot selected as a primary causeKeep one cleaning and brush-check sequence
MachineNozzle angle and brush conditionNozzle-angle change was not adopted; brush use and replacement required controlBrush-control itemAdd the brush and set a regular replacement interval; retain nozzle angle
MaterialWash water and cleaned surfaceThe supported direction came from temperature and brush controls rather than material changeNot selected as a primary causeRetain the existing water and product-material basis
MethodWater temperature, pH, cleaning time, and brush useSeparate comparisons selected temperature and brush control as change itemsPrimary control axisChange the water-temperature standard; retain pH and cleaning time
MeasurementPost-cleaning appearance and inspection resultBefore/after conditions were compared under the same inspection basisDecision evidenceRecheck the changed condition under the same criteria
EnvironmentSurrounding cleaning conditionsLess explanatory than the individual cleaning conditions and not confirmed as a single causeRetain existing controlsMonitor surrounding conditions separately

Action and retest

The wash-water temperature standard was changed, a brush was added, and a regular brush-replacement interval was set. The nozzle-angle change was not adopted after testing, while pH and cleaning time remained at their existing settings.

Standardization

The wash-water temperature, brush use and replacement interval, and retained settings were transferred into field controls. Nozzle angle, pH, and cleaning time were also recorded as conditions not to be adjusted without new evidence.

Case 09. CMK marking-nozzle clogging and deferred QR function

09

Machine · Method

Prevent nozzle-clog recurrence and defer the unvalidated QR function

The cleaning interval was standardized to control nozzle clogging, while the manual QR function was not treated as complete after follow-on production stopped.

Symptom and success criterion

Recurring marking-nozzle clogging could destabilize marking and work flow. A manual QR-code marking function was also considered, but there was no follow-on production in which to validate it repeatedly. The scope was separated: close nozzle clogging through maintenance control and defer QR functionality until production validation became possible.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Checked clogging before and after nozzle cleaning
  • Compared cleaning interval with marking work condition
  • Reviewed the required scope and target use of manual QR marking
  • Confirmed whether follow-on production and repeated validation were available

4M decision

AxisCandidateEvidenceDecisionAction
ManCleaning sequenceA consistent cleaning interval and execution point were requiredRecurrence-control factorStandardize the work sequence and check timing

MaterialMarking target and consumablesNo evidence identified a specific material change as the core causeNot selected as a primary causeRetain the existing material controls
MethodNozzle-cleaning interval and manual QR workCleaning controlled clogging; QR lacked follow-on production validationCleaning adopted; QR deferredStandardize cleaning and transfer QR to a future production gate

Action and retest

A nozzle-cleaning interval and one execution sequence were established, then clog recurrence was checked. The manual QR-code marking direction was reviewed, but actual repeated production validation was unavailable after follow-on production stopped.

Result and standardization

The nozzle-cleaning interval was transferred into marking maintenance controls. The manual QR function was retained as a future production check rather than presented as an implemented or production-applied result.

Case 10. CPF pre-charge intermittent no-operation and mismeasurement

10

Machine · Measurement · Environment

Separate board, equipment-setting, and product candidates

Intermittent no-operation and mismeasurement were separated into board, equipment-setting, and product candidates; delayed board failure remained a hypothesis requiring evidence.

Symptom and success criterion

The pre-charge equipment required review for intermittent no-operation and possible mismeasurement. Because the original equipment had a history of rain exposure, delayed abnormalities around PCB resistors or capacitors were considered, but long-run evidence and a component failure rate were unavailable. The priority was to separate board, equipment-setting, and product candidates before changing product conditions.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared the intermittent symptom with actual equipment motion
  • Checked board status separately from equipment settings
  • Separated product abnormalities from equipment mismeasurement
  • Reviewed whether past moisture exposure could be associated with the current symptom
  • Checked the availability of long-run and component-failure evidence

Local 4M+2E decision

AxisCandidateEvidenceDecisionAction
ManReset, operation, and check sequenceWork method alone did not explain the intermittent conditionNot selected as a primary causeRetain one operation and check sequence
MachinePCB board, resistors, capacitors, and equipment motionIntermittent no-operation and delayed abnormality required separate checksPrimary investigation candidateInspect the board separately from general equipment motion
MaterialInput-cell conditionProduct abnormality had to be separated from equipment mismeasurementSeparate comparison itemCompare product inspection with equipment disposition
MethodPre-charge settings and reset procedureBoard and setting problems could not be combined into one causeComparison itemSeparate setting, reset, and board-check sequence
MeasurementNo-operation, mismeasurement, and product resultEquipment output had to be correlated with the actual product resultDecision evidenceCompare equipment disposition and downstream result on the same cell
EnvironmentPast rain and moisture exposureExposure history existed, but evidence did not confirm delayed failure as the root causeRetained hypothesisKeep exposure history as inspection information, not a confirmed cause

Action and retest

The inspection order was structured to separate board, equipment-setting, and product candidates. Rather than changing product conditions prematurely, equipment motion and board status were checked first, followed by correlation between equipment disposition and downstream results on the same cell.

Result and management decision

Delayed board failure remained a follow-up hypothesis rather than a confirmed root cause. Without a component failure rate or long-run result, it was not used as a quantitative improvement result; the deliverable was a management basis for separating intermittent no-operation and mismeasurement candidates.

Case 11. CTB alternative-tube limited manufacturability validation

11

Material · Method · Measurement

Compare two alternative tubes, shrink conditions, and appearance

Two tubes and their shrink conditions were compared to select a temperature direction and appearance decision; repeat-production validation remained a later gate.

Symptom and success criterion

The two alternative tubes differed in material characteristics, shrink conditions, and visible color. The decision could not rely on color alone; shrink condition and function had to be separated from appearance. Additional cell production was unavailable, so the scope did not extend to full-process or repeated-production validation.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared the characteristics and appearance of two alternative tubes
  • Checked application condition by shrink-temperature setting
  • Separated color difference from functional problems
  • Reviewed the acceptable appearance range with quality
  • Confirmed the availability of follow-on cells and full-process validation

Local 4M+2E decision

AxisCandidateEvidenceDecisionAction
ManTube loading and shrinking sequenceOperator difference was not supported as the core change axisNot selected as a primary causeRetain one work sequence
MachineTubing and shrinking equipment conditionTube and temperature differences were the main comparison rather than an equipment faultRetain existing equipmentHold equipment constant while comparing tube and temperature
MaterialMaterial characteristics and color of two tubesTube-specific shrink and appearance differences were observedPrimary change axisRecord application characteristics and color difference by tube
MethodShrink-temperature conditionComparison by condition supported an application directionPrimary adjustment axisSelect the temperature application direction
MeasurementShrink condition, appearance, and functionQuality disposition separated color difference from functional problemsLimited-validation evidenceUse the quality-approved appearance range
EnvironmentAmbient and storage conditionsNo separate abnormality was identified within the checked scopeNot selected as a primary causeRetain the existing storage and work environment

Action and retest

The two tubes and shrink conditions were compared, a temperature application direction was selected, and quality accepted the color difference separately from functional concerns. The checked scope established tube-specific application and appearance decisions.

Result and next gate

The stage remained limited manufacturability validation of alternative tubes. Because no additional cells were produced, full-process and repeated-production performance were not confirmed, and the work was not presented as completed dual sourcing or production application. A future production run would need to recheck the complete assembly flow, repeated shrink performance, and long-term appearance.
