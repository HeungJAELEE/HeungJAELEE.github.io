Manufacturing case study

Field application

2170 Assembly-Line Yield Stabilization

A production improvement that separated measurement misclassification from process defects before controlled retests and standardization

Project period
Core stabilization May–Dec 2024 · follow-up Jan–Feb 2025
Context
Completed while working in Kumyang's Battery Technology Team
Scope
Aligned production, quality, equipment, and development teams around yield, defect, downtime, and retest evidence

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Displayed yield, actual good yield, and shop-floor defects did not agree, so measurement and decision reliability had to be checked before process settings.
What I owned
I led assembly-process trials and cross-team coordination, narrowing causes from measurement and sensors through inspection, jigs, consumables, and finally recipes.
Verified result
The team recorded 98.7% monthly average good yield in December 2024, and I directly revised the MSA criteria, SOP, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection standards.
Good yield About 40% → 98.7%

Overall project flow

Displayed equipment yield and the actual good yield confirmed by full post-inspection did not initially agree. Before changing process conditions, the yield formula, defect names, and measurement and inspection criteria were aligned, then each assembly-process cause was retested.

Timeline

  1. May–Jun 2024Align the baselineAligned the difference between displayed yield and actual good yield plus calculation and inspection criteria.
  2. Jul–Aug 2024Separate causesNarrowed candidates from measurement and inspection through jigs, alignment, consumables, and recipes.
  3. Sep–Oct 2024Retest by processValidated vision, resistance welding, and filling issues with separate conditions.
  4. Nov–Dec 2024StandardizeRechecked results under the same criteria and revised existing-product documents.

01

4M cause narrowing

Vision false rejects

The inspection interpretation was corrected without relaxing the product specification.

Problem

Cells without a process abnormality were repeatedly rejected in a particular rotation direction.

4M review

4MCandidate checkedDecision
ManVariation in alignment and setting execution by directionThe work sequence was aligned.
MachineVision alignment position by rotation directionDirection-dependent position differences were confirmed.
MaterialElectrode-thickness variationIt remained within the design margin and was removed as the primary cause.
MethodAlignment and setting sequence by directionDirection-specific standards were documented.

Measurement, action, and result

  • The inspection area and good-part range were rebuilt to reflect actual thickness variation.
  • The product specification itself was not changed.
  • Vision settings and inspection criteria were documented so actual abnormalities and false rejects could be separated.
02

4M cause narrowing

Resistance welding and formation IR

Alignment, electrodes, consumables, and inspection criteria were checked before changing recipes.

Problem

Missing resistance welds appeared as one major cause of formation-IR failure.

4M review

4MCandidate checkedDecision
ManElectrode replacement, maintenance, and setting variationMaintenance practices were standardized.
MachineHead alignment and pressureAlignment and pressure were stabilized first.
MaterialTab structure and material changeMaterial effects were separated from process-condition improvement.
MethodWelding-condition change and retest orderThe trial sequence changed one item at a time.

Measurement, action, and result

  • PQC, nugget size, fracture mode, tensile force, and formation IR were used to verify changes.
  • Resistance-welding-related loss was reduced from about 4% to about 0.3%.
  • Detailed recipes and internal control values are not public.
03

4M cause narrowing

Filling, leakage, and weight variation

Mechanical sealing and recipe effects were tested in separate stages.

Problem

Filling-related loss affected roughly 150–220 cells in a batch of about 2,000, with leakage and underweight cells appearing together.

4M review

4MCandidate checkedDecision
ManJig loading, alignment, and work orderPosition-specific loss was compared with work methods.
MachineSealing, alignment, pressure, hopper, and pumpO-ring contact, spring, hopper, and pump effects were checked separately.
MaterialO-ring and filling-related material changesMaterial effects were managed separately from equipment effects.
MethodPosition, pressure, vacuum, and impregnation sequenceRecipes were retested after mechanical actions.

Measurement, action, and result

  • Position-specific loss, leakage, fill quantity, and weight variation were recorded separately.
  • Mechanical and recipe changes were not mixed in the same trial.
  • Confirmed conditions were reflected in work and inspection documents.

Team result and my role

  • The team stabilized monthly average good yield from about 40% initially to about 98.7% in December 2024.
  • The formula was total good cells divided by total input cells.
  • My direct role covered assembly-process trials, cross-team coordination, and control-document management for the existing product.
  • I checked MSA, jigs, recipes, consumables, and inspection conditions.
