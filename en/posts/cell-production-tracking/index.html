Background

During my time at JESTECH, the NCM pilot line recorded electrode, assembly, inspection, and formation data in separate MES records. The difficulty came after the cathode, anode, separator, and two J/Rs were combined into one cell. There was no single view showing how those records were related. Material combinations could vary within the same lot, and electrode rework made production date alone an unreliable tracing key.

The process lead time from electrode production through assembly and formation was about three weeks. When a customer requested the history of specific cell serial numbers, records from surrounding production dates had to be reconciled manually. A typical investigation of ten cells took about three days.

Approach

A request to add cell-level lookup to MES was rejected because of cost. I therefore defined the relationships among existing process records in a separate tool.

The lookup key was the cell S/N. From one serial number, the tool followed the cathode, anode, separator, tape, can, cap assembly, and electrolyte, then connected them to electrode-roll position, assembly and inspection results, and formation capacity.

Implementation

  • Power Query: Consolidated MES and Excel files from each process and normalized their column structures.
  • Cell S/N relationship table: Defined the links among materials, J/Rs, and process records.
  • Excel VBA: Built the search button and linking logic so one S/N could be traced continuously from electrode roll through formation.

I built the tool over about three weeks outside normal working hours. The repeated manual-search sequence used on the floor was reviewed step by step and translated into code and a lookup form.

Result

After deployment, a single-cell history took about five to ten seconds to retrieve. A full ten-cell investigation fell from about three days to about three minutes. Treating three days as 72 hours, this represents about a 99.9% reduction in elapsed time.

Interpreting the results and preparing the customer report remained a separate task and took about half a day. Search time and report-writing time were managed as different types of work.

Use case: tracing a capacity anomaly

When a production cell shows an abnormal result, its materials, process conditions, and inspection history have to be reconstructed. Applying the tool to cells with abnormal capacity showed that several of them had passed through the same section of an electrode roll.

That evidence narrowed the field check to the electrode process, where pump micro-vibration had loosened a fastening point and changed coating quantity. The lookup did not make the engineering decision by itself; it reduced manual record matching so the team could focus on the relevant roll section and equipment.

Operation and limitations

The tool was used for production-history investigations during my employment. I handed over the search procedure and file-update method so the responsible operator could continue using the same workflow.

Because the tool relies on Excel and Power Query, its linking rules must be updated when source column names or file formats change. When an original process record is missing, that record has to be completed before the lookup is rerun.

The production context and line-acceptance work behind this tool are described in the NCM prismatic pilot-line acceptance and stabilization case.
