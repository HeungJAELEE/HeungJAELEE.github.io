Project background and objective

The NCM pilot line already had MES exports and Excel records for electrode, assembly, inspection, and formation processes. The missing function was a continuous cell-level path across those records.

A customer-selected cell required roughly one week of electrode records and two weeks of assembly and formation records to be reviewed together. Searching, matching, and organizing the history of ten cells took about three days.

I designed and built a field-ready automation tool that used one cell S/N to trace the production history from electrode roll through formation without changing the existing process records.

2. Relationship-key mapping

Link stageLookup keyLinked result
CellCell S/N and assembly timeTarget and assembly record

The cell S/N was the lookup starting key. The assembly record supplied the J/R and material-lot keys, followed by electrode roll and production interval and then assembly, inspection, and formation records.

When rework or material changes created multiple records, process time and work type kept original and follow-up histories separate.

VBA application — batch search and result-table generation

The user entered one or ten cell S/Ns and pressed the search button. VBA followed the Power Query relationship tables and rendered the full process history.

FunctionImplementation
InputOne or ten cell S/Ns
SearchExtract J/R and material keys from the assembly record
Back-traceConnect electrode roll, lot, production interval, and rework
DownstreamContinue through assembly, inspection, and formation
Result tableDisplay material, process, inspection, and capacity history by cell
Integrity checkCompare automatic output with source S/N, lot, and process time

Automatic output was cross-checked against the process source records. The result table was used to narrow common roll, lot, and process intervals before field diagnosis.

Field use case — trace capacity-anomaly cells

Situation

Several cells showed capacity shortfall, while individual process screens did not reveal the common manufacturing segment.

Trace

The abnormal S/Ns were searched together. Within about three minutes, the tool linked assembly, inspection, formation, and electrode-roll history and identified a shared coating segment on the same electrode roll.

Field confirmation

Focused equipment inspection confirmed insufficient coating associated with pump micro-vibration and a loosened fastener as the root cause. The investigation shifted from manually reopening three weeks of files to inspecting the roll and process interval selected by the trace tool.

Batch abnormal-cell S/N input
→ shared J/R and electrode-roll segment
→ focused coating-equipment inspection
→ pump micro-vibration and loose fastener
→ insufficient-coating segment confirmed
