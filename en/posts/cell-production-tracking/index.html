Field problem and users

The problem was not an absence of process MES records. Electrode, assembly, inspection, and formation data existed, but there was no cell-level view connecting the full history.

Assembly combined the cathode, anode, separator, and two J/Rs into one cell. Actual material combinations could change within the same production lot, and electrode rework made production date alone insufficient for matching original and follow-up records. With roughly one week for electrode production and two weeks for assembly and formation, an investigation reviewed records across about three weeks.

The main users were pilot-production and engineering staff and the person preparing customer sample-history reports. Searching and organizing the manufacturing conditions and inspection results for ten customer-selected cells took about three days.

A request for cell-level MES lookup was rejected because of cost. My direct scope covered mapping the field search sequence, defining the data relationships, Power Query consolidation, Excel VBA search and linking logic, source-record comparison, and user handoff.

Data-link logic

  1. The cell S/N was the starting key. The assembly record supplied the J/R and component keys instead of relying on production date alone.
  2. Assembly keys were traced back to materials and electrode records. The tool followed cathode, anode, separator, and part lots to electrode rolls and production intervals.
  3. Material changes and rework were not overwritten. When several records were linked to one cell, original and follow-up records were shown using process time and work type.
  4. The next process key continued the lookup. Assembly, inspection, and formation were linked as one cell history.
  5. The result was checked against source records. S/N, lot, and timestamp values were compared with the original MES exports and process files.

Case 01. Trace a capacity anomaly back to an electrode-roll segment

01

Material/Data · Method · Measurement

Link cell performance to manufacturing history and narrow the inspection range

The tool did not decide the root cause automatically; it identified the shared roll segment and equipment that required field inspection.

Symptom and success criterion

Capacity anomalies repeated, but a single unit-process screen did not reveal the shared cause. The success criterion was to connect each abnormal S/N to its actual material, J/R, electrode roll, and production segment and confirm the same cause through source records and field inspection.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared assembly, inspection, and formation history by abnormal-cell S/N
  • Connected J/R, material lot, electrode roll, and production segment
  • Checked coating-equipment records and field condition for the shared segment
  • Cross-checked lookup results against MES and process files

Action and retest

The lookup showed that multiple cells passed through the same segment of one electrode roll. Field inspection then identified a coating-deficit segment associated with pump micro-vibration and a loosened fastener. The tool narrowed the roll segment and equipment to inspect rather than making an automatic root-cause decision.

Standardization

The investigation sequence was fixed as S/N → J/R → material and electrode roll → assembly and inspection → formation, with every automatic link cross-checked against the source record before reporting.
