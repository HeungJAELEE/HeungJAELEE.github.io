    Indy7 Digital Twin

    A 10-person training-team PoC connecting QR product traceability, three Indy7 assembly stations, YOLO inspection, PLC/SCADA, MySQL, and a digital twin. I led the overall planning and directly owned robot control and process integration.

    Project SOW — public work baseline

    The presentation did not contain a separate formal SOW document, so the public baseline was reconstructed from its objective, outputs, and confirmed responsibility boundaries.

    Objective: connect one QR-identified product to Robot A/B/C assembly, inspection, routing, equipment state, and MySQL history under the same product context.

    Success basis: store product_sn, complete Robot A/B assembly in sequence, apply the vision result to PLC routing and Robot C loading, expose status in SCADA/database views, and separate team output from individual contribution.

    How one product moved through the demonstration

    flowchart LR Q["QR product serial"] --> DB["MySQL history"] DB --> A["Robot A body assembly"] A --> B["Robot B window assembly"] B --> V["YOLO post-assembly inspection"] V -->|good| C["Robot C loading"] V -->|defect| NG["pass / separation"] A --> S["PLC and SCADA state"] B --> S V --> S C --> S S --> T["Digital-twin view"]

    1. QR serial as the traceability key

    The system read product_sn from a QR code and stored product serial, machine name, timestamp, tray serial, vision result, and defect type in MySQL.

    2. Body and window assembly by Robots A and B

    Robot A performed body assembly and Robot B performed window assembly. Integration required predecessor completion, product arrival, Ready/Busy/Error state, handoff state, restart position, and duplicate-execution prevention—not only waypoints.

    3. First vision stage — vehicle-color classification

    The initial model classified three classes: r, g, and b.

    4. Second vision stage — normal/crack candidates

    The later model expanded to r/g/b_normal and r/g/b_crack. Because representative physical-defect data were difficult to obtain, the important system evidence is how the decision entered PLC routing—not a headline model score.

    Vision metric interpretation

    Reported textPublic interpretation
    Three-class F1 1.00Result from the educational data and evaluation split, not evidence of production-defect generalization
    all classes 1.00 at 0.877Maximum F1 1.00 was observed at confidence 0.877; it does not mean that F1 equals 0.877

    Evaluation judgement: These figures support the educational model-to-routing connection. Without representative physical defects, an independent evaluation set, and a confusion matrix, they are not presented as production-quality approval or line performance.

    5. Robot C good-product loading and defect pass

    A good result sent the product to Robot C for loading; a defect result passed or separated the product without loading. Completion required agreement among the vision decision, PLC branch, Robot C result, SCADA state, and database history.

    Supporting analysis and unverified scope

    • Pi 5 FPS 5 and Pi 5 + Hailo-10H FPS 18 belong to one demonstration condition and are not general hardware guarantees.
    • MES/ERP was a development direction, not a deployed operating system.
    • Omniverse, Isaac Sim, and Physical AI were future goals, not completed physical synchronization.
    • Three/six-class vision results came from educational data and are not production-quality approval evidence.

    Validation still required

    1. End-to-end correlation of product serial with robot, vision, PLC, and database events.
    2. Recovery rules for network loss, duplicate commands, restart, and incomplete process state.
    3. Representative defect data and false-negative/false-positive cost.
    4. Separation of safety PLC/robot safety functions from HMI command authority.
    5. Long-duration checks for missing, duplicate, or reordered database events.
    6. Physical-to-digital coordinate and state-alignment error.

    Interview answer boundaries

    What was the hardest part?

    Moving one robot was easier than ensuring that the QR serial, robot completion state, vision result, and database record all referred to the same product. I defined the overall process and interfaces first, then connected each team’s implementation to that sequence.

    What did you implement directly?

    My main scope was overall project planning and Indy7 robot control/process integration. Teammates implemented detailed vision, Ladder, and SCADA components. The team lead owned the report and final presentation.

    Can the F1 figures be presented as production performance?

    No. The three-class curve shows maximum F1 1.00 at confidence 0.000, and the six-class curve shows maximum F1 1.00 at confidence 0.877 under the exercise data. Without raw split definitions, representative physical defects, confusion matrices, and field validation, these are not production-quality results.

    Why was the system later extended with PC-HMI, MQTT, and a web twin?

    The original project connected several layers but did not make command, state, result, and history ownership explicit enough. The later architecture gave physical command authority to the Robot Controller while separating HMI, database, PLC Bridge, and read-only twin responsibilities.
