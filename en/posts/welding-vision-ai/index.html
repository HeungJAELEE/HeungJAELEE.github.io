AI Vision Modeling for Weld Defect Detection

A welding-defect vision project that separates public and field data, compares model stages, and selects a threshold around recall, precision, and F1

Jul 9, 2026 · 7 minutes · Last reviewed Jul 31, 2026

  • Vision AI

30-second summary

On the shop floor, the cost of missing a defect can matter more than aggregate accuracy, so the PoC separated data sources and prioritized recall and threshold decisions.

Problem to solve

A model that performs well on public data cannot be assumed to behave the same on field images with different lighting, angles, and surface conditions.

My scope

  • Separated public training data from field practice images and ordered metric and threshold review around missed-defect risk.

Team boundary

This was a training-team PoC; model results are not presented as production-quality performance.

Disclosure scope: Field images and company quality criteria are not disclosed; only the data separation and decision process are described.

The path from data collection to possible production use is generalized to the publicly shareable PoC scope.

Why the shop-floor decision mattered

A missed micro-crack or pinhole can allow a defective weld to continue downstream. A false alarm creates reinspection work, but a missed defect can create a larger quality risk. In an imbalanced dataset, accuracy can remain high even when defects are missed, so the operating question was not simply how many images the model classified correctly. It was whether the model protected the defect class that the process could not afford to pass.

My battery-cell assembly experience with ultrasonic and laser welding informed that decision. The intended scope was a bounded inspection-assistance PoC that prioritizes candidates for human review, not a claim of completed autonomous inspection.

Why a digital method was needed

Surface reflections and normal weld marks can resemble defects, while defect shapes are not uniform. A vision model was used because writing a complete set of fixed image rules would not cover that variation. The data sources were kept separate so that performance on public images would not be presented as field performance.

  • Approximately 60,000 AI-Hub public welding images supplied broad learning coverage.
  • 198 field images provided a limited check under field capture conditions.

They were not presented as one undifferentiated set of 60,198 training images. Camera, illumination, surface, and product conditions give each source a different purpose.

My actual role

Within the team project, I worked on evaluation design and validation: F1, recall, false-positive behavior, model comparison, and threshold interpretation. The central tradeoff was to reduce false negatives first while deciding whether the remaining false positives could be handled through reinspection.

The team record progressed from a DenseNet-stage baseline through a two-model combination to a ResNet+DeiT ensemble. My contribution was to compare those stages within a common evaluation frame and interpret the operating threshold, not to present model complexity itself as the achievement.

Data flow

Public-image learning base → model-stage comparison → limited field-image check → threshold search over probability outputs → confusion-matrix and error-image review → inspection-assistance rule

Keeping each prediction with its label and defect probability allowed precision, recall, and F1 to be compared across thresholds without retraining the model for every decision point.

Implementation sequence

  1. Align the meaning of public labels with the field defect definition.
  2. Separate the learning and field-check purposes and prevent overlap between training and evaluation.
  3. Compare the team’s recorded model stages with the same metrics.
  4. Review the mixed normal/defect region in the second test.
  5. Find threshold regions that retained 100% recall, then inspect precision and F1 within that region.
  6. Return false-positive and false-negative images to the next data, label, and capture-condition review.

Verified output and current stage

At the selected threshold on the recorded evaluation set, the report lists F1 93.10%, Recall 100%, and Precision 87.07%. Recall 100% means that no defect in that evaluation set was missed; it does not guarantee all future defects or all equipment conditions. Precision 87.07% shows that some predicted defects may be normal and require reinspection.

The current stage is a bounded PoC. The 198 field images do not cover every equipment, material, lighting, and product-shape condition, and the project is not presented as a production-line deployment.

Deployment conditions and company value

Before deployment, the work needs independent product- or Lot-based splits, class-level sample review, standardized camera position and illumination, and latency and dropped-frame tests at line speed. Model version, threshold, image, equipment condition, and operator reinspection should be traceable, with approval and rollback for changes.

Under those conditions, the model could help inspectors review high-risk candidates first and feed recurring errors back into data improvement. The company value is a shared, auditable link between quality risk, model metrics, and reinspection policy. Any reduction in escaped defects or inspection time still requires controlled before-and-after measurement.

Design choices and work flow

The project is defined as a missed-defect risk decision, not an accuracy contest, and connects data, model, threshold, and reinspection tasks under one evaluation policy.

Problem

Micro-defect features overlap normal surfaces and the data is imbalanced, so accuracy alone does not represent the cost of passing a defective weld.

Goal

Separate public and field data, compare model stages on the same metrics, and design a recall-first threshold with a reinspection policy.

Success

F1, recall, and precision are reported together with threshold rationale, false-positive handling, and explicit production limits.

Scope and boundaries

In scope

  • Purpose separation for about 60,000 AI-Hub images and 198 field images
  • DenseNet baseline, two-model combination, and ResNet+DeiT comparison
  • Second test and threshold tuning around missed-defect risk

Out of scope or unverified

  • Claiming a dedicated public source repository
  • Generalization to every weld process and camera condition
  • Shipment decisions with no human reinspection path

Implementation and verification

What had to work

  • Separate data source and training, validation, and field-review purpose.
  • Compare model stages with the same F1, recall, precision, and split policy.
  • Record false negatives and false positives across thresholds and retain reinspection.

Quality and operating rules

  • Prioritize missed-defect cost over the cost of reinspecting a normal part.
  • Separate evaluation-set results from production claims and check leakage.
  • Treat lighting, material, and camera drift as separate validation work.

Why it was designed this way

  1. Use recall as the primary operating metric

    Passing a defective weld carries more quality risk than reinspecting a normal part.

  2. Keep 198 field images as a distinct validation view

    Small field data should not be mixed into a large public set to imply broad generalization.

  3. Compare threshold policy with model choice

    The same model produces different miss and reinspection costs at different thresholds.
