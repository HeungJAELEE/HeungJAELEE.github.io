Manufacturing quality problem the team addressed

The primary concern in weld-appearance inspection was not overall accuracy. It was reducing the chance that a real defect would be passed as normal. Lowering the decision threshold too far, however, would increase good-part reinspection, inspection labor, and cycle-time burden.

The team did not intend AI to make the final accept/reject decision. The objective was to identify defect candidates from weld images and connect uncertain cases to operator review.

Why missed defects came before accuracy

ErrorManufacturing impactDecision handling
Missed defectAn actual defect can reach downstream operations or the customerReview recall and false-negative images first
False alarmGood-part reinspection and inspection labor increaseCompare precision and reinspection load
Low confidenceModel score alone is insufficient for quality dispositionRoute to recapture or operator review
Model/operator disagreementAutomation can be misusedRecheck the original image and error type

The threshold was treated as a trade-off between missed-defect risk and reinspection burden, not as the value that produced the highest single score.

What was rechecked when capture conditions changed

Public welding material was used for candidate-model learning and comparison. Separately captured samples were used to inspect how lighting, angle, surface reflection, and capture quality changed error behavior.

  1. Candidate models were compared under the same evaluation items.
  2. Missed-defect and false-alarm images were reviewed directly.
  3. Error types that increased under different capture conditions were separated.
  4. Model scores were not generalized directly to field performance, and manual-review gates remained.

Modeling and evaluation decisions I owned

  • Defined the more critical manufacturing-inspection error first.
  • Compared model candidates using the same metrics and error images.
  • Reviewed how threshold changes affected both missed defects and false alarms.
  • Prevented low-confidence or operator-disagreement cases from being automatically accepted.
  • Framed the output as an inspection-assistance flow and deployment conditions rather than a model-score showcase.

Team result and future application

The five-person team completed a training PoC that compared error changes between public learning material and separately captured samples and reviewed missed-defect risk together with reinspection burden. Future work can add an independent set separated by product, lot, and capture time; fixed camera conditions; line-speed inference checks; and production shadow testing.
