What the team set out to solve

The team did not start with accuracy. It started with the risk of passing a real weld defect as normal. A normal-heavy dataset can produce a high accuracy score even when the model catches too few defects. Lowering the threshold too far, however, creates excessive reinspection of good parts.

The team trained candidate models on public weld-defect data and checked how their errors changed on field-like images captured under different conditions. The goal was not to replace the final quality decision. It was to propose defect candidates and define when an operator should review the result.

My core responsibility was the evaluation rule, not just the model

01

MY CORE

Missed-defect risk and reinspection burden were kept in one decision loop

I first defined which error was more dangerous, then compared model candidates under that rule.
flowchart LR A["Candidate-model comparison
MY CORE"] --> B["Recall, precision, and error images"] --> C["Missed defects and false alarms together"] --> D["Threshold and decision rule
MY CORE"]
  • False negatives can pass actual defects, so recall was reviewed first.
  • False positives add good-part reinspection and cycle-time cost, so they remained visible in the same comparison.
  • Candidate models used the same split and metrics, and threshold changes were reviewed by both error types.
  • Model probability was not treated as an automatic accept/reject result. Low confidence and model/operator disagreement were routed to recapture or manual review.

A public-data score was not reused when capture conditions changed

02

EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

Public learning data and field-like images had different roles

The field-like images were a limited capture-condition check, not an independent test set.

Observation

  • The learning base included 30,000 undercut and 30,000 overlap AI-Hub images.
  • About 198 field-like images differed in lighting, angle, reflection, and capture quality.
  • Candidates that appeared strong on public data produced more misses after capture conditions changed.

Decision and confirmation

  • Public data was used for learning; field-like images were used only to examine a distribution gap.
  • Augmentation and candidate families were compared under the same error view.
  • The reevaluation confirmed that one public-data score could not be generalized directly to field performance.
  • Exact final scores were excluded because a dedicated repository, fixed evaluation set, and reproducible run environment were not verified.

Team result and current stage

The team completed a training PoC that compared models and thresholds under a missed-defect-first rule and exposed the gap between public learning data and limited field-like images. I led overall planning and directly handled modeling, evaluation, and decision-criteria design.

The verified scope is the public-data learning and evaluation process, capture-condition error review, and manual-review rule. Production use would still require independent product/Lot/time-based evaluation data, fixed camera conditions, line-speed inference checks, shadow testing, and governed model and threshold changes.
