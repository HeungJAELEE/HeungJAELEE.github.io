Manufacturing case study

Pilot process validation

NCM Pre-Forming Validation That Replaced Manual Variation with a Jig Standard

A pilot process improvement that separated positive- and negative-electrode damage risks and established shape and position criteria

Project period
Started around Nov 2023 · validated during NCM pilot production
Context
Jastech, now JAS Robotics, Cell Development Team · NCM prismatic pilot line
Scope
Issue identification, cause narrowing, jig and acceptance-standard definition, sample and teardown testing, and documentation and equipment requirements

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Manual shaping before pre-welding varied by operator and repeatedly created uncoated-edge damage and short-circuit risk.
What I owned
I separated the positive- and negative-electrode damage mechanisms, built a temporary jig, defined shape, position, and acceptance criteria, and ran sample and teardown tests.
Verified result
The condition was verified with about 400 samples, used in subsequent pilot production, and reflected in process/inspection standards, the P-FMEA, and production-equipment RFQ.
Validation scope About 400 samples

Why manual shaping could not provide repeatability

During early NCM pilot production, uncoated-edge tearing, active-material detachment, and shorts appeared after pre-welding. Manual pre-shaping reduced the issue, but the position and geometry varied by operator. Even experienced operators showed about 1.5 mm of positional variation, so manual work could not reproduce the same result reliably.

Reducing weld conditions was also considered, but lowering ultrasonic energy or pressure would compromise the required joint strength. Using the ultrasonic welder motion itself for pre-shaping increased takt time and the risk of J/R damage. A separate pre-welding shape standard was required.

Positive- and negative-electrode damage had different mechanisms

The two electrodes showed different risks. The negative electrode was vulnerable to active-material detachment around the heavy edge, while the lower ductility of the positive Al foil made its uncoated edge more likely to tear.

The repeated damage was separated into four cause groups

WorkManual position and shape variation
ToolingNo dedicated jig or insertion criterion
MaterialNegative heavy edge and positive Al-foil ductility
MethodNo standard for radius, direction, or sequence

Damage location and teardown appearance separated weld-related problems from insufficient pre-shaping. A manually formed batch showed a reduction trend, so the next step was a jig that could replace operator judgment.

A temporary jig fixed the shape and position criteria

Different candidate shapes were prepared for the material characteristics of the positive and negative electrodes. A temporary jig fixed the forming position and radius. Internal dimensions are not disclosed; the acceptance condition required no short and no tearing, active-material detachment, or excessive indentation around the uncoated edge after teardown.

Incorrect J/R placement in the jig could create new damage, so insertion-position variation was included in the criterion. When the same issue reappeared with new operators, the jig condition stayed unchanged; retraining on insertion position and work method restored the intended result.

About 400 samples were used for validation

About 200 initial scrap samples were used to narrow shape and position candidates. Once conditions stabilized, another 200 conforming samples were checked across four lots, 50 per lot. The validation sequence for the roughly 400 samples was:

Forming and insertion → Short, Hi-Pot, and IR → Weld appearance and tensile check → Teardown → Formation → Capacity, charge/discharge, and life

Shorts were checked with the equipment tester. Teardown focused on the positive and negative uncoated edges and nearby coated areas. Downstream electrical and life results were also reviewed to ensure that pre-forming did not introduce a new quality issue.

The result became a pilot standard and equipment requirement

The condition continued to be used on the same pilot line for follow-on samples, other specifications, and customer-submission samples. The work produced or revised the jig concept and drawing, shape and position criteria, process conditions, work standard, QCP, manufacturing checklist, semi-finished inspection standard, P-FMEA, and the test and teardown report.

Pre-forming was included in the production-equipment RFQ and design, and equipment fabrication proceeded. The verified scope ends with pilot use, follow-on sample production, and equipment fabrication. Shipment, site installation, and unit testing were not verified.
