Initial pilot-production condition

During early NCM pilot production, tearing, active-material shedding, and shorts repeatedly appeared around the positive- and negative-electrode uncoated edges after ultrasonic pre-welding. Lowering the welding condition could reduce damage, but it also weakened the required joint. Manual pre-forming showed the right direction, yet position and R-shape changed from operator to operator.

The formal process name is Pre-Forming. The task was to shape the two uncoated edges before pre-welding according to their material behavior and replace operator feel with jig, geometry, position, and inspection criteria.

My direct responsibility covered symptom reproduction, positive/negative cause separation, temporary-jig concept and drawing, shape, forming-position and acceptance criteria, sample and teardown tests, process documents, and the production-equipment RFQ. The team result was the use of the pilot jig and criteria on subsequent samples, operator changes, other products and sizes, and customer-submission samples.

Case 01. Separate positive- and negative-electrode damage mechanisms

01

Material-specific damage separation

Positive foil tearing and negative active-material shedding were treated as different mechanisms

Teardown appearance and damage locations separated positive Al-foil risk from the negative heavy-edge risk.

Symptom and success criterion

After pre-welding, the positive side showed foil tearing while the negative side showed active-material shedding around the heavy edge. A single forming condition could reduce one failure mode while increasing excessive compression or tearing on the other side.

The acceptance basis required no short, no tearing, shedding, or excessive compression after teardown, and maintained weld appearance and tensile result.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared positive and negative damage location and geometry after teardown.
  • Changed only the pre-shape under the same pre-welding condition.
  • Checked Short, Hi-Pot, IR, weld appearance, and tensile result together.

Action and retest

  • Lower welding energy or pressure was rejected because it reduced required joint strength.
  • Using ultrasonic-welder motion for pre-forming was rejected because it increased takt time and J/R damage risk.

The positive and negative electrodes therefore required different R shapes and forming positions.

Result and standardization

Positive Al-foil tearing and negative heavy-edge shedding were separated as different damage mechanisms, and the material-specific R shapes and forming positions were transferred into the follow-on jig-design basis. Short, teardown, and tensile decisions remained in one acceptance flow.

Case 02. Convert manual position variation into a jig condition

02

Converting operator variation into jig criteria

Manual position spread was converted into insertion, geometry, and position criteria

One manually formed batch showed the direction, then a pilot jig was designed around operator placement variation.

Symptom and success criterion

One manually formed batch confirmed a damage-reduction direction. However, even trained operators generally showed about 1.5 mm of manual position variation, and differences in direction and pressure changed both the R shape and the damage location.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Used one manually formed batch to confirm the damage-reduction direction
  • Compared forming position, pressure, and R geometry by operator
  • Observed about 1.5 mm of typical position variation even among experienced operators
  • Compared jig candidates through short, appearance, and teardown results

MaterialDifferent acceptable compression and damage modes by electrodeCompared teardown appearance and damage location by materialJig-design inputReflect in positive/negative jig geometry

EnvironmentWork environmentLower explanatory power than operator and jig factorsRemoved as primary causeRetain the existing work environment

Action and retest

  • Defined separate R shapes and forming positions for the positive and negative electrodes.
  • Moved correct J/R insertion and work sequence into the jig criteria.
  • Included placement spread so small operator differences would not create excessive compression.
  • Confirmed a suitable internal R range and controlled the exact values in process documents.

Standardization

When a new operator inserted the J/R incorrectly and reproduced the damage, jig setting and correct insertion were retrained and checked again under the same basis.

Case 03. Separate exploratory trials from four-lot validation

03

Lot, functional, and teardown validation

The exploratory 200 and four-lot confirmation were kept as different test stages

Shape-candidate narrowing and selected-condition confirmation were separated across about 400 samples.

Symptom and success criterion

A geometry and position found in exploratory work could not become a pilot standard if it only worked for one operator or one lot. The success criterion was the same short, damage, weld, electrical, and performance disposition across operator and lot changes.

Action and retest

The selected pilot-jig, R-shape, and insertion conditions were applied to four lots × 50 cells, then retested under the same inspection basis across operator and lot changes.

Result and standardization

The selected condition was checked through Short, Hi-Pot, IR, weld tensile and appearance, formation, capacity, cycling, life, and teardown. The same criteria were then used for subsequent pilot-line samples, operator changes, other products and sizes, and customer-submission samples.

The pilot-jig concept and criteria were reflected in the production-equipment RFQ, design, and fabrication requirements. The confirmed completion boundary is pilot-jig and subsequent-sample use; equipment field validation remained a separate gate.
