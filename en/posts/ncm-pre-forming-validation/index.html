Pilot process validation

NCM Pre-Forming Process Validation

A pilot process improvement that separated positive- and negative-electrode damage risks and established shape and position criteria

Project period
Started around Nov 2023 · validated during NCM pilot production
Context
Jastech, now JAS Robotics, Cell Development Team · NCM prismatic pilot line
Scope
Issue identification, cause narrowing, jig and acceptance-standard definition, sample and teardown testing, and documentation and equipment requirements

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Manual shaping before pre-welding varied by operator and repeatedly created uncoated-edge damage and short-circuit risk.
What I owned
I separated the positive- and negative-electrode damage mechanisms, built a temporary jig, defined shape, position, and acceptance criteria, and ran sample and teardown tests.
Verified result
The condition was verified with about 400 samples, used in subsequent pilot production, and reflected in process/inspection standards, the P-FMEA, and production-equipment RFQ.
Validation scope About 400 samples

Validation flow

Early NCM pilot builds repeatedly showed uncoated-edge damage and shorts after pre-welding. Lowering weld energy weakened the required joint, while manual pre-forming reduced damage but introduced position and geometry variation. The selected approach was therefore to separate the positive- and negative-electrode damage mechanisms and replace operator feel with a jig and acceptance criteria.

01

4M-based process design validation

Replacing manual variation with a jig standard

I separated positive and negative damage mechanisms and directly defined geometry, position, and insertion criteria.

Initial signal

  • The negative electrode showed active-material shedding risk around its heavy-edge region.
  • The positive Al foil was less ductile and more prone to tearing at the uncoated edge.
  • Even trained operators showed roughly 1.5 mm of manual forming-position variation.
  • Reducing welding conditions would compromise required joint strength.

Alternatives reviewed

  • Lower welding energy or pressure was rejected because it reduced required joint strength.
  • Using the ultrasonic welder motion for pre-forming was rejected because it increased takt time and J/R damage risk.
  • J/R insertion variation was included in robustness checks because incorrect placement could create new damage.

Sample validation

Manual pre-forming position varied by about 1.5 mm. To convert that variation into jig geometry, position, and insertion criteria, about 200 disposable samples were used for exploration, followed by four lots × 50 samples = 200 samples after condition stabilization. The total validation scope was about 400 samples.

Short, Hi-Pot, IR, weld appearance, tensile force, teardown, formation, capacity, cycling, and life were checked. Passing criteria required no fracture, active-material loss, excessive compression, or short around the uncoated area.

Standardization

  • Created the jig concept/drawing and electrode-specific geometry and position criteria.
  • Drafted revisions to process conditions, work standard, QCP, manufacturing checklist, in-process inspection, and P-FMEA.
  • Wrote the test and teardown report, prepared the production-equipment RFQ, and trained the internal team.

The next stage is to confirm those criteria through shipment, site installation, unit testing, and repeated production.
