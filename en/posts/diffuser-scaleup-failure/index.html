Failure case

Diffuser Ultrasonic-Stripping Scale-up Failure and Validation Gates

A case showing what must be closed before equipment fabrication and when a scale-up should stop

Project period
National R&D project during YMC employment
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Sample evaluation, risk documentation, scale-up review, and prevention-gate definition

Premise, decision, and lesson

Initial premise
Lab stripping was possible, but overlapping ultrasonic zones damaged the surface and left roughness, diffusion, and reusability risks unresolved.
What I checked
I documented sample and scale-up results and turned relative product/transducer movement, repeatability, and stopping criteria into pre-fabrication checks.
Decision rule
Normal operation was not achieved; the case established that stripping feasibility and product reusability must be judged separately.

Goal and initial premise

The existing Diffuser cleaning process used bead blasting, acid treatment, and high-pressure washing and took several days. This national R&D project investigated whether ultrasonic cavitation could replace the first two steps while retaining the final high-pressure rinse.

“The layer can be removed” and “the product can be reused without damage” are different statements. Roughness, diffusion performance, physical integrity, and reusability all had to pass.

Risks found at lab scale

The sample work identified several open risks:

  • post-stripping roughness could leave the original control range;
  • diffusion behavior could change product function;
  • reusability under the customer’s process was uncertain;
  • overlapping ultrasonic zones concentrated erosion on parts of the surface;
  • frequency adjustment alone could not create uniform treatment while product and transducer positions remained fixed.

These were not issues that a larger machine would solve automatically. They required acceptance and stop criteria before fabrication.

Risks that remained open

Equipment fabrication and scale-up proceeded before sample quality and reusability risks were fully closed. Schedule and fabrication advanced without a sufficiently explicit separation between “conditions required before fabrication” and “results that should stop the project.”

Re-reading the open risks with 4M

Looking back through 4M makes the risks that should have closed before scale-up more explicit.

4MOpen issueCheck needed first
ManDevelopment, equipment, and quality roles and stop authority were not explicitAssign review ownership and stop authority for each gate
MachineUltrasonic output, fixtures, cleaning, and handling lacked a proven transfer rule for larger area and geometryConfirm condition transfer and repeatability from lab to scale-up equipment
MaterialGeometry and surface state could change roughness, diffusion, and reusabilityCheck CTQs on representative samples and under reuse conditions
MethodStripping and product reusability were treated too much like one success criterion, without clear stop rulesSeparate stripping, damage, reuse, and repeatability decisions

This was not one person’s or one machine’s failure. The management structure did not close 4M risks before allowing the next stage to begin.

Where the sequence broke when viewed through DMAIC

This does not claim that the original project formally used DMAIC. It is a retrospective view of which steps remained open.

  1. Define — Residue stripping was defined, but the final CTQ of a product that could re-enter the customer process after stripping was not separated clearly enough.
  2. Measure — Repeatable baselines for roughness, diffusion, damage, and reusability before scale-up were insufficient.
  3. Analyze — 4M risks were visible, but cause-specific pass and stop conditions were not closed.
  4. Improve — Equipment fabrication and scale-up advanced while earlier risks remained open.
  5. Control — Pre-fabrication approval gates, damage stop rules, and pre-production revalidation conditions were insufficient.

The later rule was simple: do not move into equipment fabrication while Define, Measure, and Analyze remain open.

Scale-up result and failure decision

After scale-up, evidence was still insufficient to show that roughness, diffusion, and reusability issues had been resolved. A conditional next step was allowed, but the same damage pattern returned and the system did not reach repeatable normal operation.

The value came from converting the outcome into gates for later work.

Gates to require before the next scale-up

GatePass conditionIf it fails
Sample qualityPost-process CTQs remain within the existing control rangeReturn to condition exploration
ReusabilityRe-entry under customer-use conditions is supportedHold scale-up
RepeatabilityRepeated samples reproduce the resultRevisit cause and measurement
Equipment conditionsFixture, ultrasonic, cleaning, and handling conditions are definedHold detailed design
Stop criteriaDamage or quality deviation triggers an explicit stopHold test approval
  1. Confirm that ultrasonic stripping is technically possible.
  2. Confirm that relative product/transducer movement prevents concentrated overlap zones.
  3. Confirm sample CTQs and product reusability.
  4. Confirm that repeated samples reproduce the result.
  5. Approve the conditions that must stop the project.
  6. Begin detailed equipment design and scale-up only after the earlier gates close.
  7. Revalidate the result under production conditions.

In this project, later steps advanced before the earlier gates were closed.

What this changed in my later work

  1. Separate a technically possible phenomenon from a production-capable process.
  2. Equipment fabrication does not close unresolved product-quality risk.
  3. Approve stop criteria as well as success criteria before scale-up.
  4. A failed case becomes useful engineering knowledge when it changes the next validation sequence.
