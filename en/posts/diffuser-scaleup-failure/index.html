Manufacturing decision case

Failure case

Diffuser Ultrasonic Stripping Scale-up Failure Analysis

A case showing what must be closed before equipment fabrication and when a scale-up should stop

Project period
National R&D project during YMC employment
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Sample evaluation, risk documentation, scale-up review, and prevention-gate definition

Premise, decision, and lesson

Initial premise
Lab stripping was possible, but overlapping ultrasonic zones damaged the surface and left roughness, diffusion, and reusability risks unresolved.
What I checked
I documented sample and scale-up results and turned relative product/transducer movement, repeatability, and stopping criteria into pre-fabrication checks.
Decision rule
Normal operation was not achieved; the case established that stripping feasibility and product reusability must be judged separately.

Where the project sequence broke

The objective was to ultrasonically remove residue from a Diffuser part and return it to a reusable state. Lab work showed that stripping was technically possible, but scale-up advanced before roughness, diffusion, surface-damage, and reusability risks were closed.

01

Actual work · Failure Retrospective

Scale-up with open lab risks

Technical stripping feasibility and reusable-product success were not separated strongly enough.

Objective and premise

  • Confirm whether ultrasound could remove the residue.
  • Confirm surface quality and the ability to re-enter the customer process.
  • Treat technical stripping and production capability as separate success criteria.

4M risk map

Open lab risks

  • Stripping feasibility was confirmed.
  • Roughness, diffusion, surface damage, and reusability remained open.
  • Repeatability across samples was not closed.
  • Relative movement between the product and transducer was needed to reduce overlap concentration.

Scale-up decision

  • Equipment scale-up advanced while the earlier risks remained open.
  • Evidence was still insufficient to show that roughness, diffusion, and reuse issues had been resolved.
  • The same damage pattern returned after a conditional next step.
  • The system did not reach repeatable normal operation.
02

Post-hoc mapping · Gate Design

Gates required before the next scale-up

The failure was converted into product-quality, reuse, repeatability, equipment, and stop gates.

Gate 1 · Sample quality

  • Post-process roughness, diffusion, and other CTQs must remain in the existing control range.
  • Damage or quality deviation returns the work to condition exploration.

Gate 2 · Reusability

  • Re-entry under customer-use conditions must be supported.
  • Stripping alone does not approve scale-up.

Gate 3 · Repeatability

  • Repeated samples must reproduce the result.
  • Unstable results return to measurement and cause review.

Gate 4 · Equipment conditions

  • Fixture, ultrasound, relative movement, cleaning, and handling conditions must be defined.
  • Detailed design does not begin while these remain open.

Gate 5 · Stop criteria

  • Damage and quality-deviation thresholds and approval owners must be defined.
  • Success and stop criteria are approved together.

Use boundary

  • These gates did not formally exist during the original project.
  • They were created afterward as a prevention sequence for later projects.
