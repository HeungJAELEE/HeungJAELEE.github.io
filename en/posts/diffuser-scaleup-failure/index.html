Failure case

Diffuser Ultrasonic Stripping Scale-up Failure Analysis and R&D Stop Gates

Traced the chain from missing exhaust and chemical removal to erosion in fixed transducer overlap zones, then stopped scale-up and established future gates

Project period
National R&D project, Oct 2018–Dec 2019
Context
Completed in YMC's Technical R&D Center
Scope
Lab/full-part condition trials, roughness, microscopy, SEM–EDS, particles, root-cause chain, scale-up stop, and future gates

Premise, decision, and lesson

Initial premise
Chemical removal after missing exhaust infrastructure and erosion, particle, and reuse failure in water-plus-ultrasonic scale-up
What I checked
Damage-mechanism and structural-limit determination through lab/full-part trials, roughness, microscopy, SEM–EDS, and particles
Decision rule
Scale-up stop and legacy-process return; mandatory relative-motion, surface-damage, reuse, and EHS gates

1. Process-condition transition

StageProcess configurationWork timeQuality state
LegacyBead blast, chemical, high-pressure/manual work, cleaning, and dryingAbout three daysExisting reuse criteria
Initial limited trialExisting stripping chemical plus ultrasoundAbout eight hoursRemoval feasibility
Scale-upWater, ultrasound, high-pressure water, and manual workAbout one and a half daysErosion, particle, and reuse failure

The scale-up equipment did not include the fume-exhaust infrastructure required for chemical use. Removing chemical action increased dependence on ultrasonic impact, high-pressure water, manual work, and equipment residence time.

2. Root-cause chain

CAPEX constraint and missing fume-exhaust infrastructure
→ stripping chemical removed from the scale-up process
→ greater dependence on water and ultrasonic mechanical action
→ longer stripping and equipment residence
→ repeated impact in fixed transducer-overlap zones
→ no relative motion between product and transducer
→ concentrated cavitation erosion
→ pitting, roughness increase, surface loss, and color change
→ particle increase and repeated-reuse degradation
→ scale-up stop and return to the legacy process

3. Damage-mechanism validation

Validation axisMeasurement and observationDecision
Damage locationTransducer-overlap zone and generic aluminum sampleDamage aligned with fixed acoustic field
Surface morphologyPitting, surface loss, and color changeSubstrate erosion
RoughnessRa, Rz, and RyRoughness increase with residence time
Surface and compositionMetallurgical microscopy and SEM–EDSResidue and substrate damage separated
ParticlesPost-strip and repeated-reuse evaluationCumulative particle increase
Structural alternativesHeight, distance, overlap, and shielding trialsDamage location moved; cumulative impact remained

Height, distance, and shielding trials moved the damaged location but did not remove cumulative impact. Lack of relative motion between transducer and product was classified as the primary structural limitation.

4. Scale-up stop decision

Normal operation was stopped because the following three conditions were not satisfied together.

  • Processability: work time, high-pressure-water use, and manual work remained above target after chemical removal
  • Quality: substrate roughness, erosion, and particle criteria failed
  • Reusability: damage accumulated over repeated reuse and customer evaluation

The issue expanded around the third reuse after initial equipment performance, and the operation returned to the legacy process.

5. Risk items added after failure analysis

Risk item added or revisedMandatory controlFuture design and test input
Erosion in fixed transducer-overlap zonesRelative product/transducer motionMoving transducer or product-transfer structure
Cumulative damage under long residenceTime-based roughness and surface inspectionMaximum residence and stop criteria
Removal-rate-only dispositionIndependent substrate-damage and particle gatesQuality acceptance matrix
Degradation over repeated reuseMulti-reuse samplesRepeated-cycle validation plan
Unresolved chemical, exhaust, and wastewater conditionsEHS and infrastructure pre-approvalExhaust and wastewater CAPEX gate
Schedule-driven scale-upExplicit approver and stop criteriaStage review and stop authority

6. Lessons learned and future R&D standard

Future scale-up would enter detailed equipment design only after these gates were passed:

  1. equivalence between lab and scale-up process configuration;
  2. relative motion and elimination of fixed overlap zones;
  3. substrate roughness, pitting, and surface damage after stripping;
  4. particles and repeated reusability;
  5. cumulative damage under long-time and repeated samples;
  6. chemical, fume-exhaust, and wastewater EHS infrastructure; and
  7. explicit stop criteria and approval authority.

7. Results

  • Established initial chemical-plus-ultrasonic stripping feasibility
  • Derived the causal relationship between scale-up condition changes and quality degradation
  • Classified fixed transducer overlap and no relative motion as the primary structural limitation
  • Secured evidence of cavitation erosion, particle failure, and reuse degradation
  • Stopped scale-up and restored the legacy process
  • Established future equipment-design, quality, and EHS stop gates
