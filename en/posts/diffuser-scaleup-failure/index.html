TECHNICAL CASE STUDY

Diffuser Scale-Up Production-Introduction Failure

A YMC failure case in which a chemical-plus-ultrasonic lab result entered scale-up, but missing exhaust infrastructure, fixed transducer overlap, and no relative motion caused cavitation erosion and led to a stop decision and future R&D gates

Aug 1, 2026 · 4 minutes · Last reviewed Aug 10, 2026

  • Scale-up
  • Root Cause
  • Cavitation Erosion
  • Stop Gate

Project overview

Item Description
Period Oct 2018–Dec 2019
Type National R&D project for stripping and reuse of an aluminum diffuser
Direct scope Lab and full-part trials, condition-based samples, roughness/microscope/SEM–EDS/particle analysis, and scale-up risk disposition
Goal Shorter process and less high-pressure-water work while retaining surface quality, particles, and reusability
Final decision Scale-up stop, return to the legacy process, and future R&D stop criteria

1. Process-condition transition

Stage Process configuration Work time Quality state
Legacy Bead blast, chemical, high-pressure/manual work, cleaning, and drying About three days Existing reuse criteria
Initial limited trial Existing stripping chemical plus ultrasound About eight hours Removal feasibility
Scale-up Water, ultrasound, high-pressure water, and manual work About one and a half days Erosion, particle, and reuse failure

The scale-up equipment did not include the fume-exhaust infrastructure required for chemical use. Removing chemical action increased dependence on ultrasonic impact, high-pressure water, manual work, and equipment residence time.

2. Root-cause chain

CAPEX constraint and missing fume-exhaust infrastructure
→ stripping chemical removed from the scale-up process
→ greater dependence on water and ultrasonic mechanical action
→ longer stripping and equipment residence
→ repeated impact in fixed transducer-overlap zones
→ no relative motion between product and transducer
→ concentrated cavitation erosion
→ pitting, roughness increase, surface loss, and color change
→ particle increase and repeated-reuse degradation
→ scale-up stop and return to the legacy process

3. Damage-mechanism validation

Validation axis Measurement and observation Decision
Damage location Transducer-overlap zone and generic aluminum sample Damage aligned with fixed acoustic field
Surface morphology Pitting, surface loss, and color change Substrate erosion
Roughness Ra, Rz, and Ry Roughness increase with residence time
Surface and composition Metallurgical microscopy and SEM–EDS Residue and substrate damage separated
Particles Post-strip and repeated-reuse evaluation Cumulative particle increase
Structural alternatives Height, distance, overlap, and shielding trials Damage location moved; cumulative impact remained

Height, distance, and shielding trials moved the damaged location but did not remove cumulative impact. Lack of relative motion between transducer and product was classified as the primary structural limitation.

4. Scale-up stop decision

Normal operation was stopped because the following three conditions were not satisfied together.

  • Processability: work time, high-pressure-water use, and manual work remained above target after chemical removal
  • Quality: substrate roughness, erosion, and particle criteria failed
  • Reusability: damage accumulated over repeated reuse and customer evaluation

The issue expanded around the third reuse after initial equipment performance, and the operation returned to the legacy process.

5. Risk items added after failure analysis

Risk item added or revised Mandatory control Future design and test input
Erosion in fixed transducer-overlap zones Relative product/transducer motion Moving transducer or product-transfer structure
Cumulative damage under long residence Time-based roughness and surface inspection Maximum residence and stop criteria
Removal-rate-only disposition Independent substrate-damage and particle gates Quality acceptance matrix
Degradation over repeated reuse Multi-reuse samples Repeated-cycle validation plan
Unresolved chemical, exhaust, and wastewater conditions EHS and infrastructure pre-approval Exhaust and wastewater CAPEX gate
Schedule-driven scale-up Explicit approver and stop criteria Stage review and stop authority

6. Lessons learned and future R&D standard

Future scale-up would enter detailed equipment design only after these gates were passed:

  1. equivalence between lab and scale-up process configuration;
  2. relative motion and elimination of fixed overlap zones;
  3. substrate roughness, pitting, and surface damage after stripping;
  4. particles and repeated reusability;
  5. cumulative damage under long-time and repeated samples;
  6. chemical, fume-exhaust, and wastewater EHS infrastructure; and
  7. explicit stop criteria and approval authority.

7. Results

  • Established initial chemical-plus-ultrasonic stripping feasibility
  • Derived the causal relationship between scale-up condition changes and quality degradation
  • Classified fixed transducer overlap and no relative motion as the primary structural limitation
  • Secured evidence of cavitation erosion, particle failure, and reuse degradation
  • Stopped scale-up and restored the legacy process
  • Established future equipment-design, quality, and EHS stop criteria
