Validation lesson from failure

Diffuser Ultrasonic-Stripping Scale-up Failure and Validation Gates

A case showing what must be closed before equipment fabrication and when a scale-up should stop

Goal and initial premise

This national R&D project investigated ultrasonic stripping of residue from display Diffuser components, with the aim of moving from lab samples to repeatable equipment operation.

“The layer can be removed” and “the product can be reused without damage” are different statements. Roughness, diffusion performance, physical integrity, and reusability all had to pass.

Risks found at lab scale

The sample work identified several open risks:

  • post-stripping roughness could leave the original control range;
  • diffusion behavior could change product function;
  • reusability under the customer’s process was uncertain;
  • ultrasonic, fixture, cleaning, and handling conditions changed with area and geometry.

These were not issues that a larger machine would solve automatically. They required acceptance and stop criteria before fabrication.

Risks that remained open

Equipment fabrication and scale-up proceeded before sample quality and reusability risks were fully closed. Schedule and fabrication advanced without a sufficiently explicit separation between “conditions required before fabrication” and “results that should stop the project.”

This framing does not assign failure to an individual. It examines the sequence connecting development, equipment, quality, and decision-making.

Scale-up result and failure decision

After scale-up, evidence was still insufficient to show that roughness, diffusion, and reusability issues had been resolved. The system therefore did not reach repeatable normal operation or production success.

The value came from converting the outcome into gates for later work.

Gates to require before the next scale-up

Gate Pass condition If it fails
Sample quality Post-process CTQs remain within the existing control range Return to condition exploration
Reusability Re-entry under customer-use conditions is supported Hold scale-up
Repeatability Repeated samples reproduce the result Revisit cause and measurement
Equipment conditions Fixture, ultrasonic, cleaning, and handling conditions are defined Hold detailed design
Stop criteria Damage or quality deviation triggers an explicit stop Hold test approval

In this project, later steps advanced before the earlier gates were closed.

Transferable lessons

  1. Separate a technically possible phenomenon from a production-capable process.
  2. Equipment fabrication does not close unresolved product-quality risk.
  3. Approve stop criteria as well as success criteria before scale-up.
  4. A failed case becomes useful engineering knowledge when it changes the next validation sequence.
