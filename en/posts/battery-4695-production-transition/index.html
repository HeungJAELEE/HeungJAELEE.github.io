Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
No equipment could run automatically from Start through completion and normal MES history was absent, so assembly production-start conditions had to be rechecked against an initial SAT baseline.
Key role
I set assembly-wide trial and acceptance criteria and managed local 4M+2E, punch owners, deadlines, and retests.
Key result
Two 100-cell lots and a final 50-cell one-way run confirmed the limited-production start conditions, and I revised or created work standards, QCP, P-FMEA, checklists, and MES control items.

Production-transition background

Product development was complete, but no equipment could run automatically from Start through completion. Operators moved each unit manually, and the abnormal cycle meant MES history no longer represented the actual process state. Repeating partial repairs could not establish which equipment was ready for production.

The full assembly process was returned to an initial SAT-level baseline. Process purpose, safety conditions, product sensing, sequence, quality measurement, and production history were realigned, then actual cells were used for the production-start decision.

Representative case — Collector laser-welding auto-cycle recovery

01

Re-SAT · 4M+2E · actual-cell retest

Recovering manually operated equipment to an auto cycle

Product sensing, sequence, waits, clamp, alignment, and speed were realigned while safety interlocks remained in place.

Symptom and acceptance criteria

The equipment laser-welded the cylindrical J/R bare area to the collector plate. It could not discharge products automatically, so operators manually controlled every unit from loading through discharge.

Acceptance itemCriterion
SafetyDoor, laser, and operator-protection interlocks retained
SensingCurrent product detected reliably
MotionProcess sequence and unit-to-unit waits normalized
MechanicsClamp, pressure, and alignment match the product condition
DischargeAutomatic discharge after process completion
QualityActual cell inspection meets the defined criteria
HistoryNormal cycle and product result can be recorded

Reproduction and measurement

  • Recorded the manual sequence and repeated error point by process step
  • Compared sensors, sequence, waits, and speed against normal equipment
  • Checked how current geometry, size, and reflectivity affected sensing and alignment
  • Used one trial record for actual motion, in-process inspection, and cell results because legacy MES history was unreliable

Direct action and retest

  • Adjusted sensor position and conditions for the current product
  • Reduced functionally unnecessary waiting time
  • Reset clamp, pressure, and alignment
  • Checked process sequence and speed consistency across units
  • Retained door, laser, and operator-protection interlocks
  • Rechecked function, motion, and product conditions for about two weeks using an initial SAT-style sequence
  • Tested two lots of 100 cells, about 200 cells in total
  • Used the final 50-cell one-way run for the limited-production start decision

Punch control and approval

The punch list was not a simple done/not-done checklist. It connected symptom and quality impact, owner, due date, action, retest method, and decision result.

Control itemApplication
Symptom and impactRecord the process step, repeated error, and product-quality risk
Owner and deadlineAssign engineering, maintenance, manufacturing, or quality owner before production start
ActionRecord changes to sensors, sequence, speed, alignment, and measurement criteria
RetestCompare before and after under the same product, motion, and inspection criteria
DecisionAdopt the action only after normal operation and actual-cell testing pass

Taguchi analysis was used to review condition ranges. When an abnormal result appeared, the next test direction was reset from process purpose and product evidence. Engineering, development, quality, and manufacturing-innovation owners reviewed the final result.
