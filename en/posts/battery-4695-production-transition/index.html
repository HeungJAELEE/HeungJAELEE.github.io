Manufacturing case study

Production-transition validation

Revalidating 4695 Production Readiness from Initial SAT

A production-transition case that rebuilt automatic cycles, inspection, measurement methods, and process conditions for limited production to the quality standard

Project period
4695 assembly-line production-transition validation during Kumyang employment
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · production-readiness recovery for a development-complete product
Scope
Set assembly-wide trial, measurement, and setup directions while coordinating production, quality, and maintenance actions and retests

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Development and handover were complete, but no machine sustained a normal automatic cycle and vision inspection was inactive, so production depended on manual intervention.
What I owned
I reset the operating sequence and quality trials across assembly, then revised measurement methods, process conditions, standards, and MES control items.
Verified result
The project confirmed an improvement from about 50% to about 95% good yield under full inspection and reached limited production to the quality standard.
Fully inspected good yield About 50% → about 95%
Inspection sample consumption About 15/day → 7–8/day

Development was complete, but the line was not production-ready

Product development and equipment handover had finished, yet no machine could run a normal automatic cycle from start to finish. Vision inspection was inactive, and operators manually moved the process forward whenever equipment stopped. Existing standards described an ideal good product but did not explain the variation and manual intervention seen in repeat production.

With a request for more than 2,000 cells per month already placed, revising documents alone was not enough. I returned the entire assembly flow to an initial-SAT level and rechecked equipment behavior, product quality, and measurement feasibility.

A new punch list began with comparison to a working line

I first reviewed how each process ran and stopped on a stable 2170 line. I then interviewed 4695 operators and engineers about their current manual workarounds. The gaps became a process-ordered punch list, and production, technology, and maintenance engineers were assigned as each process reopened.

After a machine ran normally, I defined the process purpose, trial method, and decision criteria. Taguchi methods were used to find workable condition ranges, and I redirected trials when intermediate results did not match the process objective. The PLC-to-Vision decision structure was reviewed but not changed during this project.

Measurement methods were standardized before process capability

Semi-finished parts already had inspection items, but locations and methods varied by inspector. The same product could receive different results, consuming additional samples and mixing measurement variation with process variation.

I worked with quality to standardize measurement by semi-finished product and wrote the MSA criteria. The master specification and intermediate-product specifications remained unchanged; equipment settings and inspection methods were rebuilt so the process could repeatedly meet them. Daily inspection-sample consumption fell from about 15 to about 7–8. This figure refers to inspection samples, not production volume.

The verified endpoint was limited production

The project did not claim a completed production transfer. The gates were an automatic cycle from start to finish, active Vision and inspection, products meeting the quality standard, and current MES records. Where immediate mechanical redesign was not possible, a bounded operator-intervention rule was defined.

Initial full inspection showed about 50% final good cells divided by initial assembly input. After recovery and retesting, the same calculation reached about 95%. This was a cross-functional project result. My scope covered assembly-wide trial direction, measurement and setup criteria, coordination, and document revision.

Controls were left for the next production run

I revised the operating standard, QCP, checklists, P-FMEA, MES critical-control items, quality measurement standard, and MSA criteria. Limit samples and semi-finished evaluation rules were aligned so production and quality could use the same decisions.

Dedicated measurement jigs, vibration and other mechanical issues, O-ring material, and the PLC-to-Vision decision structure remained as follow-up work. The verified result was therefore limited production meeting the quality standard, rebuilt from initial SAT—not a fully completed production transfer.
