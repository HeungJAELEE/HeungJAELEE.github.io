Production-readiness handoff and initial state

Product development and equipment SAT/acceptance had already been completed. The program entered production readiness in February 2025, but actual new products could not be processed with consistent automatic operation, inspection, or measurement. After the development engineers left, the manufacturing-engineering team rechecked the equipment and process conditions against actual cells.

Operators moved individual units manually. Product geometry, reflectivity, and turn-count changes were not reflected in vision and X-ray settings, and temporary recipes had accumulated around equipment that was not mechanically aligned. Measurement items existed, but the fixture, location, sectioning, and disposition methods were not sufficiently defined.

The major recovery and product retest took place in August 2025. Two lots of 100 cells and the final 50-cell full-process test were completed at the end of August, followed by handoff and departure in September.

V1, V2, and V5 had only the YOLO model created for the SAT product and could not automatically judge the new product. There was no internal modeling owner or training-program access, and product/laser geometry could not be sent outside the company. The vision supplier did not provide training guidance, model source, or practical retraining support.

A physical master jig was requested but rejected until the final product was fixed. Existing patterned products were managed as reference samples.

The two-lot and final 50-cell results validate the product and inspection procedure. They are not performance evidence for automatic AI decisions at V1, V2, and V5 or for long-term model drift.

Design-limited equipment and controlled operation

E/L filling

Electrolyte leakage, fill shortage/variation, and contamination recurred at the seal between the hopper and chamber. The spare size was measured and compared with standard/distributor information; the supplied component’s acid/alkali response did not support the required chemical resistance.

Urgent production continued by replacing the existing spare and checking leakage, low fill, and contamination around the seal. Applying a chemically resistant component required changes to both O-ring size and the fastening/seal structure; the modification request and punch were rejected.

Final state: limited operation with existing spares / material, size, and sealing-structure redesign gate

Crimping and sizing

One press did not reach the target height, so the same operation was repeated two to three times. Total-height variation, crimp-shape and can/cap deformation, three-jaw sticking, scratches, cycle stops, and bottlenecks followed.

HMI torque and pressure-sensitive sheets confirmed high load and eccentric load transfer. No permanent structural action was implemented. Grease quantity was increased and speed was reduced for temporary operation. Production-trial units received 100% checks of height, inner diameter, appearance, sealing, leakage, drop, impact, and vent-related items.

Final state: limited production with temporary operation and 100% inspection / drive, load-path, and three-jaw redesign gate

Marking

Lot, QR, date, and time formats were fixed in the initial software, and new patterns could not be registered in MES. Lot and QR values were entered manually; marking was checked visually and with a scanner, then recorded manually in MES.

Final state: limited production through manual marking, inspection, and MES entry / template, registration, and user-change-function gate
