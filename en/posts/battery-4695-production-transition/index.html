Manufacturing case study

Limited-production validation

Revalidating 4695 Production Readiness from Initial SAT

A production-transition case that restored automatic cycles and measurement controls, then used a final one-way run to decide whether limited production could begin

Project period
4695 assembly-line limited-production validation during Kumyang employment
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · production-readiness recovery for a development-complete product
Scope
Set assembly-wide trial, measurement, and setup directions while coordinating production, quality, and maintenance actions and retests

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Development and handover were complete, but automatic cycles and vision inspection did not run normally, operators moved each unit manually, and MES history was not recorded correctly.
What I owned
I set assembly-wide trial and decision criteria, coordinated technology, maintenance, and production actions, and revised measurement methods and control documents.
Verified result
After recovery trials covering about 200 cells in two lots over roughly two weeks, the final 50-cell one-way run achieved FYD of at least 90%, which was used as the limited-production start gate.
Automatic-cycle recovery trials About 200 cells · 2 lots
Final one-way run 50 cells · FYD at least 90%

Development completion did not mean production readiness

Product development and equipment handover had finished, yet no machine sustained an automatic cycle from start to finish. Vision inspection was inactive, and operators moved each unit manually whenever the equipment stopped. This made repeat production impractical and prevented normal MES history from being recorded.

Existing standards described an ideal good product but not the variation and operator intervention found on the actual line. A request for more than 2,000 cells per month was already in place. Instead of revising documents alone, I returned the assembly flow to initial-SAT checks and revalidated equipment behavior, product quality, measurement methods, and traceability conditions.

The 4M view separated the causes behind routine manual operation

The situation first looked like a collection of equipment-setting problems. It became clear that equipment, product changes, and operating methods were misaligned together. The 4M view was used to organize that mismatch, not to assign blame.

The causes extended beyond individual equipment settings

ManAbnormal states were not closed across teams, and manual workarounds became routine
MachineSensor, interlock, sequence, speed, and setting mismatches
MaterialPost-SAT changes in geometry, dimensions, and reflectivity were not applied
MethodInsufficient stabilization, validation, change control, and working standards

I compared the 4695 manual workflow with the sequence of a working 2170 line and created a punch list. I led trial direction, decision criteria, coordination, and documentation. Technology and maintenance engineers adjusted equipment settings with me, while production supported trial runs.

The collector Laser Welding equipment was returned to an automatic cycle

The representative deep-dive was the Laser Welding equipment that joined the uncoated J/R area to the collector. Operators had been moving it manually from beginning to end. I compared its sequence with a working machine and checked sensor positions and conditions, clamp pressure and alignment, and the speed and sequence between units.

CompareReview the working-machine sequence RecordList manual actions and stop points RecoverAdjust sensors, clamps, alignment, and speed RetestCheck automatic cycle and product quality DecideUse a one-way run as the start gate

Door, laser, and operator safety interlocks remained unchanged. Only unnecessarily long unit waiting times in process interlocks were adjusted. The PLC-to-Vision decision structure was reviewed but not changed during the project.

Recovery trials and the final one-way run defined the start gate

Recovery and condition trials covered about 200 cells in two lots over roughly two weeks. This entire quantity was not one yield denominator; it was the scope used to test different recovery conditions.

The final check was a 50-cell one-way run without operators moving each unit manually. FYD was at least 90% in this run, and the result was used to decide that limited production could begin. The previously published yield figure was removed because production, quality-good, and inspection-sample denominators had been mixed.

The final 50-cell result was a limited-production start decision. It does not establish long-term repeat-production yield or commercial mass production.

Automatic operation and measurement decisions were left as controls

The work did not end when the equipment moved. Measurement methods and decision criteria were aligned by semi-finished product. I revised the operating standard, QCP, and manufacturing checklist; created the P-FMEA, MES critical-control items, and equipment punch list; and expanded semi-finished inspection and quality-measurement standards.

Dedicated measurement jigs, several vibration and mechanical issues, O-ring material, and the PLC-to-Vision structure remained follow-up work. The confirmed result was restoring the manufacturing readiness of a development-complete product through the limited-production start gate.
