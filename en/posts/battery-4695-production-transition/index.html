Why production readiness had to be rechecked

The product and equipment SAT had been completed before the project entered production readiness in February 2025. Development and engineering should have overlapped during the transition, but the previous development owners had left. The engineering team therefore had to recover the equipment and validate the product flow again.

Operators moved individual units manually. No machine ran continuously from Start to completion, temporary recipes had accumulated around misaligned equipment, and new-product geometry, reflectivity, and turn count were not fully reflected in X-ray and AI-vision inspection.

The work followed this sequence:

Confirm process purpose
→ restore equipment geometry, jigs, and alignment
→ reset process conditions
→ define inspection and measurement
→ retest with actual cells
→ decide limited production
→ transfer standards and training

Case 1 — Recovery of three laser processes

Common decision sequence

  1. Restored jig contact, alignment angle, and equipment geometry.
  2. Separated temporary recipes fitted to abnormal machine geometry.
  3. Reset process conditions on the restored baseline.
  4. Checked joining through sectioning, etching, microscopy, cell teardown, and tensile testing.
  5. Treated beam entry as a structure-specific internal-damage risk.
  6. Retested auto cycles and actual cells.
EquipmentInitial problemDirect action and validationDecision
Collector Laser WeldingJ/R bare-area-to-collector variation and no auto cycleReset geometry, alignment, jig contact, and conditions; section, microscopy, and teardownRecovered and applied
Taping & Can Insert & Can Collector WeldingUnstable positive-collector weld area, penetration, tensile result, and appearanceEnlarged effective weld area; five etched sections and five tensile/fracture checksConditions reset and applied
Beading & Collector WeldingNegative-collector position, alignment, and detection failuresAdjusted detection angle and weld position; checked five initial samples and two lotsRecovered and applied

Internal layer counts, diameters, gaps, and recipes are excluded. The public result is the restored equipment baseline and product evidence from sections, teardown, and tensile testing.

Case 2 — Resetting X-ray and AI-vision inspection boundaries

X-ray

The new product changed turn count, material, and internal geometry, but the previous Count and ROI settings remained. An engineering team member performed the settings, cell teardown, sectioning, and microscopy. I set the trial direction, decided whether the product was actually abnormal, reviewed the retest, and coordinated approval.

X-ray output was compared with teardown and section results, then automatic inspection was applied for turn count, electrode overlap, and overhang.

The YOLO models created for the SAT product could not be reused in several new-product zones. There was no internal modeling owner, confidential product and laser geometry could not be transferred externally, and the vision supplier did not provide a training guide, model source, or incremental-training function. The response was therefore to separate usable automatic inspection from controlled manual inspection rather than claim model retraining.

A physical master jig was requested but not approved. A product made with the previous pattern was controlled as a reference sample, with position and limit-sample rules documented.

Passing two lots and the final 50 cells confirmed the product and inspection procedure. It is not an accuracy result for all five AI-vision models.

Case 3 — Temporary controls and design-modification gates

These three machines were not fully recovered during my employment. Controlled temporary actions supported the production schedule, while component, drive-system, and software changes remained design gates.

E/L Filling decision

The spare O-ring size was measured and checked against the equipment vendor, domestic catalogues, and specialist suppliers. Supplier confirmation and acidic/alkaline exposure tests indicated that the supplied component did not support the required chemical-resistance condition. No external material certification was performed, so the public description does not identify the material definitively.

Applying the required material would also have required a size and hopper-to-chamber joint change. The punch and modification request were rejected, so production used repeated spare replacement and operator leak checks.

Crimping & Sizing decision

HMI torque stayed near its maximum range and one press did not reach target height. Pressure-sensitive sheets were checked twice by me, maintenance, and an engineering team member and showed that the upper and lower load centers did not coincide.

The permanent drive and load-transfer redesign remained outside the executed scope. Grease quantity and motion speed were adjusted, while total height, inner diameter, crimp appearance, airtightness, leakage, drop, impact, and vent performance were checked for limited operation.

Marking decision

The new QR pattern could not be registered through MES. Operators manually entered lot and QR data, ran marking, checked it visually and with a scanner, then entered the result into MES.
