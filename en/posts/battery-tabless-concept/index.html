Project background and objective

The tabless concept replaced the conventional lead tab with a broad-area electrical connection between the electrode and collector. The challenge was to produce a functioning 2170 A sample quickly without dedicated tabless equipment or dedicated jigs.

The existing 4695 equipment, jig arrangement, work conditions, and process sequence were temporarily reconfigured for tabless sample builds. Broad-area laser welding also introduced the risk of beam entry into the jelly roll and separator, while manual assembly could create alignment damage and short circuits.

I owned the early manufacturing process and hands-on sample work: process conditions, temporary 3D-printed jigs, laser welding, assembly, sectioning and teardown, Hi-Pot, and functional evaluation. The development team owned structural review, product requirements, and development documentation.

Early manufacturing validation flow

Build path. Jelly-roll fixing, collector alignment, laser welding, can insertion, and downstream work were connected into one temporary manufacturing route.

Welding and safety. Weld appearance alone was not used for disposition. Condition samples were sectioned and torn down to check internal beam entry and damage to the jelly roll and separator. Hi-Pot and short checks were applied before functional evaluation.

Exploratory builds. About 100 samples were built while weld, alignment, insertion, internal-damage, and insulation failures were separated by process stage.

Functional evaluation. Five samples meeting the safety and assembly criteria proceeded through IR, formation, charge, capacity, and same-condition life comparison, confirming the electrical function of the tabless structure.

1. Temporary manufacturing route without dedicated equipment

Temporary 3D-printed jigs

Without a dedicated jig, manually positioning the jelly roll and collector could shift the joint location and damage the jelly roll or collector during can insertion.

Temporary 3D-printed jigs were configured to hold the jelly roll and define the collector reference position. Holding condition and collector position were compared by jig, and samples were screened through alignment and assembly-damage checks before progressing.

Mixed equipment and manual work on the 4695 line

The product reference position and jig fixation were adapted so the existing 4695 laser equipment could perform the weld. Can insertion and assembly steps outside the equipment capability were connected through a defined manual sequence.

Jelly-roll fixing
→ Collector alignment
→ Laser welding
→ Sectioning and teardown
→ Can insertion and assembly
→ Hi-Pot and short checks
→ Functional evaluation

This route connected welding, assembly, and inspection into an A-sample manufacturing flow despite the absence of dedicated tabless equipment.

2. Internal laser-entry and short-circuit validation

A broad tabless joint can look acceptable externally while allowing laser energy to enter the cell or damage the separator. Weld appearance was therefore not the sole acceptance basis.

  • Sectioned welds by condition to review the joint and beam path
  • Tore down cells to inspect the jelly roll and separator for internal damage
  • Linked Hi-Pot and short results to the corresponding weld sample
  • Advanced only samples that passed the safety checks into formation and functional evaluation

Sectioning and teardown results were used to adjust jig alignment, holding position, laser path, and welding conditions. This established the welding and safety verification sequence for the A-sample build.

3. About 100 exploratory builds and failure-mode separation

At the A-sample stage, with incomplete equipment and jigs, the first priority was not to find one perfect condition immediately but to identify exactly where each build failed.

  • Laser-weld defect: Rechecked joint geometry, condition, and jig alignment
  • Internal beam entry: Used sectioning and teardown to inspect jelly-roll and separator damage
  • Hi-Pot or short failure: Connected insulation results to the welded sample
  • Collector misalignment: Adjusted jig references and manual positioning
  • Assembly damage: Compared the jelly roll and collector before and after can insertion
  • Jelly-roll fixation failure: Revised temporary-jig support points and work sequence

Failure modes from about 100 exploratory builds were recorded by process stage and fed into the next build condition. The resulting evidence became design input for dedicated jigs, laser work position, and inspection methods.

4. Five functional samples and electrical evaluation

Sequential failure-mode screening produced five samples suitable for functional evaluation. Each sample entered electrical testing only after assembly and insulation safety checks.

  • Hi-Pot and short checks
  • IR measurement
  • Formation
  • Charge and capacity evaluation
  • Life comparison against lead-tab cells under the same materials and charge/discharge conditions

The five samples confirmed that the tabless structure could be assembled and could express normal electrical function. The comparison with the lead-tab structure also showed a favorable direction in IR and life behavior.

Final result and pilot design inputs

Results confirmed at the A-sample stage

  • Created a manufacturing route using existing 4695 equipment and temporary 3D-printed jigs without dedicated tabless equipment
  • Separated weld, beam-entry, short, alignment, and assembly-damage failure modes across about 100 exploratory builds
  • Secured five functional samples after sectioning, teardown, and Hi-Pot safety checks
  • Confirmed structural and electrical function through IR, formation, charge, capacity, and same-condition life comparison
  • Completed early manufacturing validation of the next-generation 2170 tabless A-sample structure

Design inputs for the next pilot stage

  • Dedicated jigs that repeatedly reproduce jelly-roll fixing and collector alignment
  • Laser equipment, work position, and internal-damage inspection suited to the tabless joint geometry
  • Removal of manual dependency in can insertion and assembly
  • Unit-process repeatability, inspection reproducibility, and productivity validation

The temporary manufacturing route and recorded failure modes were organized into requirements for dedicated jigs, equipment, and pilot inspection.
