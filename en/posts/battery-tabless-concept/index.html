Project background and manufacturing question

The tabless concept replaced the conventional lead tab with a broad-area connection between the electrode and collector. There was no dedicated equipment or jig, so a build path had to be created with existing 4695 equipment and temporary jigs while checking internal laser-beam entry and short-circuit risk.

The A-sample question was specific: before discussing repeat production, could the available equipment produce a functioning tabless sample? I directly handled process conditions, temporary 3D-printed jigs, sample builds, welding, assembly, inspection, and evaluation. The development team owned structural review, product requirements, and development documentation.

Case 01. Jelly-roll and collector holding with temporary jigs

01

Temporary build path

J/R and collector fixing with temporary jigs

A minimum path connecting J/R fixing, collector alignment, and laser work was created without dedicated equipment.

Symptom and success criterion

No dedicated jig existed to hold the jelly roll and collector. Manual positioning could create collector misalignment, assembly damage, and can-insertion failures at the same time. The success criterion was not one isolated motion, but a sample route that could pass welding and assembly and reach functional testing.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared jelly-roll holding and collector position across temporary 3D-printed jigs
  • Checked alignment and appearance before and after laser welding
  • Checked jelly-roll and collector damage during can insertion and downstream assembly
  • Recorded work sequence and failure location by sample

Local 4M+2E decision

MachineNo dedicated jigHolding condition by temporary 3D-printed jigPrimary manufacturing constraintCompare temporary jigs and derive the dedicated-jig requirement
MaterialExisting cell materialsSame materials retained to isolate the structural changeRemoved as primary causeKeep the existing materials
MethodJelly-roll holding, collector alignment, and can-insertion sequenceAssembly and damage result by stagePrimary manufacturing routeApply fixed holding points, alignment, and insertion order
MeasurementPosition, appearance, assembly state, and teardownSame checks before advancing the sampleSelection basisScreen each sample under the same gate before advancing
EnvironmentWork environmentNo direct link to failure locationRemoved as primary causeRetain the existing work environment

Action and retest

Temporary jigs were configured for jelly-roll holding, collector alignment, and laser welding. Can insertion and part of the assembly remained manual, and only samples that passed position and damage checks were advanced.

Result and next gate

A minimum manufacturing route reaching functional evaluation was established. Dedicated jigs and equipment and removal of operator dependency remained the next pilot gates for repeatability.

Case 02. Internal laser entry and short risk

02

Welding and safety validation

Internal laser entry and short risk

Weld appearance was supplemented with sectioning, teardown, and Hi-Pot checks for internal beam entry and short risk.

Symptom and success criterion

The main failure modes were laser-weld defects, internal beam entry, and Hi-Pot or short failures. A visually joined weld could still contain internal damage, so the section and internal condition had to be checked before a sample entered functional evaluation.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared weld appearance and cross-section by condition
  • Tore down cells to check jelly-roll and separator damage
  • Linked Hi-Pot and short results to the welded sample
  • Advanced only samples that passed the safety checks

Action and retest

Weld conditions were checked through sectioning and teardown before functional samples were built. Hi-Pot and short checks were then linked so that only samples without internal beam or insulation problems reached functional evaluation.

Result and next gate

The A-sample safety and weld-verification sequence was defined. The next stage requires the same internal checks on dedicated jigs and repeat samples.

Case 03. Selection and evaluation of five functional samples

03

Functional evaluation

Five functional samples

Failure modes were separated across about 100 exploratory builds, leaving five samples for electrical and performance-direction checks.

Symptom and success criterion

Samples built on existing 4695 equipment and temporary jigs contained mixed welding, alignment, insertion, and insulation failures. The success criterion was to separate these failure modes first and obtain samples that could proceed through Hi-Pot, IR, formation, charging, capacity, and comparison testing.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Separated weld, internal beam, short, assembly-damage, alignment, and insertion failures across about 100 exploratory builds
  • Checked Hi-Pot, IR, and formation on functional candidates
  • Checked charging, capacity, and life under the same comparison conditions
  • Compared against lead-tab cells using the same materials and charge/discharge conditions

Action and retest

Failure modes were removed in sequence across about 100 exploratory builds, resulting in five samples suitable for functional evaluation. All five passed Hi-Pot, IR, formation, charging, capacity, and the same-condition life comparison.

Result and next gate

Structural build feasibility and functional direction were confirmed. The five samples were not converted into a manufacturing yield; dedicated jigs and equipment, unit-process repeatability, internal laser inspection, and productivity moved to the next pilot gate.

Result and next gates

The A-sample stage established a tabless build path and functioning samples. It was not stopped because the structure was technically impossible; the A-sample objective of structural build feasibility was completed, and follow-on investment did not proceed.

A later stage would need dedicated jigs and equipment, unit-process repeatability, internal laser-entry inspection, reduced operator dependency, productivity, and pilot validation.

Validation boundary

The public result is about 100 exploratory builds, five functional samples, and A-sample structural build feasibility. The five samples are not reported as production yield. Unit-process repeatability, pilot, production preparation, and exact IR or life-improvement figures are outside the completed scope.
