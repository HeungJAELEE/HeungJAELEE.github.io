A-sample concept validation

Next-Generation 2170 Tabless A-Sample Early Manufacturing Validation (PoC)

Created a manufacturing route with existing 4695 equipment and temporary jigs, separated failure modes across about 100 trials, and completed cell evaluation on five functional samples

Project period
Jan 2025 · next-generation 2170 tabless A-sample early manufacturing validation
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · new tabless A sample using existing 4695 equipment
Scope
Early manufacturing process design, temporary 3D-printed jigs, laser and assembly conditions, sectioning and teardown, Hi-Pot and short checks, and cell evaluation

Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
With no dedicated tabless equipment or jig, existing 4695 equipment and manual work had to produce an A sample while controlling internal laser entry, shorts, alignment, and assembly damage.
Key role
I directly handled the early manufacturing route, temporary 3D-printed jigs, laser and assembly conditions, sectioning, teardown, Hi-Pot and short checks, and cell evaluation.
Key result
Failure modes were separated across about 100 trials, and cell evaluation on five functional samples confirmed structural build and functional feasibility.
Exploratory build scope About 100 builds
Functional samples 5 samples

Project overview

  • Development task: Build a 2170 A sample using a tabless structure that connects the electrode and collector over a broad area instead of a conventional lead tab
  • Manufacturing constraint: No dedicated tabless equipment or jig; the route had to combine existing 4695 equipment with controlled manual work
  • Critical risks: Internal laser entry, separator damage, collector misalignment, jelly-roll damage during can insertion, and shorts
  • Direct scope: Early process design, temporary 3D-printed jigs, laser-welding and assembly conditions, sectioning and teardown, Hi-Pot and short checks, and cell evaluation
  • Team interface: Linked manufacturing findings with the development team’s structural review and product requirements

1. Temporary manufacturing route without dedicated equipment

Temporary 3D-printed jigs

  • Defined jelly-roll holding points and the collector reference position
  • Compared holding condition and alignment by jig
  • Checked alignment before and after laser welding and damage during can insertion
  • Set the screening basis for samples progressing to the next operation

Existing 4695 equipment plus controlled manual work

  • Reset the product reference position and jig-fixation method for the tabless sample
  • Connected can insertion and assembly steps outside the equipment capability through a defined manual sequence
  • Built one sample route from welding through safety checks and cell evaluation
Jelly-roll fixing
→ Collector alignment
→ Laser welding
→ Sectioning and teardown
→ Can insertion and assembly
→ Hi-Pot and short checks
→ Cell evaluation

2. Preventing internal laser entry and shorts

Destructive checks — sectioning and teardown

  • Sectioned welds by condition to inspect the joint and laser path
  • Tore down cells to check the jelly roll and separator for internal damage
  • Reset jig alignment, holding position, laser path, and welding conditions from the findings

Non-destructive checks — insulation gate

  • Applied Hi-Pot and short checks to every assembled sample
  • Advanced only samples meeting insulation and assembly-safety criteria to cell evaluation
  • Linked each weld sample with its insulation result to locate the failing operation

3. About 100 trials and failure-mode separation

Failure location was separated by process stage across about 100 exploratory builds, and the findings were fed directly into the next build condition.

  • Laser-weld defect: Rechecked joint geometry, condition, and jig alignment
  • Internal laser entry: Used sectioning and teardown to inspect jelly-roll and separator damage
  • Hi-Pot or short failure: Linked the insulation result to the weld sample
  • Collector misalignment: Revised jig references and manual positioning
  • Can-insertion damage: Compared the jelly roll and collector before and after insertion
  • Jelly-roll fixation failure: Reconfigured jig support points and fixation order

Trial records were organized as the basis for the dedicated jig, laser work position, assembly method, and inspection method required at the pilot stage.

4. Cell evaluation of five functional samples

Five functional samples meeting the safety and assembly criteria proceeded to cell evaluation.

  • Hi-Pot and short checks
  • IR
  • Formation
  • Charge and capacity
  • Life comparison with lead-tab cells under the same materials and charge/discharge conditions

The evaluation confirmed normal assembly and electrical function of the tabless structure. Comparison with the lead-tab structure also showed a favorable direction in IR and life behavior.

Final results

  • Created a tabless A-sample manufacturing route using existing 4695 equipment and temporary 3D-printed jigs
  • Separated weld, laser-entry, short, alignment, and assembly-damage failure modes across about 100 trials
  • Secured five functional samples after sectioning, teardown, and Hi-Pot safety checks
  • Confirmed cell feasibility through IR, formation, charge, capacity, and life comparison
  • Completed the next-generation 2170 tabless A-sample early manufacturing PoC

Follow-on items for pilot design

  • Equipment and jigs: Dedicated jig to suppress jelly-roll and collector alignment variation
  • Laser: Work position, optical conditions, and internal-damage inspection suited to the tabless joint
  • Assembly: Remove manual can insertion and define an automated assembly route
  • Inspection: Standardize internal-damage inspection and review feasible automatic-inspection methods
  • Pilot validation: Confirm unit-process repeatability, inspection reproducibility, and productivity

Manufacturing constraints and failure modes identified during the A-sample trials were transferred as follow-on items for pilot review.

Related projects

  • 2170 assembly-line good-yield stabilization: established the stable 2170 assembly basis that preceded the new-structure work
