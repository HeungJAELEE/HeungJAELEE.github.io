Manufacturing Concept Validation for a 2170 Tabless Cell

A concept case that manually built five samples with 4695 equipment and temporary jigs, then checked charge, formation, IR, and life potential

Project period
2170 Tabless concept building and basic performance checks during Kumyang employment
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · manufacturability review of a new cell structure
Scope
Owned process conditions, temporary jigs, sample building, welding, assembly, inspection, and evaluation; development owned structure design and documents

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
A collector-based Tabless structure differed from the lead-tab process and could not be repeated without dedicated jigs and inspection methods.
What I owned
I built temporary jigs for 4695 equipment and handled process conditions, manual assembly, welding, inspection, and performance evaluation.
Verified result
Five working samples charged and formed normally; a limited same-material, same-cycle comparison showed lower IR and improved life.
Working samples 5

The goal was a working sample, not production readiness

A conventional lead-tab cell carries current through a single tab, while a Tabless structure connects a collector across a wider electrode area. The expected electrical benefit also changes assembly, welding, and inspection, so the existing 2170 process could not be reused as-is.

No dedicated equipment or jigs were available. The goal was not repeat production or pilot validation, but to build a structurally different sample with the same core materials and confirm that it could operate. Development owned structural design and documents; I owned process conditions, jigs, sample building, welding, assembly, inspection, and performance evaluation.

Safety and process feasibility came first

The new structure made Laser-ingress and short-circuit risks harder to judge with existing inspection methods. Before cell performance, I checked whether each unit process could be completed and whether manual assembly could maintain safety and quality. Production speed and automation were intentionally left for a later stage.

Existing 4695 equipment and temporary jigs established feasibility

I used 4695 equipment and supplemented structure-specific interfaces with 3D-printed and temporary jigs. Steps that could not run automatically were assembled and welded manually. The process punch list recorded the dedicated jigs, equipment design, inspection methods, and repeatability work needed next.

Five samples showed the effect of the structure change

Five working samples were built. Charging and formation completed normally, and IR was lower than the target. In a comparison against lead-tab cells using the same materials and charge-discharge conditions, the Tabless structure also showed improved life.

This was a limited concept comparison. Five working samples do not establish repeat production, pilot readiness, or manufacturability at scale. Dedicated equipment and jigs, automated assembly and welding, short-circuit and safety inspection, measurement methods, and repeatability remain separate gates for the next stage.
