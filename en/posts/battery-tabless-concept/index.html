A-sample concept validation

Next-Generation 2170 Tabless A-Sample Early Manufacturing Validation (PoC)

Completed cell evaluation on five functional samples by designing a no-equipment/no-jig manufacturing route with DMADV, 4M, and Taguchi trials inside the IATF-based PROTO gate

Project period
Jan 2025 · next-generation 2170 tabless A-sample early manufacturing validation
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · new tabless A sample using existing 4695 equipment
Scope
IATF-based PROTO A-sample gate, DMADV and 4M risk analysis, Taguchi trial matrix, temporary 3D-printed jigs, laser/assembly, safety checks, and cell evaluation

Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
Without dedicated tabless equipment or jigs, existing 4695 equipment and manual work had to meet the safety and functional CTQs of a PROTO A sample.
Key role
I defined objectives and measurements through DMADV, then compared jig, laser, and assembly factors selected through 4M in a Taguchi trial matrix while directly building and evaluating samples.
Key result
Narrowed failure modes across about 100 trials and confirmed A-sample feasibility through Hi-Pot, IR, formation, capacity, and life evaluation on five functional samples.
Exploratory build scope About 100 builds
Functional samples 5 samples

Project overview

  • Development task: Build a 2170 A sample using a tabless structure that connects the electrode and collector over a broad area instead of a conventional lead tab
  • Quality process: Company development flow based on IATF 16949: PROTO (A Sample) → PILOT (B and C Samples) → MP (CCB.PO) → SOP (mass production)
  • Completed gate: PROTO A-sample manufacturing and cell evaluation completed
  • Manufacturing constraint: No dedicated tabless equipment or jig; the route had to combine existing 4695 laser equipment with controlled manual work
  • Critical CTQs: Joint condition, internal laser entry and separator damage, insulation and shorts, IR, formation, capacity, and life
  • Direct scope: Early process design, 4M risk analysis, Taguchi trial matrix, temporary 3D-printed jigs, laser and assembly conditions, sectioning, teardown, and cell evaluation
  • Final result: Compressed and separated failure modes across about 100 trials and confirmed build and functional feasibility through cell evaluation on five functional samples

Taguchi-based trial matrix

The roughly 100 builds were not random iteration. Jig, laser, and assembly factors selected through 4M were arranged into a Taguchi-style condition matrix, and each trial response narrowed the combination for the next build.

Factor groupConditions comparedMain responsesDesign decision
Jig and alignmentJelly-roll support points, collector references, and fixation methodAlignment variation, fixation failure, and insertion damageBuild a temporary jig that retained alignment and fixation

1. Temporary manufacturing-route design

Temporary 3D-printed jigs

  • Designed and applied temporary jigs that fixed jelly-roll support points and the collector reference
  • Compared fixation, alignment, and damage during can insertion by jig
  • Screened samples for the next operation using alignment before and after laser welding
  • Repeatedly revised support points, fixation, and work order from the trial results

Existing 4695 equipment plus controlled manual work

  • Reset the product reference position and jig-fixation method for the tabless sample
  • Connected can insertion and assembly steps outside the equipment capability through a defined manual sequence
  • Built a minimum manufacturing flow from welding through safety checks and cell evaluation
Jelly-roll fixing
→ Collector alignment
→ Laser welding
→ Sectioning and teardown
→ Can insertion and assembly
→ Hi-Pot and short checks
→ Cell evaluation

2. Preventing internal laser entry and shorts

Destructive checks

  • Sectioned welds by condition to inspect the joint cross-section and laser path
  • Tore down cells to check jelly-roll and separator damage
  • Fed the results back into jig alignment, fixation, laser path, and the next trial condition

Non-destructive checks

  • Applied Hi-Pot and short checks to assembled samples
  • Advanced only samples meeting insulation and assembly-safety criteria to cell evaluation
  • Linked each weld sample with its insulation result to separate the failing operation

3. About 100 trials and failure-mode reduction

  • Laser-weld defect: Redesigned joint geometry, condition, and jig alignment
  • Internal laser entry: Used sectioning and teardown to inspect jelly-roll and separator damage
  • Hi-Pot or short failure: Linked welding and assembly samples with insulation results
  • Collector misalignment: Revised jig references and fixation
  • Can-insertion damage: Compared the jelly roll and collector before and after insertion
  • Jelly-roll fixation failure: Reconfigured jig support points and fixation order

Failure modes were managed as process-specific responses to narrow the factor combination for the next trial. The resulting conditions and failure records became follow-on design items for the dedicated jig, laser work position, assembly route, and inspection method.

4. Cell evaluation of five functional samples

Five functional samples meeting the safety and assembly criteria proceeded to cell evaluation.

  • Hi-Pot and short checks
  • IR
  • Formation
  • Charge and capacity
  • Life comparison with lead-tab cells under the same materials and charge/discharge conditions

The evaluation confirmed normal assembly and electrical function of the tabless structure. Comparison under the same conditions also showed a favorable direction in IR and life behavior.

Final results

  • Completed the PROTO A-sample gate of the IATF 16949-based development-quality process
  • Structured the A-sample objective, CTQs, measurement, and verification through DMADV
  • Selected critical jig, laser, assembly, and inspection factors through a 4M risk map
  • Compressed condition combinations and failure modes across about 100 trials through a Taguchi-based trial matrix
  • Created a tabless manufacturing route using existing 4695 equipment and temporary 3D-printed jigs
  • Secured five functional samples after sectioning, teardown, and Hi-Pot safety checks
  • Confirmed A-sample build and functional feasibility through IR, formation, charge, capacity, and life comparison

Follow-on design items for PILOT B/C samples

  • Equipment and jigs: Precision dedicated jig to suppress jelly-roll and collector alignment variation
  • Laser: Work position, optical conditions, and internal-damage inspection suited to the tabless joint
  • Assembly: Remove manual can insertion and define an automated assembly route
  • Inspection: Standardize internal-damage inspection and evaluate automatic-inspection applicability
  • Pilot validation: Unit-process repeatability, inspection reproducibility, and productivity
