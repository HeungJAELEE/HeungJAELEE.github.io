Manufacturing case study

A-sample concept validation

Validating a 2170 Tabless A-Sample Build with Temporary Jigs

An exploratory A-sample case that used existing 4695 equipment and manual processes to secure functional samples and define the gates before repeat production

Project period
Jan 2025 · 2170 Tabless A-sample structure build and basic performance checks
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · manufacturability review of a new cell structure
Scope
Owned process conditions, temporary jigs, sample building, welding, assembly, inspection, and evaluation; development owned structure design and documents

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
A collector-based Tabless structure differed from the lead-tab process and could not be repeated without dedicated jigs and inspection methods.
What I owned
I built temporary jigs for 4695 equipment and handled process conditions, manual assembly, welding, inspection, and performance evaluation.
Verified result
After about 100 exploratory builds, five samples were suitable for functional checks; a limited same-material, same-cycle comparison indicated lower IR and improved life direction.
A-sample exploration scope About 100 exploratory builds · 5 functional samples

The A-sample goal was structural build feasibility

A conventional lead-tab cell carries current through one tab, while the Tabless structure connects a collector across a wider electrode area. The materials remained the same, and only the current-path structure changed so its electrical and life potential could be explored.

The January 2025 A-sample stage did not target pilot or production readiness. The first question was whether the structure could be built and function without dedicated equipment. Development owned structural design and related documents; I owned process conditions, temporary jigs, sample building, welding, assembly, inspection, and performance evaluation.

Manufacturing risks came before a complete 4M or DMAIC cycle

There was no stable process baseline, so a complete 4M table or DMAIC sequence would have overstated the stage. Instead, I grouped the risks that could prevent an A sample from being built and used them as gates across structure, equipment, methods, and inspection.

Structure · MaterialJ/R and collector damage and current path
Machine · JigNo dedicated equipment for positioning and holding
MethodManual can insertion and Laser Welding dependency
Inspection gatesBeam ingress, Hi-Pot, short, IR, and formation

The first risks reviewed were Laser Welding failure and internal beam ingress, followed by Hi-Pot and short failures, J/R and collector damage, alignment, and holding. Production speed was intentionally left until after basic build feasibility.

Existing equipment and temporary jigs were used to explore conditions

The build used 4695 equipment with temporary jigs for J/R positioning, collector alignment, and Laser Welding. The can was inserted manually. Steps that could not run automatically were performed one at a time while assembly and welding conditions were adjusted.

Review risksCheck damage, alignment, and beam ingress Build jigsHold the J/R and align the collector ExploreManual assembly and Laser Welding Select conditionsUse teardown and sectioning Check functionHi-Pot, IR, formation, and life

Teardown and sectioning were performed on earlier condition-selection samples, not the final functional samples. Those checks were used to review internal beam ingress and structural damage before choosing the next build conditions.

Exploratory builds produced samples suitable for functional checks

About 100 exploratory builds were used to secure five samples suitable for functional checks. These numbers are not a production-yield ratio. They describe the A-sample exploration needed to find build conditions with temporary jigs and manual processes.

The final five samples passed Hi-Pot, IR, formation, charge, capacity, and life comparison checks. Against lead-tab cells using the same materials and cycling conditions, the limited comparison indicated lower IR and improved life direction. Exact performance values are not published, and this small comparison is not presented as statistical proof.

Confirmed direction and remaining manufacturing gates were separated

The confirmed scope was structural build feasibility and a limited basic-performance comparison. Repeat production, pilot operation, automation, and production readiness were not validated.

The next stage would require dedicated jigs and equipment, automated assembly and welding, repeatable inspection methods, short-circuit and laser-safety validation, stable process conditions, and productivity gates. This was an A-sample concept validation that built the structure and defined the next manufacturing risks, not a production result.
