Translating Material and Structure Changes into 25P LFP Process Conditions

A new-cell case that traced electrolyte, Clad Tab, and inspection changes through internal sample gates, about 20,000-cell trial production, and customer qualification

Project period
Jan 2025 A sample · Feb 2025 B/C samples and trial production · Mar 2025 customer qualification
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · LFP power-cell development for an overseas customer request
Scope
Process-condition decisions, trials, sample building, semi-finished inspection, and standards documentation in joint development

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Material and structure changes in the LFP power cell made existing 2170 assembly and inspection settings unsuitable.
What I owned
Working with development, I separated change effects by process and owned assembly-wide sample building, inspection, condition decisions, and control-document revisions.
Verified result
The team advanced through internal A, B, and C samples, about 20,000-cell initial trial production, customer qualification, and CCB P.O approval. Commercial production did not proceed because of pricing.
Initial trial production About 20,000 cells
Crimping design decision Adjusted for equipment and life constraints

Existing 2170 conditions could not be reused as-is

25P began as an LFP power-cell request from an overseas customer. The electrolyte, winding turns, Clad Tab, embo geometry, cell height, and tubing differed from the existing product. These material and structure changes propagated from impregnation to X-Ray and Vision decisions, resistance welding, crimping and sizing, tubing, marking, and pre-charge.

Development defined the product requirements and structure. I owned assembly-wide condition trials, sample building and inspection, condition decisions, and control-document revisions. Manufacturing supported sample builds, maintenance supported equipment settings, and quality participated in measurement and disposition.

Material and structure changes were mapped to affected processes

I did not force all four 4M categories into a generic table. The confirmed starting point was material change, so the work traced how it affected machines and methods process by process.

Owners were assigned by process, and unit-equipment conditions were tested separately. Samples near the center and both ends of the process variation were evaluated together. Borderline conditions were retested, and only passing conditions were connected to the next process and development stage.

Impregnation and inspection decisions were checked against the product

Matching the designed fill quantity was not enough for the LFP electrolyte. Teardown showed an initial unimpregnated width of about 6 mm. Vacuum pressure and hold time, pressure and hold time, and repeat count were tested separately, reducing the observed width to about 1.5 mm. Teardown appearance, mass, capacity, IR, and life were then checked before the condition was moved into the checklist, QCP, and internal process standard.

Changes in winding turns also made the X-Ray count and inspection region unsuitable. Cells judged NG were torn down, sectioned, and checked by microscope to separate real winding defects from inspection misclassification. Count, threshold, and ROI were then adjusted. Vision settings were also updated for the Clad Tab geometry and reflectivity and monitored in place across four production batches.

Clad Tab and height-process conditions were established again

The Clad Tab material and embo geometry changes were mandatory, so the material itself could not be removed as a factor. Pressure, output, time, and electrode diameter were tested, and weld quality was compared through nugget size, tensile force, and fracture mode. The selected conditions were moved into the resistance-welding and sample-inspection standards.

The existing beading head and knife conditions remained. CCR height and crimping/sizing settings were adjusted for the new cell height. Hi-Pot, total height, sealing, impact-leak, and vent tests were used for confirmation. The final condition balanced target geometry with 3-jaw investment and component-life constraints.

Internal sample gates led to about 20,000-cell trial production

Development began with an A sample in January 2025, followed by B and C samples and about 20,000-cell initial trial production in February. A, B, and C were internal company stages, not universal IATF stages. Only sample combinations that passed semi-finished inspection gates across assembly advanced.

An exact trial-production yield recalled during interview was excluded because the source record could not be checked. The public result is limited to the roughly 20,000-cell trial-production scope, customer delivery, and completed development gates.

Customer qualification was completed, but commercial production did not proceed

Customer qualification and CCB P.O approval were completed in March 2025. The process standard, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection standards were also prepared for production transfer. These were cross-functional results across development, technology, quality, maintenance, and manufacturing.

The technical work and customer approval were completed, but commercial production did not proceed because of pricing. This case demonstrates turning new-cell material and structure differences into process conditions and inspection gates through trial production and customer qualification.
