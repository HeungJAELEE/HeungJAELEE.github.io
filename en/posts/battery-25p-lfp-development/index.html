Manufacturing project summary

The overseas-customer LFP power cell had substantial material and structure changes, so legacy impregnation, welding, and inspection conditions could not be reused directly.

Development background and objective

An overseas customer requested a new LFP power cell whose materials and structure differed from the existing 2170 cell. Electrolyte, clad tab, turn count, and can height changed together, so adjusting a single process could not secure the final capacity, IR, and life requirements. I connected process-condition setting, sample builds, inspection, evaluation, and documentation across the A, B, and C sample stages and trial production.

Development stages and timeline

  1. Jan 2025A SampleBuilt the internal prototype and defined material/structure changes plus capacity, IR, and life CTQs.
  2. Feb 2025B SampleRechecked impregnation, welding, inspection, and downstream conditions in the pilot type.
  3. Feb 2025C Sample and trial productionChecked process and inspection flow in pre-MP and about 20,000-cell trial production.
  4. Mar 2025Customer qualification and production approvalQualification and production approval were completed, but commercial production did not proceed because of pricing.

Development gates and process-condition linkage

  1. Listed customer requirements and changes from the existing cell.
  2. Connected process checks to the manufacturing CTQs of capacity, IR, and life.
  3. Used A Sample to establish build feasibility and basic inspection criteria.
  4. Used B Sample to review pilot conditions and representative defect causes.
  5. Used C Sample and about 20,000-cell trial production to check process links and inspection flow.
  6. Connected reviewed process and inspection documents plus stage results to customer qualification.

Why the work is divided into three representative cases

The representative cases use different decision methods. Electrolyte impregnation depended on recipe trials and teardown measurement, clad-tab welding on material, alignment, electrodes, and joint verification, and X-ray and vision on separating actual abnormalities from false inspection decisions.

01

Impregnation check

Electrolyte impregnation conditions

Actual impregnation was checked by teardown instead of relying on the design fill quantity.

Change and retest

  • The LFP electrolyte required different impregnation conditions from the existing cell.
  • Vacuum pressure and hold time, pressurization and pressure-hold time, and repeat count were reset.
  • The unimpregnated width was measured directly after teardown, while weight, capacity, IR, and life were checked together.

Result and documentation

  • The measured unimpregnated width fell from about 6 mm to 1.5 mm: a 4.5 mm and about 75% relative reduction.
  • Development approved the related cause analysis.
  • The recipe checklist, QCP, and internal standard were revised.
02

Welding-condition check

Clad Tab resistance welding

Welding conditions and inspection criteria were rebuilt for the multilayer clad tab and embo changes.

Change and retest

  • Two-layer Ni-Cu or three-layer Ni-Cu-Ni clad-tab structures, embo geometry, and electrode diameter were reviewed.
  • Material, alignment, electrode condition, pressure, electrical conditions, and time were rechecked.
  • Pressure, electrical conditions, time, and electrode diameter were adjusted.
  • Nugget size, tensile force, and fracture mode were used to verify the weld.

Result and documentation

  • Welding conditions and inspection criteria were revised.
03

Inspection-condition check

X-ray and vision decision conditions

Actual product abnormalities were separated from inspection-condition effects through teardown, sectioning, and production monitoring.

X-ray

  • Count and Center/Middle/Edge ROI ranges were rechecked for the changed turn count.
  • The threshold was adjusted for the material change.
  • Suspected cells were disassembled and sectioned, then reviewed visually and microscopically.
  • Development and engineering owners approved the result.

Vision

  • Only vision settings were adjusted for the changed embo geometry and reflectivity.
  • Four production batches were monitored on site for false decisions and repeat behavior.
