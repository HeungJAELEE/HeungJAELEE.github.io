TECHNICAL CASE STUDY

LFP Power Cell Development

An NPI case conducted through the company’s IATF 16949-based PROTO, PILOT, and MP quality gates: customer CTQs were translated into process requirements through DMADV, critical processes were optimized through 4M risk analysis and Taguchi orthogonal-array condition matrices, and the work advanced through about 20,000-cell trial production, customer qualification, and CCB.PO technical approval

Aug 3, 2026 · 6 minutes · Last reviewed Aug 9, 2026

  • IATF 16949
  • DMADV
  • Taguchi DOE
  • LFP
  • NPI

Project overview

  • Development task: Develop a 25P LFP power cell on the existing 2170 cylindrical assembly line with changes in electrolyte, clad tabs, turn count, and can geometry
  • Quality process: Company development flow based on IATF 16949: PROTO (A Sample) → PILOT (B and C Samples) → MP (CCB.PO) → SOP (mass production)
  • Customer CTQs: Capacity, IR, and cycle life
  • Direct scope: Change and CTQ definition, 4M risk analysis, Taguchi condition matrices, sample builds, process decisions, trial production, and customer qualification
  • Completed gate: PROTO, PILOT, and MP technical approval completed; SOP remained the subsequent production-start gate
  • Final result: About 20,000-cell trial production, customer qualification and CCB.PO approval, plus completed process, inspection, and production-handoff documents

IATF 16949-based development and production-handoff gates

The A, B, and C samples were not merely chronological builds. Each stage had a distinct quality decision and entry condition for the next gate. The 25P project completed PROTO and PILOT and reached MP technical approval through CCB.PO.

Measurement and environment checks

  • Measurement: Unimpregnated width and weight, nugget/tensile/fracture, X-ray/vision, total height/sealing, capacity/IR/life · Teardown, sectioning, microscopy, and cell evaluation · Measure and Verify · Advance only combinations meeting the response criteria together

Taguchi DOE-based condition optimization

Rather than testing every possible full-factorial combination, the critical factors and levels identified through 4M were organized into Taguchi orthogonal-array condition matrices. Only combinations reproduced through confirmation runs advanced to the next sample and trial-production stages.

Process Critical design factors Main responses Selection logic
LFP impregnation Vacuum pressure, vacuum hold, pressurization, pressure hold, and repetition count Unimpregnated width, fill weight, capacity, IR, and life Reduce the unimpregnated area while maintaining cell CTQs
Clad-tab resistance welding Electrode diameter, head alignment and pressure, electrical settings, and application time Nugget, tensile force, and fracture mode Stable joint strength and fracture location
Beading, CCR, crimping, and sizing Assembly height, pressure amount, and sizing conditions Total height, Hi-Pot, airtightness/leakage, drop/impact, and vent Meet geometry, sealing, and safety checks together
X-ray and vision Count/ROI, product position, illumination, focus, and inspection region Agreement between actual teardown and inspection disposition Reduce false rejection without allowing actual defects to escape

Core process design and confirmation

1. LFP electrolyte impregnation

  • Confirmed insufficient electrolyte and an unimpregnated area under the legacy fill quantity and sequence
  • Set vacuum pressure, hold time, pressurization, pressure-hold time, and repetitions as DOE factors
  • Directly measured teardown width and linked it with weight, capacity, IR, and life
  • Reduced the directly measured unimpregnated width from about 6 mm to about 1.5 mm
  • Transferred the selected conditions into equipment/recipe checklists, QCP, and the work standard

2. Multilayer clad-tab resistance welding

  • Redesigned the joining condition for two-layer Ni-Cu, three-layer Ni-Cu-Ni, and the new embo geometry
  • Corrected the electrode, head alignment, and pressure state before comparing electrical settings and application time
  • Evaluated condition groups through nugget size, tensile force, and fracture mode
  • Applied the stable combination to B/C samples and trial production
  • Transferred the welding condition and joint-inspection criteria into field documents

3. Changed can height and sealing processes

  • Retained the beading knife and head geometry while resetting CCR height and crimping/sizing height and pressure
  • Adjusted the sizing condition by approximately 0.3 mm
  • Connected post-beading Hi-Pot and height with post-crimp total height, airtightness, leakage, drop/impact, and vent results
  • Transferred the combination meeting geometry, sealing, and safety checks into the process standard, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection criteria

4. X-ray and vision disposition

  • X-ray: correlated rejected-cell teardown and sectioning with actual winding condition and Count/ROI
  • Vision: reset product position, illumination, focus, and inspection region for the changed embo and clad-tab reflectivity
  • Monitored the revised settings on site across four production batches
  • Applied only inspection settings that agreed with the actual internal structure

Final results

  • Operated the IATF 16949-based PROTO → PILOT → MP → SOP development-quality gates
  • Translated customer CTQs into process factors, responses, and sample acceptance through DMADV
  • Optimized impregnation, welding, sealing, and inspection through 4M risk analysis and Taguchi DOE
  • Completed A/B/C samples and about 20,000-cell trial production
  • Obtained customer qualification and CCB.PO technical approval
  • Revised and handed off work standards, QCP, P-FMEA, checklists, and inspection criteria for the MP gate
