25P LFP Power Cell Development, Trial Production, and Qualification

A new-cell case that reset assembly conditions for material and structure changes, then completed about 20,000-cell initial trial production and customer qualification

Project period
From development kickoff through initial trial production and customer qualification during Kumyang employment
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · LFP power-cell development for an overseas customer request
Scope
Process-condition decisions, trials, sample building, semi-finished inspection, and standards documentation in joint development

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Material and structure changes in the LFP power cell made existing 2170 assembly and inspection settings unsuitable.
What I owned
Working with development, I split trials by process, built and inspected samples, documented conditions, and advanced only those that passed each gate.

Existing 2170 settings could not build the new cell

25P began as an LFP power-cell request from an overseas customer. Changes to electrolyte, winding turns, Clad Tab, embo geometry, and cell height meant that existing 2170 settings generated defects in downstream processes. Cell capacity, IR, and life mattered, but every semi-finished part also had to pass the next process gate.

The changes affected impregnation, X-Ray decisions, resistance welding, Vision, crimping and beading, tubing, and marking. Development defined the product requirements and structure; I owned assembly-wide condition trials, sample building, semi-finished inspection, and control documents.

Variables were separated by process, then reconnected

When material and structure change together, one completed-cell test cannot show which process caused the outcome. Owners were assigned by process and each unit condition was tested separately. Each stage used roughly seven to ten condition samples, including samples near the process center and both specification boundaries.

Borderline results were retested, and equipment, tooling life, and repeatability were considered together. For crimping, the final design decision balanced the target geometry against 3-jaw investment and component-life constraints rather than publishing a target value alone.

Clad Tab required a new welding window

The LFP power cell required a new Clad Tab structure. The material change was mandatory, and the embo geometry also changed, so alignment, electrode, pressure, output, and time all required review. Pressure, output, time, and electrode diameter became the controllable trial factors.

Resistance welding was not judged from appearance alone. Nugget size, tensile force, and fracture mode were compared. The confirmed window was moved into the welding conditions and sample-inspection standard.

The work reached initial trial production and customer qualification

Conditions that passed unit-process gates were connected for pilot and trial production. About 20,000 cells were produced in the initial run, delivered to the customer, and qualified. This was a joint result across development, technology, quality, maintenance, and manufacturing.

My scope covered assembly-wide condition decisions and trials, sample building and inspection, and revision of process standards. Technical development and customer qualification were complete, but follow-on production did not proceed because of pricing and commercial considerations. The case therefore demonstrates translation of new materials and structure into manufacturable conditions through trial production and qualification, not a mass-production result.
