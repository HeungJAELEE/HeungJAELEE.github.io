Project background and objective

An overseas customer requested a 25P LFP power cell to be developed on the existing 2170 cylindrical assembly line. The electrolyte, turn count, multilayer clad tab, embo geometry, and can height all changed together. Reusing the legacy conditions could therefore create linked failures in impregnation, resistance welding, X-ray and vision disposition, and downstream sealing dimensions.

As the lead process-development engineer in the engineering team, I owned project kickoff, CTQ and change definition, process-condition decisions, sample builds, inspection and evaluation, and stage decisions. Manufacturing supported sample and trial production, maintenance supported equipment settings, quality supported measurement and disposition, and development approved product requirements and stage results.

The objective was to establish process conditions that met the product CTQs—capacity, IR, and cycle life—and connect the work from A-sample build feasibility through B-sample optimization, C-sample trial production, and customer qualification.

NPI development flow

A Sample confirmed that the changed materials and structure could be assembled on the existing equipment. Full-process build feasibility and basic in-process and electrical checks determined whether the project moved forward.

B Sample compared the critical conditions for impregnation, resistance welding, X-ray, vision, and sealing. Conditions were changed in controlled groups, and only those supported by teardown, joint, and inspection evidence were retained.

C Sample and trial production connected the selected conditions through upstream and downstream processes and checked the production and inspection flow across about 20,000 cells. Process disposition was linked with cell-performance evaluation rather than treated as a separate activity.

Customer qualification and CCB P.O used the trial-production and performance evidence to complete customer review and obtain technical approval for shipment-ready production conditions.

Core process-condition development

1. LFP electrolyte impregnation

Problem. The legacy design fill quantity and sequence produced insufficient electrolyte, and teardown showed an unimpregnated area.

Critical variables. The LFP electrolyte’s impregnation behavior was evaluated through vacuum pressure, vacuum hold time, pressurization, pressure-hold time, and repeat count. Equipment vacuum and pressure actions were checked for repeatable execution.

Verification. Cells built under the same condition were torn down for direct width measurement. Fill weight, capacity, IR, and life evaluation were connected to the teardown result so that visual improvement alone did not determine the decision.

Result. Direct teardown measurement reduced the unimpregnated width from about 6 mm to about 1.5 mm. The selected conditions were transferred into the equipment/recipe checklist, QCP, and work standard.

2. Multilayer clad-tab resistance welding

Problem. Two-layer Ni-Cu or three-layer Ni-Cu-Ni clad tabs and the new embo geometry had different joining behavior from the existing tab, so the legacy welding condition did not provide a stable joint.

Critical variables. Electrode diameter, head alignment, and pressure state were corrected first. Electrical settings and application time were then compared for the multilayer material, separating equipment state and material geometry from the process setting.

Verification. Nugget size, tensile force, and fracture mode were checked under the same criteria. Joint strength and fracture location—not surface appearance alone—were used to select the condition.

Result. Resistance-welding conditions and joint-inspection criteria for the multilayer clad tab were established and transferred into the next sample stage and trial production.

3. Sealing-process reset for the changed can height

Problem. The changed can size and height affected beading geometry, CCR height, crimping/sizing total height, airtightness, and safety-related checks in sequence.

Critical variables. The beading knife and head geometry were retained, while CCR height and crimping/sizing height and pressure were reset. Sizing was adjusted by approximately 0.3 mm after considering additional 3-jaw requirements, component life, and sealing margin.

Verification. Post-beading Hi-Pot and height were connected with post-crimp total height, airtightness, leakage, drop/impact, and vent results.

Result. Assembly dimensions and sealing conditions for the new can specification were established and transferred into the process standard, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection criteria.

4. X-ray and vision disposition reset

X-ray

The changed turn count caused normal cells to fail under the inherited Count and ROI settings. Rejected cells were torn down and sectioned, and actual turn count, electrode overlap, and overhang were correlated with visual and microscope results. After development and engineering review, Count and ROI ranges were adjusted for the new structure and rechecked to prevent actual winding defects from escaping.

Vision

The new embo geometry and clad-tab reflectivity caused over-rejection under the inherited inspection range. Rather than redesigning the entire optical system, product position, illumination, focus, and inspection-region settings were tuned using new-product reference samples. The revised condition was monitored on site across four production batches for recurrence of false decisions.
