25P LFP Power Cell Process Development and Trial-Production Validation

Converted electrolyte, clad-tab, turn-count, and can changes into CTQs and local 4M+2E trials across A/B/C samples, about 20,000-cell trial production, and customer qualification

Project period
Jan 2025 A sample · Feb 2025 B/C samples and trial production · Mar 2025 customer qualification
Context
Kumyang Battery Technology Team · LFP power-cell development for an overseas customer request
Scope
Project kickoff, CTQ and change definition, process-condition decisions, sample builds, inspection and evaluation, stage gates, and process/inspection documentation

Manufacturing project summary

Problem definition
Electrolyte, clad tab, turn count, can height, and inspection conditions all changed from the existing 2170 cell, requiring new process conditions from impregnation through downstream assembly.
Key role
I translated CTQs and changes into local 4M+2E trials and managed sample builds, inspection, evaluation, gates, and control documents from project kickoff.
Key result
A/B/C samples, about 20,000-cell trial production, customer qualification, and CCB P.O approval were completed; commercial production did not proceed because of pricing.
Trial-production scope About 20,000 cells
Teardown-verified unimpregnated width About 6 mm → about 1.5 mm

Project background and manufacturing challenge

The overseas-customer 25P LFP power cell changed the electrolyte, turn count, clad tab, embo geometry, can height, and inspection conditions together. Reusing the existing 2170 conditions could create linked errors across impregnation, resistance welding, X-ray and vision inspection, and downstream assembly.

I worked from project kickoff through process-condition decisions, sample builds, inspection and evaluation, documentation, and gate operation. Capacity, IR, and cycle life were used as the product CTQs, and each change was translated into a process-specific trial before the work progressed through A/B/C samples, about 20,000-cell trial production, customer qualification, and CCB P.O approval.

Case 01. LFP electrolyte impregnation conditions

01

Material · Method · Measurement

LFP electrolyte impregnation

Impregnation was checked through direct teardown measurement and cell performance instead of relying on design fill quantity.

Symptom and success criteria

The legacy fill quantity produced insufficient electrolyte and an unimpregnated area after teardown. The target was to reduce that area while maintaining acceptable weight, capacity, IR, and life behavior.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Directly measure unimpregnated width after teardown on cells built under the same condition
  • Compare weight before and after filling plus teardown appearance
  • Review capacity, IR, and life together with the impregnation condition

Local 4M+2E decision

4MCandidate checkedEvidenceDispositionAction
ManWork sequence and loading methodStandard-work compliance compared with teardownNot retained as a primary causeKeep the work sequence
MachineVacuum and pressurization action and repeated sequenceEquipment action compared with position-specific impregnationMajor equipment conditionRecheck action and hold conditions
MaterialImpregnation behavior of the LFP electrolyteDifferent teardown result from the legacy electrolytePrimary change axisTrial conditions for the new electrolyte
MethodVacuum pressure, hold time, pressure, pressure-hold time, repeatsCondition-group comparison of width and performancePrimary adjustment axisRetest condition samples
MeasurementTeardown width, weight, capacity, IR, lifeDirect measurement connected to performanceAcceptance evidenceUse the same stage criteria
EnvironmentProduction environmentCompared with the other axesNot retained as a primary causeRetain normal controls

Action and retest

Vacuum pressure, vacuum hold time, pressurization, pressure-hold time, and repeat count were compared by condition. Conditions with improved teardown results were rechecked through weight and cell performance before moving to the next sample stage.

Result and standardization

Direct teardown measurement showed the unimpregnated width moving from about 6 mm to about 1.5 mm. This is a comparison measurement, not a published acceptance specification. The confirmed condition was transferred into the equipment-change and recipe checklist, QCP, and internal standard.

Case 02. Multilayer clad-tab resistance welding

02

Machine · Material · Method

Multilayer clad-tab resistance welding

Equipment alignment, electrode geometry, weld conditions, and joint inspection were realigned for the new material and embo geometry.

Symptom and success criteria

Two-layer Ni-Cu or three-layer Ni-Cu-Ni clad tabs and changed embo geometry produced process defects under the legacy welding condition. The target was a joint condition meeting the same nugget, tensile, and fracture-mode criteria.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Compared samples by two-layer and three-layer clad-tab and embo geometry
  • Checked electrode diameter, alignment, and pressure
  • Compared nugget size, tensile force, and fracture form by condition

Local 4M+2E decision

Action and retest

Electrode diameter, alignment, and pressure state were checked first, then pressure, electrical condition, and time were compared by condition. Each condition was re-evaluated through nugget size, tensile force, and fracture mode.

Result and standardization

The selected welding conditions and joint-inspection criteria were revised. Exact electrical, time, and pressure values remain outside the public scope.

Case 03. X-ray disposition after the turn-count change

03

Material · Method · Measurement

X-ray disposition for the changed turn count

Suspected cells were torn down and sectioned to separate actual winding defects from inspection-condition effects.

Symptom and success criteria

The new turn count could cause normal cells to fail under the legacy count and ROI settings. The criterion was to reduce false rejection without allowing an actual winding defect to pass.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Repeated inspection with the inherited Count, Threshold, and Center/Middle/Edge ROI settings
  • Tore down and sectioned cells rejected by X-ray
  • Correlated actual turn count, electrode overlap, and overhang with X-ray output

Local 4M+2E decision

MeasurementTeardown, sectioning, visual and microscope reviewActual active-material position and winding stateFinal decision evidenceReview before release

Action and retest

Suspected cells were disassembled and sectioned, then reviewed visually and microscopically. After development and engineering review, count and ROI ranges were changed, and actual winding abnormalities were checked again for escape risk.

Result and standardization

The X-ray disposition criteria were updated for the new product geometry. Exact count, threshold, and ROI values remain outside the public scope.

Case 04. Vision disposition after embo and reflectivity changes

04

Machine · Material · Method

Vision disposition for embo and reflectivity changes

Vision settings were adjusted for the new geometry and reflectivity, then monitored across production batches.

Symptom and success criteria

Changed embo geometry and clad-tab reflectivity caused false decisions under the existing inspection range. A setting was required that separated actual geometry problems from inspection errors while production continued.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Repeated inspection with reference samples of different geometry and reflectivity
  • Checked product position, illumination, focus, and inspection region
  • Monitored the adjusted condition through four production batches

Result and standardization

The confirmed settings and check sequence were transferred into inspection and production-control items.

Case 05. Beading, CCR, crimping dimensions, and sealing conditions

05

Machine · Material · Method · Measurement

Beading, CCR, and crimping dimensions and sealing

The downstream effect of changed can height was checked through geometry, sealing, and safety-related inspections.

Symptom and success criteria

Changed can size and height required new checks for post-beading geometry and Hi-Pot, CCR height, and total height, sealing, leakage, and vent after crimping and sizing. A dimensional change in one process affected downstream safety checks.

Reproduction and measurement

  • Checked Hi-Pot and height after beading and the CCR measurement
  • Checked total height, airtightness, leakage, and vent after crimping and sizing
  • Compared geometry, drop, and impact results by condition

Local 4M+2E decision

Action and retest

Beading knife and head geometry were retained, while CCR height and crimping and sizing height and pressure were reset. A 0.5 mm sizing reduction request was reviewed, but tooling purchase and service-life constraints led to an agreed change of about 0.3 mm.

Result and standardization

Hi-Pot and height after beading and total height, sealing, drop/impact leakage, and vent after crimping were reviewed. The applied conditions and inspection method were transferred into the process standard, QCP, P-FMEA, and inspection criteria.
