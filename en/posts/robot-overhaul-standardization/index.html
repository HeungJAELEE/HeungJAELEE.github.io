    Vacuum-Robot Overhaul: Turning Verbal Know-How into SOPs

    The three-minute case

    Why it mattered

    Vacuum-robot overhaul depended heavily on verbal instruction and individual experience. Disassembly and assembly order varied by operator, and torque, alignment, leak, and repeatability checks did not always read as one continuous workflow. The practical need was to turn experienced judgment into a baseline another operator could follow.

    My scope

    I performed vacuum-robot disassembly and assembly while organizing the torque, alignment, leak, and repeatability checks. I converted the field sequence and decision items into SOPs and checklists, then used them in operator training and initial work review.

    Workflow

    Review verbal work practices → document disassembly and assembly → connect torque, alignment, leak, and repeatability checks → issue SOPs and checklists → train operators

    Key decisions and verification

    • The documents linked each assembly stage to the inspection item and decision sequence that followed it.
    • Torque and alignment were treated as assembly conditions; leak and repeatability were treated as result checks. This made omissions visible.
    • The SOPs and checklists were used in actual work so I could check whether the next operator reproduced the same sequence.

    Result and standardization

    Verbal know-how became SOPs, checklists, and training material used on the shop floor. During the same period, team output increased from 1–2 to 4–5 units per month. This was a team result, not my individual output, and I do not attribute the increase to the documents alone.

    Transferability

    An overhaul standard needs more than a disassembly and assembly sequence. Fastening and alignment conditions, leak and repeatability checks, and operator training must sit in one reproducible flow.

    Limits

    The customer remains anonymous, and company drawings, customer specifications, and detailed torque values remain confidential. I do not claim a final customer approval that I did not personally verify.

    Implementation and verification

    What had to work

    • Record the sequence from incoming check through disassembly, cleaning, replacement, assembly, and final inspection.
    • Put alignment, encoder zero, leak, repeatability, and teaching decisions in the checklist.
    • Use the same SOP for operator training and first-work review.

    1. Connect assembly and inspection in one document
