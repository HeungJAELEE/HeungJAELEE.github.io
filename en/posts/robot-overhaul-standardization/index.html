Vacuum-Robot Overhaul Standardization

Turning tacit work into a repeatable sequence for disassembly, assembly, alignment, leak testing, repeatability, and training

Project period
Feb 2021–Mar 2022
Context
Completed in Bontech's Robot Technology Team

Problem and context

Vacuum-robot overhaul does not end with cleaning and parts replacement. Arm alignment, encoder zero, vacuum leak, and repeatability must be within the acceptance rule before the robot returns to equipment. Work order and inspection know-how had depended heavily on individual experience, leading to different assembly times and missed checks.

What I owned

  • Performed disassembly, cleaning, and bearing, belt, and motor replacement.
  • Checked arm alignment, encoder zero, vacuum leak, repeatability, teaching, and commissioning.
  • Documented parts lists, disassembly/assembly order, torque, alignment, and inspection criteria.
  • Trained operators with SOPs and checklists and reviewed their first work.
  • Supported overseas line setup and sample overhaul; the public result stops at the initial sample qualification I directly confirmed during employment.

What I checked and changed

Stage Checks Documented baseline
Incoming/disassembly External condition, motion, part locations Incoming checklist and disassembly order
Cleaning/replacement Contamination, bearings, belts, motors Replacement criteria and parts list
Assembly Fastening order, torque, arm position Assembly sequence and cautions
Alignment/zero Arm alignment and encoder zero Reference and check method
Final inspection Leak, repeatability, teaching, commissioning Test order, decision, and abnormal-response steps

The documents were written so the next operator could follow the same assembly order and find an abnormality through the same tests.

Result and limits

  • Documented verbal know-how as SOPs, checklists, and training material and used them in actual work.
  • During the same period, team output increased from 1–2 to 4–5 units per month.
  • Connected assembly and inspection in one flow to reduce operator-dependent omissions.

I do not attribute the output increase to the documents alone because public evidence does not preserve demand, product mix, or monthly workday differences. Equipment drawings, customer identities, serial numbers, and detailed torque values remain private. A long-duration final approval completed after I left the company is not included in my result.
