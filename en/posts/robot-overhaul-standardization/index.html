    Vacuum-Robot Overhaul: Turning Verbal Know-How into SOPs

    A vacuum-robot overhaul case that documented disassembly, assembly, torque, alignment, leak, and repeatability checks; team output rose from 1–2 to 4–5 units per month during the same period

    Jul 31, 2026 · 4 minutes · Last reviewed Jul 31, 2026

    • Vacuum Robot
    • Overhaul
    • Leak Test
    • Repeatability
    • SOP

    Problem and context

    Vacuum-robot overhaul does not end with cleaning and parts replacement. Arm alignment, encoder zero, vacuum leak, and repeatability must be within the acceptance rule before the robot returns to equipment. Work order and inspection know-how had depended heavily on individual experience, leading to different assembly times and missed checks.

    What I owned

    • Performed disassembly, cleaning, and bearing, belt, and motor replacement.
    • Checked arm alignment, encoder zero, vacuum leak, repeatability, teaching, and commissioning.
    • Documented parts lists, disassembly/assembly order, torque, alignment, and inspection criteria.
    • Trained operators with SOPs and checklists and reviewed their first work.
    • Supported overseas line setup and sample overhaul; the public result stops at the initial sample qualification I directly confirmed during employment.

    What I checked and changed

    StageChecksDocumented baseline
    Incoming/disassemblyExternal condition, motion, part locationsIncoming checklist and disassembly order
    Cleaning/replacementContamination, bearings, belts, motorsReplacement criteria and parts list
    AssemblyFastening order, torque, arm positionAssembly sequence and cautions
    Alignment/zeroArm alignment and encoder zeroReference and check method
    Final inspectionLeak, repeatability, teaching, commissioningTest order, decision, and abnormal-response steps

    The documents were written so the next operator could follow the same assembly order and find an abnormality through the same tests.

    Result and limits

    • Documented verbal know-how as SOPs, checklists, and training material and used them in actual work.
    • During the same period, team output increased from 1–2 to 4–5 units per month.
    • Connected assembly and inspection in one flow to reduce operator-dependent omissions.

    I do not attribute the output increase to the documents alone because public evidence does not preserve demand, product mix, or monthly workday differences. Equipment drawings, customer identities, serial numbers, and detailed torque values remain private. A long-duration final approval completed after I left the company is not included in my result.

    Problem

    Work order and inspection know-how were passed on verbally, so assembly time and checks varied by operator.

    Goal

    Create SOPs and checklists that cover the same sequence from disassembly through leak, repeatability, and teaching.

    Success

    SOPs, checklists, and training are used in actual work, with assembly and inspection omissions managed under one baseline. Team output during the same period is recorded separately as a career result.

    Scope and boundaries

    In scope

    • Disassembly, cleaning, and bearing/belt/motor replacement
    • Arm alignment, encoder zero, leak, repeatability, and teaching
    • Parts lists, assembly order, torque, SOPs, checklists, and training

    Out of scope or unverified

    • Customer identities, equipment drawings/serials, and detailed torque
    • Claiming a long-duration final approval completed after employment
    • Applying one work baseline to every robot model

    Implementation and verification

    What had to work

    • Record the sequence from incoming check through disassembly, cleaning, replacement, assembly, and final inspection.
    • Put alignment, encoder zero, leak, repeatability, and teaching decisions in the checklist.
    • Use the same SOP for operator training and first-work review.

    Quality and operating rules

    • Do not disclose customer, drawing, equipment identity, or detailed torque.
    • Separate directly completed employment results from later approval.
    • Present the output increase only as a result in this work environment.

    Why it was designed this way

    1. Connect assembly and inspection in one document

      Stage checks reduce the chance of finding an assembly omission only at final inspection.

    2. Include training in completion

      Publishing a document alone does not change the actual work sequence, so first work was reviewed.

    3. Limit overseas-result wording

      Only the initial sample qualification directly confirmed during employment is public.
