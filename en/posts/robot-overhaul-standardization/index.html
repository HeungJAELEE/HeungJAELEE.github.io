1. Background and objective

At the start of YMC’s new robot business, the target models had no documented parts lists, overhaul sequence, torque, alignment, encoder-zero, or inspection basis. A robot could appear fully assembled while latent failures remained and only surfaced at the customer site.

  • Base/body assembly offset and poor alignment could produce coordinate error, interference, and loss of repeatability.
  • Encoder-zero drift and drive-assembly variation could cause home-return and teaching errors.
  • Seal damage or contaminated assembly surfaces could cause vacuum leakage, particles, and outgassing.
  • Bearing, belt, motor, or encoder problems could create vibration or alarms under load.
  • Installation and teaching-coordinate mismatch could cause transfer errors and line stops.

Replacing parts alone could not control operator-dependent work or latent failure. External technology transfer and teardown of normal used robots, failed units, and customer-returned units were used to map model structure and failure patterns. The overhaul process was then designed through an FMEA sequence of failure mode, effect, cause, preventive control, and detection control.

The check sheet was not an administrative record. It served as the field-level control plan that confirmed every preventive and detection control had been completed before the robot moved to the next process gate.

Preventive and detection control pairing was the default. Assembly sequence and alignment criteria served as preventive controls; FARO repeats served as the precision-detection control. Seal and cleaning criteria served as preventive controls; helium leak served as the non-visible leakage-detection gate.

Note: Environment applied only to vacuum and cleanliness failure modes and was excluded from the primary causes of the other gates.

3. Execution by critical failure mode

1) Misalignment, encoder-zero error, and repeatability loss

Failure modes

  • Base/body assembly offset
  • Arm fastening and torque variation
  • Alignment-reference mismatch
  • Encoder zero-point drift

Preventive controls

  • Confirmed part relationships and assembly order through teardown of normal and failed units
  • Fixed part specifications, orientation, and position in the parts list and overhaul manual
  • Standardized the base-to-body-to-arm sequence, fastening, alignment, and encoder-zero work
  • Required each checkpoint to be recorded before the operator declared assembly complete

Detection controls

  • Used FARO to measure the arm endpoint and travel path at least 15 times
  • Checked repeat position, straightness, and motion-path variation in one measurement sequence
  • Repeated alarm, home, zero, Z-axis, theta-axis, and dual-arm motion checks

The precision-assembly check sheet recorded the sequence of part confirmation, fastening, alignment, zero, FARO result, and motion check. Each prior step created the valid starting condition for the next measurement.

2) Vacuum leak, particles, and outgassing

Failure modes

  • Damaged or incorrect seals
  • Assembly-surface offset, debris, or residue
  • Inadequate cleaning and drying
  • Lubricants or life-limited parts unsuitable for vacuum use

Preventive controls

  • Checked wear and specification of bearings, belts, motors, encoders, and seals during incoming inspection and disassembly
  • Added component specification, condition, and vacuum suitability to the parts list and replacement criteria
  • Standardized part cleaning, drying, assembly-surface checks, and residue control

Detection controls

  • Applied helium leak as a mandatory gate after base/body assembly
  • Recorded the inspection order and result by seal, assembly surface, and connection
  • Moved to arm assembly and final precision checks only after leak confirmation

Vacuum leakage cannot be dispositioned by appearance alone. Seal, cleaning, and assembly controls were therefore handled as preventive checks, while the helium-leak result served as the detection record for release.

3) Loaded operation and customer-site teaching error

Failure modes

  • Normal no-load operation followed by vibration or alarms with the end effector installed
  • Travel-path interference or repeated return error
  • Mismatch between site installation references and teaching coordinates

Preventive controls

  • Checked drive parts, belt tension, arm interference, and assembly condition before motion testing
  • Standardized the pre-shipment motion program, teaching order, and load condition
  • Defined the site installation, alignment, and coordinate-check sequence in the setup check sheet

Detection controls

  • Completed at least 50 loaded cycles and a dry run with the end effector installed
  • Recorded return position, alarms, interference, and vibration in the shipment inspection form
  • Confirmed installation, alignment, teaching, actual motion, and initial sample qualification at the customer site

Factory acceptance and site acceptance served different purposes. Internal checks verified the mechanical, vacuum, and drive condition of the robot. Site checks verified layout, coordinates, product motion, and handoff in the actual line environment.

4. Zero-to-one internalization at YMC and repeat-overhaul expansion at Bontech

YMC — turn a new model into an internal standard

  • Led practical work in a three-person robot team
  • Used external technology transfer and teardown of normal used, failed, and returned units to map part specifications and assembly relationships
  • Authored model-specific parts lists, disassembly, assembly, operation, inspection manuals, and run sheets
  • Built incoming, teardown, precision-assembly, leak, FARO, cycle, shipment, and teaching check sheets
  • Completed initial qualification and shipped about five units of one model

Bontech — extend the system into repeat overhaul and field operations

  • Reorganized recurring part wear, precision-setting, leakage, and work-sequence problems as FMEA control items
  • Applied part-life and replacement criteria, operator OJT, and independent-work assessment to Bontech’s operating environment
  • Performed installation, alignment, teaching, leakage, repeatability, and initial sample qualification at domestic and overseas sites
  • Connected internal overhaul results and customer-site acceptance results in the equipment history and handed them over to operations

YMC established the new-model basis. Bontech extended it into repeat work and site setup. The result was a manufacturing and equipment standard that allowed multiple operators to reproduce the same sequence and inspection gates rather than relying on one technician’s experience.

5. FMEA creation, revision, and document cascade

Initial FMEA creation at YMC

  • Teardown of normal, failed, and customer-returned robots
  • Mapping of part location, orientation, dimensions, and assembly relationships
  • Registration of alignment, encoder-zero, vacuum-leak, loaded-motion, and site-teaching failure modes
  • Identification of missing current controls

Pre-qualification FMEA revision

  • FARO repeatability, straightness, and motion-path detection items
  • Helium-leak, cleanliness, and residue gates
  • Loaded-cycle and dry-run detection items
  • Site installation, coordinate, interference, teaching, and initial-sample acceptance

Repeat-operation FMEA revision at Bontech

  • Bearing, belt, motor, encoder, and seal life and wear failure modes
  • Operator setting, inspection omission, and repeat-rework items
  • Replacement intervals, OJT, and independent-work controls
  • Domestic/overseas site setup, initial qualification, and handoff items

6. Results and standardization

  • Separated new-model failure patterns into FMEA failure modes, effects, and causes
  • Defined preventive and detection controls for each failure mode
  • Established parts lists and check sheets for incoming, disassembly, assembly, leak, precision, loaded cycles, and site setup
  • Applied at least 15 FARO repeats and at least 50 loaded cycles to shipment acceptance
  • Completed YMC’s initial qualification and shipment of about five units of one model
  • Expanded the approach at Bontech into repeat overhaul, operator OJT, domestic/overseas installation, teaching, and initial qualification
