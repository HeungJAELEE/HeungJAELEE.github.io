Field standardization

Vacuum-Robot Overhaul Standardization

Turning tacit work into a repeatable sequence for disassembly, assembly, alignment, leak testing, repeatability, and training

Project period
Feb 2021–Mar 2022
Context
Completed in Bontech's Robot Technology Team
Scope
Disassembly, cleaning, component replacement, precision assembly, final inspection, SOP, and training

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Experience-dependent assembly and inspection created variation in work time and missed checks.
What I owned
While performing overhaul and precision assembly, I documented parts, torque, alignment, leak, and repeatability checks in run sheets and SOPs and trained operators.
Verified result
Rework and overtime were observed to decrease, while team monthly output increased from 1–2 to 4–5 units during the same period.

Standardization flow

A vacuum-robot overhaul does not end with component replacement. Disassembly, cleaning, precision assembly, alignment, encoder zero, leak, repeatability, and teaching must be checked in the same sequence and under the same criteria. I converted experience-dependent work into a standard process linked to functional acceptance.

01

4M-based work standardization

Turning operator experience into a repeatable acceptance flow

Component control, precision assembly, alignment, and functional inspection became one work system.

Previous state

  • Disassembly, assembly, and inspection sequences varied by operator experience.
  • Completing assembly did not establish shipment readiness without alignment, encoder-zero, leak, and repeatability checks.
  • Reducing rework and overtime required a repeatable standard, not only expert know-how.

4M cause map

4MCandidateEvidence checkedDecision and action
ManSkill-dependent work sequence and checksCompared first-run work with rework pointsCreated stage checkpoints and a training sequence.
MachineAlignment, zero, leak, and repeatability were separateAssembly completion did not equal functional acceptanceConnected test equipment and decisions to the work flow.
MaterialReplacement part, seal, and cleanliness variationComponent condition affected assembly and leak resultsDefined parts lists and replacement/cleaning criteria.
MethodNo standard disassembly, torque, or inspection methodRecords varied even for the same robotDefined sequence and decisions in run sheets, SOPs, and checklists.

Direct work

  • Performed arm, center, and base disassembly, cleaning, component replacement, and precision assembly.
  • Checked bearings, belts, motors, seals, tightening order, and torque.
  • Set arm alignment and encoder zero.
  • Verified helium leak, repeatability, straightness/wobble, and teaching results.

Functional acceptance gate

StageAcceptance check
Incoming and disassemblyAppearance, drive condition, component position
Cleaning and replacementContamination, wear, replacement parts, seal condition
AssemblyTightening order, torque, arm position
AlignmentArm alignment and encoder zero
Final inspectionLeak, repeatability, straightness/wobble, teaching

End-effector vertical wobble of 7 mm or less at maximum travel and final-position lateral repeat variation of 50 μm or less are described only as customer inspection criteria, not portfolio KPIs.

Standardization and field use

  • Created parts lists, run sheets, SOPs, checklists, and training materials.
  • Applied them to actual work and operator training and supported initial execution.
  • Team monthly shipments increased from 1–2 to 4–5 units during the same period.
  • This is a team result from the period, not an isolated effect attributed solely to standardization.

Standards left in operation

  • Disassembly, assembly, tightening order, and torque
  • Component condition, replacement, and cleaning criteria
  • Alignment and encoder-zero method
  • Leak, repeatability, and teaching sequence
  • Hold and rework flow for abnormalities
  • Operator training and initial work confirmation

Overseas setup and initial qualification are mentioned only as a limited application of this standard. Customer or site identifiers and a separate overseas-project outcome are not disclosed.
