Manufacturing case study

Field standardization

Vacuum-Robot Overhaul Standardization

Turning tacit work into a repeatable sequence for disassembly, assembly, alignment, leak testing, repeatability, and training

Project period
Feb 2021–Mar 2022
Context
Completed in Bontech's Robot Technology Team
Scope
Disassembly, cleaning, component replacement, precision assembly, final inspection, SOP, and training

Field problem and result summary

Field problem
Experience-dependent assembly and inspection created variation in work time and missed checks.
What I owned
While performing overhaul and precision assembly, I documented parts, torque, alignment, leak, and repeatability checks in run sheets and SOPs and trained operators.
Verified result
Rework and overtime were observed to decrease, while team monthly output increased from 1–2 to 4–5 units during the same period.

Why standard work was needed

Disassembly, assembly, and inspection varied by operator experience, creating differences in work time and missed checks. A common flow was needed from component replacement through alignment, encoder zero, leak, repeatability, and teaching.

Scope I performed directly

  • Arm, center, and base disassembly, cleaning, component replacement, and precision assembly
  • Bearing, belt, motor, and seal checks plus fastening order and torque control
  • Arm alignment and encoder zero
  • Helium leak, repeatability, straightness, vibration, and teaching checks

Standards that reduced work variation

VariationExisting issueStandardized item
Work orderDifferent disassembly and assembly order by skill levelStep checkpoints and run sheets
Parts and sealsDifferent replacement and cleaning controlParts list and replacement/cleaning criteria
FasteningDifferent order and torqueFastening order and torque criteria
Records and trainingMissed checks and inconsistent recordsSOP, checklist, training material, and first-piece confirmation

Final functional acceptance

StageAcceptance check
Incoming and disassemblyAppearance, motion, and component position
Cleaning and replacementContamination, wear, replacement parts, and seals
AssemblyFastening order, torque, and arm position
AlignmentAlignment and encoder zero
Final inspectionLeak, repeatability, straightness, vibration, and teaching

Specific customer-internal acceptance thresholds are not public.

Field application and result

Parts lists, run sheets, SOPs, checklists, and training material were used in actual work and operator training. Team monthly output increased from 1–2 to 4–5 units during the same period, but the increase is not attributed to standardization alone. Overseas setup and initial qualification are described only as work where the standard was applied.
